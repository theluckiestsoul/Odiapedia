**Dahibara aloodum** (ଦହିବରା ଆଳୁଦମ) is Odisha's best-known street food, closely tied to the city of **Cuttack**. Soft urad dal fritters (*bara*) are soaked in thin, tempered curd water (*dahi*) and served with a spicy potato curry (*aloodum*) and usually a dried-pea curry (*ghuguni*). The plate is finished with chopped onion, coriander and crunchy sev. Stalls across Odisha sell it, but many Odias still consider Cuttack the home of the real thing.

## Origin and history

Dahibara itself, lentil fritters soaked in curd, is an old preparation found in many parts of India. The combination with aloodum and ghuguni is the Cuttack contribution. According to local accounts reported by OdishaBytes, the dish was first sold in **Bidanasi** (old Cuttack) and in Mahalpara in Athagarh. It was popularised from around the early 1960s by vendors at **Killa Padia**, next to the historic Barabati Fort. These accounts are local tradition, not archival records.

## How is dahibara aloodum made?

1. **Grind the batter.** Grind soaked urad dal with very little water into a thick, smooth batter. Season with ginger, chilli and salt and whip it to make it airy.
2. **Fry the bara.** Drop small portions into hot oil and fry until golden and cooked through.
3. **Soak.** Put the hot bara first in plain water to soften them, then squeeze gently and move them to the tempered curd water. Leave them to soak for a few hours. Wikipedia mentions about 3 to 5 hours.
4. **Cook the aloodum.** Fry onion and ginger-garlic paste, add tomato and spices, then the boiled potatoes and some water. Simmer into a thick, spicy curry.
5. **Cook the ghuguni.** Simmer boiled peas with onion, tomato and spices.
6. **Assemble.** Place two or three soaked bara in a bowl with some curd water. Spoon over aloodum and ghuguni, then top with onion, coriander, cumin powder and sev.

## Variations

- **Dahibara aloodum ghuguni:** The full Cuttack plate with all three components.
- **Plain dahibara:** Bara in curd water only, often eaten on hot summer days.
- **Dahibara with chutney:** Some vendors add tamarind or tomato chutney.
- **Dahibara at home:** Families also make it at home, usually milder than street versions.

## When is it eaten?

Dahibara aloodum is an everyday street snack, eaten for breakfast or in the evening. Many people consider it especially refreshing in summer because of the curd. Dahibara is also made at home for family gatherings. **Dahibara Aloodum Dibasa** on 1 March is an informal day when Odias celebrate the dish.

## Where to try it in Odisha

- **Cuttack:** The heart of the tradition. Stalls cluster around the old city, Barabati Fort and busy junctions. OdishaBytes has profiled long-running vendors at places such as Shelter Chhak.
- **Bhubaneswar:** Stalls across the city; Incredible India lists dahibara aloodum among Bhubaneswar's food experiences.
- **Other towns:** Most towns in coastal Odisha have dahibara vendors.

Odiapedia does not rank individual vendors. Ask locals in Cuttack for the stall they trust.

## Nutrition notes

The dish combines protein from urad dal and peas, carbohydrate from potatoes, and curd. The fritters are deep-fried and the curries can be oily and spicy, so it is a filling, moderately rich snack rather than a light one.
