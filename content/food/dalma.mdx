recipe:
  yield: 4–6 servings
  prep: 20
  cook: 30
  course: Main course
  diet:
  - Vegetarian
  ingredients:
  - 1 cup toor (arhar) dal, washed and soaked 20 minutes
  - 3½–4 cups water
  - '2½–3 cups mixed vegetables in 1-inch pieces: raw papaya, raw banana, pumpkin, brinjal, a small potato, drumstick or pointed gourd'
  - ½ tsp turmeric and salt to taste
  - 1-inch piece ginger, grated
  - 2 tbsp ghee (or mustard oil)
  - 1 tsp panch phutana
  - 2 dried red chillies and 1 bay leaf
  - 2 green chillies, slit
  - 'Bhaja masala: 1 tsp cumin seeds and 2 dried red chillies, dry-roasted and ground'
  - 2 tbsp grated coconut and coriander leaves to finish
  steps:
  - Cook the dal with the water, turmeric and ginger until soft but not completely broken down — about two whistles in a pressure cooker or 15–20 minutes in an open pot.
  - Add the firm vegetables (papaya, banana, potato, drumstick) with salt and cook 5 minutes, then add the soft ones (pumpkin, brinjal). Simmer until tender but still holding their shape; dalma should be thick and pourable.
  - Heat the ghee in a small pan; add the bay leaf, red chillies and panch phutana and let the mustard seeds pop without burning. Add the green chillies.
  - Pour the tempering over the dal and cover for a minute so it infuses.
  - Stir in the roasted cumin–chilli powder, then finish with coconut, coriander and a spoon of ghee. Serve hot with rice.
  tips:
  - For the temple-style (satvik) version, cook without onion or garlic and temper only in ghee.
  - Roast and grind the bhaja masala fresh — its smoky note is what makes dalma taste Odia.

# Dalma — The Soul Food of Odisha

  currentLang="en" 
  languages={{

  }} 
/>

**Dalma** (ଡାଲମା) is the signature dish of Odisha — a hearty, aromatic stew of **lentils cooked with vegetables** and tempered with the iconic **panch phutana** (five-spice blend). It is not just food; it is an emotion for every Odia.

## Quick Facts

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Type** | Lentil-vegetable stew |
| **Main Ingredients** | Toor dal (some cooks use chana dal), vegetables, panch phutana |
| **Origin** | Odisha, India |
| **Served With** | Steamed rice |
| **Significance** | Part of Jagannath Temple Mahaprasad |

## Why Dalma Is Special

Unlike the dal of other Indian states, Dalma is unique because:
- Vegetables are **cooked with** the lentils, not separately
- Uses **panch phutana** (5-spice tempering) — a hallmark of Odia cooking, similar to Bengali panch phoron
- **No garlic, no onion** in the traditional version
- Has a distinctive **smoky sweetness** from the vegetables
- Part of the sacred **Mahaprasad** at Jagannath Temple, Puri

> "If you haven't tasted Dalma with rice, you haven't truly eaten Odia food."

## The Sacred Panch Phutana

The signature five-spice blend that defines Odia cuisine:

| Spice | Odia Name |
|-------|-----------|
| **Cumin seeds** | ଜିରା |
| **Mustard seeds** | ସୋରିଷ |
| **Fenugreek seeds** | ମେଥି |
| **Fennel seeds** | ପାନମଧୁରୀ |
| **Nigella seeds (Kalonji)** | କଳାଜିରା |

These five spices, when crackled together in hot ghee, create the unmistakable aroma of Odia cooking.

## How to make dalma

The [recipe card below](#recipe) is our one home recipe, with toor dal. Common home variations:

- **Chana dal** in place of toor dal, or a mix of the two, for a firmer texture.
- A pinch of **jaggery** for a slightly sweet finish.
- The **temple-style (satvik)** version is cooked without onion or garlic and tempered in ghee.

Vegetables should stay in pieces rather than dissolve, and the ghee tempering goes in at the end.

## Dalma in Jagannath Temple

At the **Jagannath Temple in Puri**, Dalma is one of the holiest foods:
- Part of the temple offerings often described as **Chhappan Bhog** (56 offerings)
- Cooked in the temple's **kitchen (Rosaghar)** — one of the world's largest temple kitchens
- Uses traditional earthen pots and wood fire
- **No onion, no garlic** — strictly sattvic
- Shared with devotees as **Mahaprasad**

## Nutritional Benefits

Nutrition varies with the lentil, vegetables and amount of ghee used. In general:
- **Protein** from lentils
- **Fiber** from vegetables
- **A balanced meal** when eaten with rice

## Cultural Significance

Dalma represents:
- **Simplicity** — Made with humble, everyday ingredients
- **Nutrition** — Complete meal when paired with rice
- **Temple tradition** — Sacred offering to Lord Jagannath
- **Odia identity** — The one dish every Odia craves when away from home
- **Inclusivity** — Vegetarian, sattvic, and universally loved

## Perfect Odia Thali

Dalma is always served as part of the Odia thali:
- **Rice** (bhata) — steamed
- **Dalma** — the star
- **Bhaja** — fried vegetables
- **Besara** — mustard-flavored curry
- **Saga** — leafy greens
- **Papad** — crispy
- **Ambula** — tangy side dish
