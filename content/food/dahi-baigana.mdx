recipe:
  yield: 4 servings
  prep: 15
  cook: 15
  course: Side dish
  diet:
  - Vegetarian
  ingredients:
  - 250–300 g brinjal, sliced into rounds
  - ½ tsp turmeric, ½ tsp chilli powder, salt
  - 1½ cups thick curd, whisked
  - 1–2 tsp sugar and a pinch of black salt
  - 3 tbsp mustard oil
  - 1 tsp panch phutana
  - 1–2 dried red chillies, 1 green chilli, 8–10 curry leaves, ½-inch ginger
  - ½ tsp roasted cumin powder and coriander leaves
  steps:
  - Coat the brinjal slices with turmeric, chilli powder and salt and rest 10 minutes.
  - Shallow-fry in 2 tbsp mustard oil until soft and browned on both sides; drain.
  - Whisk the curd with sugar, black salt and a little salt, thinning with 2–3 tbsp water if very thick.
  - Heat the remaining oil and crackle the panch phutana, chillies, curry leaves and ginger. Let the tempering cool a minute so it does not split the curd.
  - Stir the tempering and brinjal into the curd, sprinkle with roasted cumin and coriander, and serve at room temperature.
  tips:
  - On festival days make it without onion or garlic; ghee can replace mustard oil.
  - Add the brinjal just before serving if you like it with some bite.

**Dahi baigana** (ଦହି ବାଇଗଣ) — "curd brinjal" — is a mildly sour, cooling side dish of fried brinjal in tempered yoghurt. It is made especially for festivals, when it is cooked without onion or garlic, and is a familiar part of a festive Odia thali alongside [dalma](/food/dalma) and rice.

## What makes it Odia

The brinjal is first coated in turmeric and chilli and fried in **mustard oil**, and the yoghurt is tempered with **panch phutana** — the five-spice mix of mustard, cumin, fenugreek, fennel and nigella used throughout Odia cooking. A pinch of sugar balances the sourness of the curd.
