**Chhena gaja** (ଛେନା ଗଜା) is a traditional sweet from Odisha made from fresh chhena (soft cheese made by curdling milk) kneaded with a little semolina. The dough is shaped into small rectangular blocks, fried, and cooked in thick sugar syrup. The finished sweet has a lightly crisp, sugar-glazed outside and a soft, grainy inside. It belongs to the same chhena tradition as [rasagola](/food/rasagola), [chhena poda](/food/chhena-poda) and [chhena jhili](/food/chenna-jhilli).

## Origin and region

The word *gaja* refers to a family of small, rectangular sweets in Odisha. Gaja is also among the dry sweets of the Jagannath Temple tradition (see [Mahaprasad](/food/mahaprasad)). **Chhena gaja** is the version made with chhena.

The exact origin of chhena gaja is not well documented. Wikipedia's article associates it with Balasore in north Odisha. Today it is made by sweet shops across coastal Odisha. It is especially associated with **Pahala**, the sweet-making hub on the national highway between Bhubaneswar and Cuttack. ETV Bharat reports that Pahala's sweet trade began roughly 70 to 80 years ago with small roadside rasagola stalls. Pahala now has more than 100 sweet shops selling chhena gaja, chhena poda and rasagola.

Odiapedia found no record of a Geographical Indication (GI) for chhena gaja as of 2026. Its better-known cousin, the Odisha Rasagola, received a GI tag in 2019. See [famous foods of Odisha](/food/famous-foods) for other Odia sweets.

## Ingredients

Quantities below are typical for a home batch of about 12 to 15 pieces. Sweet makers adjust them by feel.

| Ingredient | Typical quantity | Role |
|------------|------------------|------|
| Fresh chhena (from about 1 litre full-cream milk) | about 200 g | Main body of the sweet |
| Semolina (suji) | 2 to 3 tablespoons | Binds the chhena and gives firmness |
| Sugar | about 300 g | For the syrup |
| Water | about 1.5 cups | For the syrup |
| Green cardamom | 2 to 3 pods, crushed | Flavour (optional) |
| Ghee or vegetable oil | for frying | Frying medium |

## How is chhena gaja made?

1. **Make the chhena.** Bring milk to a boil and curdle it with lemon juice or vinegar. Strain through muslin, rinse and hang it to drain until the chhena is moist but not wet.
2. **Knead.** Knead the chhena with semolina on a flat surface until smooth and slightly firm.
3. **Shape.** Press the dough into a flat slab about 1 to 1.5 cm thick and cut it into small rectangles. Some cooks let the pieces dry briefly so they hold their shape.
4. **Fry.** Fry the pieces gently in ghee or oil until light golden.
5. **Cook in syrup.** Simmer the pieces in a thick sugar syrup flavoured with cardamom until they absorb it.
6. **Glaze and cool.** Lift the pieces out as the syrup thickens. As they cool, a thin layer of sugar sets on the surface.

Some cooks boil the shaped pieces directly in syrup without frying. Wikipedia describes this method, and both methods are found.

## Variations

- **Fried and glazed:** The most common shop style, golden outside with a sugar crust.
- **Syrup-boiled:** Not fried first, softer and paler.
- **Juicy style:** Served wetter, with more syrup.
- **Flour gaja:** The older gaja made from flour rather than chhena. Gaja is also a dry temple sweet in Puri.

## When is chhena gaja eaten?

Chhena gaja is an everyday sweet, sold year-round and eaten as a snack with tea or after a meal. Families buy it in bulk for weddings, thread ceremonies, pujas and festivals such as [Durga Puja](/culture/durga-puja). Travellers often buy boxes as gifts at highway sweet stalls. Because it holds its shape and keeps for a few days without refrigeration, it travels better than syrup-filled sweets such as rasagola.

## Where to try chhena gaja in Odisha

- **Pahala**, between Bhubaneswar and Cuttack, is the best-known roadside sweet market.
- **Bhubaneswar** and **Cuttack** have sweet shops throughout the cities. Incredible India lists chhena gaja among Bhubaneswar's food experiences.
- **Puri** and the towns of coastal and north Odisha, including Balasore, also have local sweet makers who sell it.

Prices and shop quality vary, and Odiapedia does not list or rate individual shops.

## Nutrition notes

Chhena gaja is made from milk solids, semolina and sugar, and is usually fried, so it is high in sugar and energy. Odiapedia has not found a reliable published nutritional analysis of chhena gaja and does not give calorie figures.
