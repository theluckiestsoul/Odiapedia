recipe:
  yield: 4 servings
  prep: 20
  cook: 25
  course: Main course
  diet: []
  ingredients:
  - 500 g prawns, shelled and deveined
  - ½ tsp turmeric and ½ tsp salt
  - 3 tbsp mustard oil (or oil and ghee)
  - 1 bay leaf, small cinnamon stick, 3–4 green cardamoms, 3–4 cloves
  - 1 onion, ground to a paste
  - 1 tsp ginger paste and ½ tsp garlic paste
  - 2–3 green chillies, ½ tsp red chilli powder (optional)
  - 1 cup thick and ½ cup thin coconut milk
  - 1 tsp sugar, salt, 1 tsp ghee
  steps:
  - Coat the prawns with turmeric and salt and sear in hot oil about a minute a side, just until they curl. Remove.
  - Fry the whole spices in the same oil, then the onion paste until golden and the oil separates.
  - Add ginger, garlic, chillies and chilli powder for 2 minutes, then the thin coconut milk; simmer 5 minutes.
  - Lower the heat, add the thick coconut milk, sugar, salt and the prawns, and simmer gently 4–5 minutes without boiling hard.
  - Finish with ghee and serve with rice.
  tips:
  - A spoon of mustard paste instead of coconut milk turns it into chingudi besara.
  - Overcooked prawns turn rubbery — small ones need only 2–3 minutes.

**Chingudi malai** (ଚିଙ୍ଗୁଡ଼ି ମାଲାଇ) is prawns (*chingudi*) simmered in coconut milk with whole spices — a rich dish for special meals in coastal Odisha. The best-documented version of the dish is Bengal's **chingri malai curry**, and we have found no separate written history of an Odia origin, so it is fairest to call it a dish shared along the eastern coast and cooked in Odia homes.

Prawns from **Chilika** and the coast are prized in Odisha. For a more distinctly Odia preparation, the same prawns are cooked in a mustard-paste gravy as **chingudi besara** (see [machha besara](/food/machha-besara)).
