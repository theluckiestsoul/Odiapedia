**Santula** (ସନ୍ତୁଳା) is a light mixed-vegetable dish from Odisha. Seasonal vegetables are boiled or gently sauteed and then finished with a tempering of **panch phutana** (the Odia five-spice mix), dried or green chillies and, in many homes, garlic. Incredible India describes santula as a vegetable curry cooked with minimal spices. It is everyday home food, the quiet counterpart to richer dishes such as [dalma](/food/dalma) and besara.

## Origin and region

Santula is cooked throughout Odisha, in villages and cities alike. No documented date or place of origin exists. It belongs to the Odia home cooking tradition of using little oil and spice and letting the vegetables' own flavour come through.

Because santula is mild and easy to digest, many Odia families cook it for children, older people and anyone who wants a light meal. OdiaFoods notes that santula is valued for being gentle on the stomach. This is a cultural practice, not a medical claim.

## What is panch phutana?

**Panch phutana** (*pancha* means five, *phutana* means tempering) is the five-spice tempering used throughout Odia cooking. It is a whole-spice mix of mustard seeds, cumin, fenugreek, fennel (aniseed) and nigella (kalonji). For santula, a small amount is crackled in hot oil and poured over or stirred into the vegetables.

## How is santula made?

**Sijha (boiled) santula:**

1. Cut the vegetables into similar-sized pieces.
2. Boil them with turmeric and salt in a little water until just tender. Do not let them turn to mush.
3. Heat a teaspoon of oil in a small pan. Add panch phutana, chilli and garlic, and let the seeds crackle.
4. Pour the tempering over the vegetables, stir gently and simmer for a minute or two.
5. Optionally, top with fried, crushed badi.

**Bhaja (fried) santula:**

1. Heat a little oil and crackle the panch phutana, chilli and garlic.
2. Add the cut vegetables, turmeric and salt and saute for a few minutes.
3. Add a splash of water, cover and cook until tender, then let it dry slightly.

Some modern recipes, such as the one published by Odisha TV, add onion and cook the vegetables in a pressure cooker first. Many traditional households make santula without onion.

## Variations

- **Sijha santula:** Boiled vegetables with a light tempering. This is the simplest and mildest version.
- **Bhaja santula:** Vegetables sauteed in the tempering, a little drier and more aromatic.
- **Santula with badi:** Crushed fried badi adds crunch and savouriness.
- **Single-vegetable santula:** Raw papaya or pumpkin santula, made when only one vegetable is at hand.

## When is santula eaten?

Santula is an everyday dish rather than a festival food. It appears regularly in Odia home meals with rice, dal or dalma, and with breakfast foods such as roti and [chakuli pitha](/food/odia-pitha).

## Where to try santula in Odisha

Santula is mostly a home dish, so it is rarely the highlight of a restaurant menu. Visitors usually find it:

- In Odia thali meals at local eateries and hotels in **Bhubaneswar**, **Cuttack** and **Puri**
- At roadside "hotels" (local dhabas) serving rice meals in towns across the state
- In Odia homes, where it is easiest to taste the light, home-style version

## Nutrition notes

Santula is made mainly of vegetables with very little oil, so it is generally low in fat compared with fried or curry dishes. Specific health claims such as "immunity boosting", which appear in some media recipes, are not supported by any published study Odiapedia could find and are not repeated here.
