  yield: About 20 pithas

  ingredients:
  - 500 g short-grain raw rice
  - 400–500 g jaggery
  - ½ cup water for the syrup
  - ¼ cup white sesame seeds
  - 2 tbsp grated dry coconut (optional)
  - A pinch of cardamom (or edible camphor) and salt
  - Oil or ghee for deep frying
  steps:
  - Soak the rice 4–7 hours, drain and dry on a cloth until just damp.
  - Grind or pound it to a fine flour while still slightly moist, and sieve.
  - Boil the jaggery with the water to a one-string syrup.
  - Lower the heat and add the flour gradually, stirring hard, until a thick glossy dough leaves the pan. Mix in coconut, cardamom and salt and cool until you can handle it.
  - Shape lime-sized balls, press into sesame seeds and flatten to discs about 5 mm thick.
  - Deep-fry over medium heat until golden-brown, then press each between two flat ladles to squeeze out excess oil. They crisp as they cool.
  tips:
  - 'Test one first: undercooked syrup makes pithas break up in the oil; overcooked syrup makes the dough hard.'
  - Stored airtight, arisa keeps for weeks.

**Arisa pitha** (ଆରିସା ପିଠା) is a crisp-edged, chewy cake of rice flour cooked into jaggery syrup, pressed in sesame seeds and deep-fried. It is a ritual food of the month of **Margasira**, especially for **Manabasa Gurubara**, the Thursday worship of Lakshmi, and because it keeps well it is also an everyday snack. Wikipedia's article on pitha records a legend that the merchants Tapassu and Bhallika offered it to the Buddha, and some families keep a custom of sending baskets of arisa to in-laws.

## The secret is the syrup

Freshly ground, slightly moist rice flour is stirred into jaggery boiled to a **one-string syrup**. Undercooked syrup makes the cakes spread and break in the oil; overcooked syrup makes the dough hard — so cooks always fry a test piece first.
