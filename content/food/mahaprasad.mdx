**Mahaprasad** (ମହାପ୍ରସାଦ) is the sanctified food of the Shree Jagannath Temple in Puri, Odisha. It is cooked every day in the temple kitchen in earthen pots over wood fires, offered to Jagannath, Balabhadra and Subhadra, and then shared by devotees, most famously at the temple's open food market, **Ananda Bazar**. For many Odias, eating Mahaprasad together, regardless of caste or status, is as central to a Puri pilgrimage as the [darshan of Jagannath](/history/jagannath-temple) itself.

## What does Mahaprasad mean?

*Prasad* means food that has been offered to a deity and returned as a blessing; *maha* means great. In the Jagannath tradition, food prepared in the temple kitchen becomes Mahaprasad once it has been offered to Jagannath and then, according to temple tradition, to Goddess Bimala inside the temple complex.

Cooked Mahaprasad is often called **abadha** (ଅବଢ଼ା) in everyday Odia, especially the rice-based meal bought at Ananda Bazar.

## The temple kitchen (Rosaghara)

The Jagannath Temple kitchen, known as the **Rosaghara**, is widely described as one of the largest temple kitchens in India.

Key features of the kitchen, as reported by temple cooks and news outlets:

- **Earthen pots and wood fire.** Mahaprasad is cooked only in new earthen pots on wood-fired hearths. No gas or electricity is used.
- **Stacked pots.** Several earthen pots are stacked one above another over a single hearth. ETV Bharat reports nine pots per hearth. A popular belief holds that the topmost pot cooks first. Treat this as temple lore rather than a verified fact.
- **Hereditary cooks.** The food is prepared by hereditary temple cooks called **suaras**, led by the Mahasuara. In 2015 the cooks' association told IANS that 600 to 700 cooks work there.
- **Strict rules.** The food is vegetarian and prepared without onion or garlic. The cooks do not taste it before it is offered.

Figures for the kitchen's size vary widely between sources. Published counts of hearths range from a few hundred to more than 700, and counts of daily diners range from about 20,000 on ordinary days to around one lakh. Odiapedia does not give a single number for this reason. According to tradition, Goddess Mahalakshmi herself supervises the cooking.

## Kotha bhoga, Ballabha and the daily offerings

The temple offers food to the deities several times a day. The terms used for these offerings are often mixed up.

| Term | What it refers to |
|------|-------------------|
| **Gopala Ballabha bhoga** | The first offering of the morning. It is a light, uncooked meal, traditionally including items such as puffed rice (khai), banana, curd and coconut. |
| **Sakala Dhupa (Kotha bhoga / Raja bhoga)** | The first cooked meal of the day, served around mid-morning. The Temple Administration also calls it Kotha Bhoga or Raja Bhoga. It includes dishes such as khechudi, kanika (sweet ghee rice), saga (greens), pulis and enduri. |
| **Madhyahna, Sandhya and Bada Singhara Dhupa** | The later midday, evening and night offerings in the temple's daily schedule. |
| **Sankudi Mahaprasad** | The cooked items: plain rice, ghee rice, mixed rice, dal, mixed vegetable curries, saga and porridge-like dishes. |
| **Sukhila Mahaprasad** | The dry items, such as khaja, gaja and other sweets. They keep well and are carried home. |

The Shree Jagannath Temple Administration now lets devotees sponsor the Sakala Dhupa (Kotha bhoga) through its official booking system. Check the temple website for current details.

## What is Chhappan Bhog?

**Chhappan bhoga** (*chhappan* means fifty-six) refers to the tradition of offering 56 varieties of food to Jagannath. The full list is not officially published in one place, and published lists from guides and websites differ in names and grouping. The 56 items are generally said to include rice dishes (anna, kanika, khechudi), dals, vegetable curries such as besara and mahura, saga, pithas and sweets such as khaja, jagannath ballabha and kakara. Some writers also describe the exact recipes as a closely guarded tradition of the temple cooks.

## Typical dishes of Mahaprasad

| Dish | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Anna / Sadha anna | Plain rice |
| Kanika | Sweetened ghee rice |
| Khechudi | Rice and lentils cooked together, sometimes with ghee and ginger |
| Dali | Sweet dal with vegetables |
| Besara | Mixed vegetables in a mustard sauce |
| Mahura | A mixed vegetable curry |
| Saga | Sauteed leafy greens |
| Khiri | Rice pudding |
| Khaja, gaja, kakara | Dry and fried sweets (sukhila) |

Besides onion and garlic, the temple kitchen is reported to avoid a number of vegetables regarded as foreign to its old tradition, potato among them. Published lists of excluded items differ, so they are not reproduced here.

## Ananda Bazar: eating Mahaprasad

**Ananda Bazar** ("market of joy") is the open-air food market inside the temple complex, to the north-east of the main entrance (Singhadwara). Here cooked Mahaprasad is sold in earthen pots to pilgrims. People sit and eat together on the floor, often from sal-leaf plates. This shared meal is traditionally seen as erasing differences of caste and status; IANS described thousands eating side by side "irrespective of their caste, creed or status".

## Nirmalya (Kaivalya)

**Nirmalya**, also called **Kaivalya**, is Mahaprasad rice dried in the sun. Pilgrims take it home in small packets as a blessing. It is used in household rituals, and according to tradition a few grains are placed in the mouth of a dying person.

## When is Mahaprasad eaten?

Mahaprasad is cooked and offered every day of the year. Demand peaks during major temple festivals, especially the [Rath Yatra](/culture/rath-yatra). During Rath Yatra the deities stay at the Gundicha Temple, where special offerings are made, including the *poda pitha* traditionally offered at the Mausima temple on the return journey. Families also buy Mahaprasad for weddings, thread ceremonies and memorial rites.

## How to get Mahaprasad in Puri

- **Inside the temple:** Ananda Bazar, for Hindu pilgrims. Non-Hindus are not permitted inside the Jagannath Temple.
- **Dry sweets:** Khaja and other sukhila items are sold in and around the temple. In 2024 Odisha TV reported that the Puri district administration had said only khaja bought inside Ananda Bazar counts as sukhila Mahaprasad.
- **Booking offerings:** Use the official Shree Jagannath Temple Administration website. Avoid touts who claim to sell "official" Mahaprasad outside the temple.

Rules and arrangements change. Check the Temple Administration's website before you visit.
