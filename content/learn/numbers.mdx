Let's learn to count in Odia! Numbers are essential for shopping, telling time, and everyday conversations.

Notice how 29 is "one less than thirty" — ଅଣ (aṇa, "less") + ତିରିଶି. The same pattern gives 19, ଊଣେଇଶି ("one less than twenty").

## Big numbers

Like other Indian languages, Odia groups large numbers in lakhs and crores rather than millions.

| Number | Odia Numeral | Odia Word | Pronunciation |
|--------|--------------|-----------|---------------|
| 100 | ୧୦୦ | ଶହେ | śahe |
| 1,000 | ୧,୦୦୦ | ହଜାରେ | hajāre |
| 1,00,000 (one lakh) | ୧,୦୦,୦୦୦ | ଲକ୍ଷେ | lakṣe |
| 1,00,00,000 (one crore) | ୧,୦୦,୦୦,୦୦୦ | କୋଟିଏ | koṭie |

## Useful Phrases with Numbers

- **କେତେ?** (kete?) — "How many?" / "How much?"
- **ଏକ ଟଙ୍କା** (eka ṭaṅkā) — "One rupee"
- **ଦୁଇଟା ଆମ୍ବ** (duiṭā āmba) — "Two mangoes"
- **ତିନି ଘଣ୍ଟା** (tini ghaṇṭā) — "Three hours"

## Practice Dialogue

**At the market:**
- Customer: ଏହା କେତେ? (ehā kete?) — "How much is this?"
- Seller: ପାଞ୍ଚ ଟଙ୍କା (pāñca ṭaṅkā) — "Five rupees"

## Key Pattern

> Notice that 11-19 have their own names that must be memorised. Odia counts in tens like other Indian languages, but the word for 20, କୋଡ଼ିଏ (koḍie), also means "a score", and counting goods in scores (କୋଡ଼ି) is a traditional market practice.
