The building blocks of the Odia script: vowel letters, the first group of consonants, and the vowel signs that join vowels to consonants.

## Vowels (ସ୍ୱରବର୍ଣ୍ଣ)


Odia is usually taught with **about a dozen vowel letters** (counts vary depending on whether rare letters such as ୠ, ଌ and ୡ are included). Let's start with the most common ones:

| Odia | Transliteration | Sound | Example Word |
|------|-----------------|-------|--------------|
| ଅ | a | close to "o" in "hot" (a rounded sound) | ଅନ୍ନ (anna - cooked rice) |
| ଆ | ā | "a" in "father" | ଆମ୍ବ (āmba - mango) |
| ଇ | i | "i" in "sit" | ଇଲିଶି (iliśi - hilsa fish) |
| ଈ | ī | "ee" in "see" | ଈଶ୍ୱର (īśwara - god) |
| ଉ | u | "u" in "put" | ଉପର (upara - above) |
| ଊ | ū | "oo" in "moon" | ଊଷା (ūṣā - dawn) |

### Practice These!

Try writing each vowel 5 times:
```
ଅ ଅ ଅ ଅ ଅ
ଆ ଆ ଆ ଆ ଆ
ଇ ଇ ଇ ଇ ଇ
```

## Consonants (ବ୍ୟଞ୍ଜନବର୍ଣ୍ଣ)

Odia has **more than 30 consonants** (exact counts vary between primers). Let's learn the first group of five, the କ-ବର୍ଗ (ka-barga):

| Odia | Transliteration | Sound |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| କ | ka | "k" in "kite" |
| ଖ | kha | "kh" (aspirated k) |
| ଗ | ga | "g" in "go" |
| ଘ | gha | "gh" (aspirated g) |
| ଙ | ṅa | "ng" in "sing" |

## Your First Words

Let's combine what you've learned:

- **କା** (kā) = "caw" (sound a crow makes)
- **ଖା** (khā) = "eat" (command)
- **ଗୋ** (go) = "cow" (a Sanskrit word used in compounds such as ଗୋମାତା; the everyday Odia word is ଗାଈ, gāī)

## Vowel signs (ମାତ୍ରା)

Each vowel has two forms. The **vowel letter** (ଆ, ଇ, ଉ …) is used on its own, at the start of a word or syllable. After a consonant, the vowel is written instead as a **vowel sign** (ମାତ୍ରା) attached to that consonant. A consonant with no sign already carries the inherent vowel **a**: କ is read *ka*.

| Vowel letter | Vowel sign | With କ | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| ଆ | ା | କା | kā |
| ଇ | ି | କି | ki |
| ଈ | ୀ | କୀ | kī |
| ଉ | ୁ | କୁ | ku |
| ଊ | ୂ | କୂ | kū |
| ଏ | େ | କେ | ke |
| ଓ | ୋ | କୋ | ko |

So the sound *kā* is written କ + ା = **କା**, not କଆ. When typing on a phone or computer, choose the vowel sign after the consonant, not the vowel letter.

## Practice Exercise

Read these syllables; each is a consonant plus a vowel sign:
1. କ + ା = କା (kā)
2. ଖ + ି = ଖି (khi)
3. ଗ + ୁ = ଗୁ (gu)

Now spot the difference: **ଆମ୍ବ** (āmba, mango) starts with the vowel letter ଆ because the word begins with a vowel; **କାମ** (kāma, work) uses the vowel sign ା after କ.

## Key Takeaway

> Odia letters are round, which is traditionally explained by writing on palm leaves with a stylus: long straight lines could split the leaf.
