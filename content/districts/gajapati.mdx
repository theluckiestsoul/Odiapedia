## Overview

**Gajapati** (ଗଜପତି) is a hilly district in southern Odisha on the border with Andhra Pradesh. Its headquarters is **Paralakhemundi**, the former seat of the Gajapati rulers of the Paralakhemundi estate. The district is named after **Maharaja Krushna Chandra Gajapati Narayan Deo**, who helped bring about the separate province of Odisha in 1936 and became its first Premier in 1937.

Gajapati was carved out of [Ganjam district](/district/ganjam) and began functioning as a separate district on **2 October 1992**. It has a single sub-division, Paralakhemundi, and seven blocks: Gosani, Kashinagar, Gumma, Rayagada, Nuagada, R. Udayagiri and Mohana.

The 2011 Census counted 577,817 people. More than half of them, 313,714 according to the district administration, belong to Scheduled Tribes, mainly the **Saura** (Sora). At the 2011 Census about 42% of residents spoke Odia as their first language, about 34% Sora and about 16% Telugu. The district covers 4,325 sq km by the Census figure, while the district administration gives 3,850 sq km.

Gajapati is best known for the sacred peak of **Mahendragiri**, the Tibetan settlement and monastery at **Jirang**, the palace and temples of Paralakhemundi, and its forested hills and waterfalls.

## Geography and climate

Gajapati lies in the Eastern Ghats. According to the district administration, more than 60% of its land is hilly. The district borders Kandhamal to the north, Ganjam to the east, Rayagada to the west and Andhra Pradesh to the south.

The main river is the **Mahendratanaya**, a tributary of the Vamsadhara. It rises in the Mahendragiri hills and flows past Paralakhemundi. The highest point is **Mahendragiri** (about 1,501 m), in Rayagada block. It is known for its rich plant and reptile life, with more than 600 species of flowering plants recorded. In 2014 an expert committee recommended that the Mahendragiri area be declared a biosphere reserve.

## History

According to the district administration, the Paralakhemundi kingdom was founded in the later half of the 15th century by **Kolahomee**, a son of the Gajapati king Kapilendra Deva. The area had earlier been part of the Khemundi territory and was home to Saura and other hill communities. The Paralakhemundi rulers kept the title *Gajapati*, tracing their line to the Gajapati kings of Odisha.

Under British rule Paralakhemundi became a zamindari estate within Ganjam district of the Madras Presidency. Its rulers were active builders and patrons. The **Gajapati Palace** in Paralakhemundi is a 19th-century royal residence that mixes Indo-Saracenic and European styles; its design is usually credited to the British architect Robert Fellowes Chisholm, though published dates for its construction vary. Around 1900 the Maharaja also built a light railway linking Paralakhemundi with Naupada on the coastal main line.

Paralakhemundi also became an early centre of modern education and civic life in southern Odisha. Its municipality was established in 1885, and the town's SKCG Autonomous College, founded more than a century ago, is often described as the second-oldest college in Odisha after Ravenshaw College in Cuttack.

**Maharaja Krushna Chandra Gajapati Narayan Deo** took a leading part in the movement for a separate Odia province and pressed the case in London (see [Utkal Divas](/culture/utkal-divas)). When Orissa province was formed in 1936, Paralakhemundi was included in it. He served as Premier of Orissa from 1 April to 18 July 1937 and again from 24 November 1941 to 30 June 1944.

After independence the area remained part of Ganjam district until Gajapati district was created on 2 October 1992.

A new chapter began in 1963. On 1 May that year, according to Odisha Tourism, the first Tibetan refugees arrived at Chandragiri, settling in a group of camps that the Tibetans call **Phuntsokling**.

## Places to visit

### Mahendragiri

Mahendragiri is identified in tradition with the Mahendra Parvata of the Ramayana and Mahabharata, and is regarded as the abode of the sage Parashurama. Old stone temples on the mountain are attributed by tradition to the Pandavas, and three of them, the **Bhima, Kunti and Yudhishthira temples**, are protected by the Archaeological Survey of India as Monuments of National Importance. A Shiva shrine, the Gokarneswar temple, stands near the summit. The mountain draws large crowds of pilgrims at Maha Shivaratri and is popular with trekkers. A motorable road now reaches close to the top.

### Jirang (Chandragiri) and the Padmasambhava Mahavihara

The Tibetan settlement around Chandragiri has a large monastery, the **Padmasambhava Mahavihara**, which is also a college for young monks. Its foundation stone was laid in 2003, and the Dalai Lama inaugurated it in January 2010. Odisha Tourism describes it as the largest monastery in eastern India. The settlement is about 87 km from Brahmapur (Berhampur).

### Paralakhemundi

The district headquarters has the **Gajapati Palace**, a 19th-century Indo-Saracenic royal residence, along with other royal buildings and old temples. The palace is listed by INTACH as a heritage building, so check locally whether visitors are allowed inside. Paralakhemundi's Jagannath temple holds a well-known Ratha Yatra. The town is also known for its horn-work artisans.

### Gandahati Waterfall

Gandahati is a forest waterfall about 30 km from Paralakhemundi and about 153 km from Brahmapur. It drops roughly 20 m over rocks in a wooded valley and is a popular picnic spot, at its fullest after the monsoon.

## Culture and festivals

The **Saura** (Sora) are the largest tribal community in the district. They are known for their ritual wall paintings (*idital*), white figures drawn on red-brown mud walls, and for the practice of tattooing. The related painting tradition of the Lanjia Saura of neighbouring Rayagada district received a GI tag in 2024. Kui-speaking Kandha communities also live in the district.

The district's culture mixes Odia, Telugu and tribal traditions. Festivals listed by the district administration include [Ratha Yatra](/culture/rath-yatra), Dussehra, [Kumar Purnima](/culture/kumar-purnima) and Pongal, which reflects the Telugu-speaking population. Maha Shivaratri at Mahendragiri is the largest pilgrimage. The local speech belongs to the southern, Ganjami group of [Odia dialects](/language/odia-dialects).

Traditional crafts include horn work, bamboo products and [pattachitra](/culture/pattachitra)-style painting. Paralakhemundi has a long tradition of learning and music. The 18th–19th-century poet-composer Gopalakrushna Pattanayak, whose songs are still part of the Odissi repertoire, was born here.

## Food

Gajapati's best-known food product is **Odisha Khajuri Guda**, a jaggery made from the sap of date palms and rooted in the district's traditional, largely tribal, food production. It was registered as a Geographical Indication in January 2024 (see [Odisha GI tags](/culture/odisha-gi-tags)).

## Economy

The district administration describes Gajapati's economy as agrarian. Farming, forest produce and cottage industries such as horn work, bamboo craft and painting support most families. The hilly terrain is considered well suited to horticulture. In 2006 the Ministry of Panchayati Raj named Gajapati one of India's 250 most backward districts, making it eligible for the Backward Regions Grant Fund.

## Notable people

- **Maharaja Krushna Chandra Gajapati Narayan Deo**: ruler of Paralakhemundi, a leader of the Odisha province movement and the first Premier of Orissa (1937).
- **Gopalakrushna Pattanayak**: poet and composer of Odissi songs, born in Paralakhemundi in 1784.

## How to reach

- **By air:** The nearest major airport in Odisha is Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar.
- **By rail:** Paralakhemundi station is on the Naupada–Gunupur line, which joins the Howrah–Chennai main line at Naupada in Andhra Pradesh. The line was converted to broad gauge, and passenger services resumed in August 2011. For Gandahati, Palasa in Andhra Pradesh is also a convenient railhead.
- **By road:** Paralakhemundi and Jirang are usually reached by road from Brahmapur (Berhampur) in Ganjam; Jirang is about 87 km from Brahmapur. Buses link Paralakhemundi with Brahmapur, Bhubaneswar and towns in Andhra Pradesh.

For neighbouring districts, see [Ganjam](/district/ganjam), [Rayagada](/district/rayagada) and [Kandhamal](/district/kandhamal).
