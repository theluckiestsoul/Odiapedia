**Mayurbhanj** (ମୟୂରଭଞ୍ଜ) is the largest district of Odisha by area, lying in the far north of the state on the borders of Jharkhand and West Bengal. Its headquarters is **Baripada**. Mayurbhanj is best known for the forests of Similipal, the Mayurbhanj style of Chhau dance and the heritage of the Bhanja rulers.

## Overview

Mayurbhanj covers 10,418 sq km, the largest area of any Odisha district in the 2011 Census, and had a population of 2,519,738, the third highest in the state. It is one of Odisha's most tribal districts: Scheduled Tribes made up about 58.7 percent of the people in 2011, and Santali is the second most spoken language after Odia.

The district takes its name from the Bhanja dynasty, which ruled the area for many centuries as the princely state of Mayurbhanj. The state merged with Odisha on **1 January 1949**, and the old state became the present district. Baripada, the former royal capital, remains the administrative and cultural centre.

Mayurbhanj has four sub-divisions: **Baripada** (Sadar), **Kaptipada** (headquarters Udala), **Bamanghaty** (Rairangpur) and **Panchpir** (Karanjia). With 26 community development blocks, it has more blocks than any other district in Odisha.

The district is famous above all for **Similipal National Park**, the core of one of India's oldest tiger reserves, which covers roughly a quarter of the district. It is also known for the unmasked [Mayurbhanj Chhau](/culture/chhau-dance), the chlorite-stone temple at Khiching, sabai grass craft and the GI-tagged Similipal Kai Chutney.

## Geography and climate

Mayurbhanj sits on the north-eastern edge of the Odisha plateau. It borders Jhargram district of West Bengal, and East Singhbhum, Seraikela Kharsawan and West Singhbhum districts of Jharkhand, to the north and east. Within Odisha it borders Balasore to the south-east and Kendujhar to the west.

The Similipal hills rise in the centre of the district. Its highest point, **Khairiburu**, is about 1,178 m high, and nearby **Meghasani** reaches about 1,158 m. These hills feed several rivers, including the **Budhabalanga**, **Baitarani**, **Subarnarekha**, **Salandi**, Deo and Sone. Forest covered about 4,458 sq km, or roughly 43 percent of the district, according to figures summarised on Wikipedia.

The climate is subtropical, with hot summers and a heavy monsoon.

**Similipal** was declared a tiger reserve under Project Tiger in 1973, a biosphere reserve by India in 1994, and joined UNESCO's World Network of Biosphere Reserves in 2009. The wider reserve covers about 2,750 sq km. See our [Similipal National Park travel guide](/travel/simlipal-national-park) for seasons and permits.

## History

According to the district administration, the **Bhanja dynasty** ruled the region from about the 9th century CE. The early kingdom was known as **Khijjinga Mandala**, after its capital Khijjinga Kotta, the present-day **Khiching**. At its height it took in Mayurbhanj, Kendujhar and parts of present-day Jharkhand and West Bengal. Family tradition linked the rulers to the peafowl (mayura), which is often given as the origin of the name.

The capital later moved to **Haripur**, and around 1800 to **Baripada**. The region faced an invasion by the Delhi Sultan Firuz Shah Tughlaq in 1361. It later came under Mughal rule, then under the Marathas in the mid-18th century, and in 1803 it became a feudatory state under the British.

Mayurbhanj was regarded as one of the more progressive princely states. **Maharaja Sriram Chandra Bhanj Deo** (reigned 1882–1912) built roads and schools, constituted the Baripada Municipality in 1905, and promoted a narrow-gauge railway: the first section of the **Mayurbhanj State Railway**, from Rupsa to Baripada, opened on 20 January 1905. He presided over the first session of the Utkal Sammilani in 1903, a milestone in the movement for a separate Odisha (see [Utkal Divas](/culture/utkal-divas)).

After Independence, Maharaja Pratap Chandra Bhanj Deo signed the instrument of merger on **17 October 1948**, and Mayurbhanj merged with Odisha on **1 January 1949**.

## Places to visit

### Similipal National Park

Similipal's sal forests, grasslands and waterfalls are the district's main draw. **Barehipani** and **Joranda** are among the highest waterfalls in Odisha, and visitors enter through the Pithabata gate near Baripada or the Kaliani gate near Jashipur. The park closes during the monsoon; check the official reserve website for dates. Details are in our [Similipal travel guide](/travel/simlipal-national-park).

### Khiching and the Kichakeswari Temple

Khiching, in the Panchpir sub-division about 130 km from Baripada, was the early capital of the Bhanjas. Its **Kichakeswari temple**, dedicated to the family deity of the dynasty, is old, though sources differ on its date (the summaries on Wikipedia give the 8th century and about 920 CE), and was rebuilt in 1934 under Maharaja Pratap Chandra Bhanj Deo using chlorite stone. A museum founded at Khiching in 1922 displays sculpture from the area.

### Devkund

Devkund (Debakunda) is a waterfall and pool on the edge of Similipal, about 25 km from Udala and 65 km from Baripada. A temple of **Goddess Ambika** stands above the lowest fall. It was built by Rajkumar Prafulla Chandra Bhanj Deo, who, according to local accounts, found a natural rock form of the goddess there.

### Baripada

Baripada's **Haribaldev temple**, the town's Jagannath shrine, was built in 1575 CE of laterite and is about 84.5 ft tall, according to the district administration. During the Baripada [Rath Yatra](/culture/rath-yatra), held a day after Puri's, only women pull the chariot of Devi Subhadra. Wikipedia dates this custom to 1975. Baripada is also the main centre of Chhau performance.

### Bhimkund

Bhimkund is a sacred pool in the Baitarani river near Booring village in Thakurmunda block, about 40 km from Karanjia, according to the district administration.

### Haripurgarh

Haripur, which became the Bhanja capital after Khiching, is the site of the **Rasika Raya temple** at Haripurgarh, listed among the district's historic temples.

## Culture and festivals

**Mayurbhanj Chhau** is the district's signature art. UNESCO inscribed Chhau dance, covering the Mayurbhanj, Seraikella and Purulia styles, on its intangible heritage list in 2010. Unlike the other two styles, Mayurbhanj Chhau is danced without masks. The main season is **Chaitra Parba** in spring, when troupes perform in Baripada. See [Chhau dance](/culture/chhau-dance).

The district administration names **Makar Parba** and **Karama Parba** as the two major festivals, and the Baripada Rath Yatra as the main religious festival. **Jhumar** is a folk song and dance tradition performed at weddings and festivals.

Mayurbhanj is home to many tribal communities. The largest are the **Santal**, **Ho** and **Bhumij**, and the Kharia and Mankirdia live in and around Similipal. **Pandit Raghunath Murmu**, born near Rairangpur, created the **Ol Chiki** script for Santali in 1925.

The main craft is [sabai grass craft](/culture/sabai-grass-craft): rural women roll and weave the tough sabai grass into baskets, mats and decorative pieces.

## Food

- **Similipal Kai Chutney**: a chutney made with red weaver ants, registered as a Geographical Indication for Mayurbhanj in 2024. See [GI tags of Odisha](/culture/odisha-gi-tags).
- **Mudhi mansa**: puffed rice served with mutton curry, which Odisha Tourism associates with Mayurbhanj. See [famous foods of Odisha](/food/famous-foods).

Everyday Odia fare such as [pakhala bhata](/food/pakhala-bhata) and [dalma](/food/dalma) is common across the district.

## Economy

Most people work on the land. In the 2011 Census, about 19.5 percent of workers were cultivators and about 46.5 percent were agricultural labourers. **Paddy** is the main crop, with groundnut, maize, mung and mustard also grown.

The district has iron ore deposits at **Gorumahisani**, **Badampahar** and **Sulaipat**, served by railway branch lines towards Jamshedpur. Smaller deposits of china clay, kyanite and other minerals are also recorded. Forest produce and sabai grass are important sources of rural income. OrissaPOST reported in 2021 that sabai was grown on about 22,758 hectares of the district. Tourism centres on Similipal and Baripada.

## Notable people

- **Maharaja Sriram Chandra Bhanj Deo** (1870–1912), the ruler who modernised Mayurbhanj State.
- **Pandit Raghunath Murmu** (1905–1982), born at Dandbose near Rairangpur, creator of the Ol Chiki script for Santali.
- **Droupadi Murmu**, born in 1958 at Uparbeda village, MLA for Rairangpur from 2000 to 2009, Governor of Jharkhand, and the 15th President of India from 26 July 2022.
- **Jagadish Mohanty** (born 1951 at Gorumahisani), an influential modern Odia fiction writer.

## How to reach

- **By air:** The district administration puts Baripada about 207 km from Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, and about 195 km from Kolkata airport.
- **By rail:** Baripada station is on the line from Rupsa to Bangriposi, which links with the Howrah–Chennai main line. Trains run to Balasore and Bhubaneswar. Badampahar station is about 18 km from Jashipur, on the northern side of Similipal.
- **By road:** Baripada is about 255 km from Bhubaneswar and about 60 km from Balasore by road. NH-18 runs from Balasore through Baripada towards Jharkhand, and NH-49 crosses the district.
