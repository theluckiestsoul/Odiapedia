**Angul** (ଅନୁଗୋଳ), also written **Anugul**, is a district in central Odisha whose headquarters is Angul town. Angul district is a major industrial centre, with the **Talcher coalfield**, large power stations and the **NALCO** aluminium smelter. It is also home to the forests of the **Satkosia gorge** on the Mahanadi and the hot springs of **Deulajhari**.

## Overview

Angul lies close to the geographical centre of Odisha. It covers 6,375 sq km according to the district administration and the 2011 Census, though Wikipedia gives a smaller 6,232 sq km. Its population in 2011 was 1,273,821. About 16 percent of the people lived in towns, a relatively high share for Odisha because of the industrial townships around Angul and Talcher.

The district was created on **1 April 1993**, when the old Dhenkanal district was divided under a state government notification of 27 March 1993. It brought together the former British-ruled Angul area and three former princely states, **Talcher**, **Athmallik** and **Pallahara**. These remain the district's four sub-divisions: **Angul**, **Talcher**, **Athmallik** and **Pallahara**.

Angul is often described as an industrial hub of Odisha. Wikipedia notes that Angul town, with 44,390 residents in 2011, is nicknamed the "Aluminium City", and that Talcher is called the "Black Diamond City" because of its coal. Large parts of the district are still forested and hilly, especially around Satkosia, Pallahara and Athmallik.

## Geography and climate

Angul is bordered by Dhenkanal and Cuttack to the east; Deogarh, Kendujhar and Sundargarh to the north; Sambalpur and Subarnapur to the west; and Boudh and Nayagarh to the south.

Two great rivers cross the district. The **Brahmani** flows through the north and east, where the **Rengali dam** has been built across it. The **Mahanadi** forms the south, where it cuts a gorge about 22 km long through the Eastern Ghats at **Satkosia**. The **Satkosia Tiger Reserve** (about 964 sq km, declared in 2007) combines the Satkosia Gorge and Baisipalli wildlife sanctuaries. The Satkosia Gorge wetland is also a Ramsar site.

## History

Angul was once a small hill kingdom. According to a history published by the Angul district court, the British took over the area in 1803, after the Marathas lost Odisha. **Raja Somnath Singh Jagadeb**, who ruled from 1813, is described as the last king of Angul. He resisted the East India Company and was deposed; his territory was confiscated on **16 September 1848**, while Wikipedia dates his revolt and removal to 1847. Angul was then run directly as a government estate.

In **1891** Angul was made a separate district, with the Khondmals (present-day Kandhamal) attached to it. Wikipedia says the district was abolished when Odisha became a province in 1936. Its area then became part of Cuttack district and, after 1949, of Dhenkanal district.

The neighbouring princely states each had their own histories, according to the district administration:

- **Talcher** was renamed by King Padmanabha Birabara Harichandan after the family goddess **Taleswari**.
- **Athmallik** takes its name from eight (*atha*) chieftains called *malliks*, among whom the territory was once divided. Forty-two kings of the dynasty are said to have ruled, with capitals moving from Hondapa to Thakurgarh, Nuagaon and Purunagarh.
- **Pallahara** was founded before the 18th century by Santosh Pala, who took the name Ganeswar Pala on becoming king.

In the 1930s the **Praja Mandal** movement spread through these states. In Talcher it was led by **Pabitra Mohan Pradhan** and reached its peak in 1938–39. The former Chief Minister **Nabakrushna Choudhury** also organised Praja Mandal work from Angul and later lived there. Talcher merged with India on 1 January 1948, and the area remained part of Dhenkanal district until Angul district was created in 1993.

## Places to visit

### Satkosia and Tikarpada

The **Satkosia gorge**, where the Mahanadi flows between forested hills, is the district's best-known natural sight. Nature camps at **Tikarpada**, **Chhotkei**, **Purunakote** and elsewhere offer boating, walks and birdwatching. The river is a habitat of the gharial and mugger crocodile, and the forests hold leopards, elephants and sloth bears. A plan to bring tigers from Madhya Pradesh was suspended in 2019 after a translocated tiger died. See our [Satkosia travel guide](/travel/satkosia).

### Deulajhari

Deulajhari, about 6 km from Athmallik and 90 km from Angul, is described by the district administration as an ancient centre of Shaivism. The temple of **Siddheswar** stands among hot springs. The administration says 24 of the 84 springs recorded in temple tradition are still active, with water from about 40°C to 62°C. Named springs include Agnikunda, Taptakunda and Himakunda. A temple of Maa Maheshwari also stands within the complex. The 24-acre site is enclosed by a dense natural grove. The nearest station is Boinda, about 44 km away.

### Saila Srikhetra, Angul

The Jagannath temple of Angul, known as **Saila Srikhetra**, stands on top of the **Sunasagad** hill in the town.

### Rengali

The **Rengali dam** across the Brahmani has a hydroelectric power station and a large reservoir, listed by the district administration for its scenery.

### Other sites

- **Kosala Ramachandi**: a village shrine about 28 km from Angul on the Angul–Bagedia road.
- **Bhimkand**: a sacred site with a reclining image known as the "sleeping Vishnu", listed by Odisha Tourism.
- **Maa Hingula temple**: a goddess shrine that Odisha Tourism recommends visiting.

## Culture and festivals

Angul shares the festival calendar of central Odisha, including [Raja](/culture/raja-parba), [Nuakhai](/culture/nuakhai) and [Durga Puja](/culture/durga-puja). Local goddesses have their own yearly festivals. The district administration notes the annual **Lovi Thakurani yatra** at Garh Santry, and Odisha Tourism lists visits to the **Maa Hingula** temple. Jagannath worship centres on Saila Srikhetra in Angul town.

Odia is spoken by about 95.5 percent of people (2011), with Ho and Hindi as the next most common languages.

## Food

Odisha Tourism's Angul guide highlights:

- **Mahanadi river fish** cooked in mustard-based gravy, similar to [machha besara](/food/machha-besara).
- Everyday Odia meals of [pakhala bhata](/food/pakhala-bhata), [dalma](/food/dalma) and vegetables.
- Millet-based dishes made by tribal communities from forest produce.
- Sweets such as [chhena poda](/food/chhena-poda), [rasagola](/food/rasagola) and *arisa pitha* (see [Odia pitha](/food/odia-pitha)).

## Economy

Angul is one of the most industrialised districts in Odisha.

- **Coal:** The **Talcher coalfield** covers about 500 sq km. The Geological Survey of India estimates its reserves at about 38.65 billion tonnes, the highest of any coalfield in India. Coal was first found at Gopalprasad in 1837, and the field has been operated by **Mahanadi Coalfields Limited** since 1992. The coal is of relatively low grade with a high ash content, but it is mined on a large scale: Wikipedia reports that nearly 100,000 tonnes a day were being sent to power stations across eastern and southern India by 2011.
- **Power:** NTPC runs large thermal power stations in the Talcher area, fed by local coal.
- **Aluminium:** **NALCO**'s smelter at Angul has a capacity of 4.60 lakh tonnes a year, according to the company.
- **Fertiliser:** Talcher Fertilizers Limited is building a coal-gasification ammonia–urea plant at Talcher with a planned capacity of about 1.27 million tonnes a year.

Agriculture and forest produce remain important outside the industrial belt.

## Notable people

- **Pabitra Mohan Pradhan** (born 8 February 1908 at Poipala, Talcher sub-division), leader of the Talcher Praja Mandal movement. He was elected from Talcher in 1952 and 1957, defeated two former rulers in Talcher and Pallahara in 1961, and later served as a state cabinet minister.
- **Nabakrushna Choudhury** (1901–1984), Chief Minister of Odisha from 1950 to 1956. He was born at Kherasa (now in Jagatsinghpur district) but carried the Praja Mandal movement forward from Angul and spent his later years there.

## How to reach

- **By air:** Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, about 140 km away according to Odisha Tourism; Wikipedia gives about 150 km from the state capital.
- **By rail:** **Angul** and **Talcher** have railway stations with express and passenger services. Boinda is the closest station for Athmallik and Deulajhari.
- **By road:** National and state highways link Angul with Dhenkanal, Cuttack and Bhubaneswar to the east and Sambalpur to the west, with regular bus and taxi services.

Odisha Tourism suggests at least two days and October to March for the district. Satkosia is also good for wildlife from mid-February to mid-June. See [how to reach Odisha](/travel/how-to-reach-odisha) and the [map of Odisha](/map).
