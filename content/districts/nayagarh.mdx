## Overview

**Nayagarh** (ନୟାଗଡ଼) is a district in central Odisha, lying between the Mahanadi valley and the hills of the Eastern Ghats west of Bhubaneswar. Its headquarters is the town of **Nayagarh**. The district is known for its temples, especially **Kantilo Nilamadhab** and the **Raghunath temple at Odagaon**. It is also known for **chhena poda**, the baked cheese sweet that local tradition says was first made here.

Nayagarh district was created on **1 April 1993**, when the old Puri district was divided into Puri, Khordha and Nayagarh. It brings together four former princely states, or garjats: **Nayagarh, Ranpur, Khandapara and Daspalla**.

The district covers 3,890 sq km. It had 962,789 people at the 2011 Census, a literacy rate of 80.42% and a sex ratio of 915 females per 1,000 males. It has four Assembly seats. Nayagarh and Ranpur fall in the Puri Lok Sabha constituency, Khandapada in Cuttack and Daspalla in Kandhamal.

## Geography and climate

Nayagarh is a district of low hills, forests and paddy-growing valleys. It is bordered by Khordha to the south and east, Cuttack to the east and north, Angul to the north, Boudh to the north-west, Kandhamal to the west and Ganjam to the south-west.

The **Mahanadi** runs along the north of the district, where it cuts through the Eastern Ghats near Satkosia. Kantilo stands on its bank. In this stretch the **Baisipalli Wildlife Sanctuary** (168.35 sq km, created on 6 May 1981) protects mixed deciduous and sal forest with elephants, leopards, bears, sambar and spotted deer. Since 2007 it has formed part of the **Satkosia Tiger Reserve** with the neighbouring Satkosia Gorge Sanctuary (see the [Satkosia travel guide](/travel/satkosia)).

## History

According to the district administration, the Nayagarh state was founded by **Suryamani**, a prince of the **Baghela** dynasty of Rewa in present-day Madhya Pradesh. Tradition says he came to Puri on pilgrimage, stayed on, built a fort and took a tiger's head as his emblem. Wikipedia dates the founding to the 13th century.

The ninth ruler, **Baghel Singh** (1480–1510), moved the capital to the present site, which became known as Baghua Nayagarh. During the reign of the twelfth ruler, **Raghunath Singh** (1565–1595), the kingdom expanded after the Odia king Mukunda Deva was defeated in 1565. Nayagarh took Ranpur's territories of Odagaon, Sarankul and Baunsiapara. Before his death Raghunath Singh divided his lands. Harihar Singh received Nayagarh, and Jadunath Singh received four *khandagrams*, which became the state of **Khandapara**.

**Ranpur** had earlier been a vassal of the Suryavamsi Gajapati kings of Odisha and came under British control in 1803. Like Daspalla to the west, it was one of the tributary Garjat states of Orissa under the British.

The 1930s saw strong **Praja Mandal** (people's association) movements in these states. In Nayagarh, people protested a monopoly on the betel trade by almost entirely giving up betel. On **5 January 1939**, at Ranpur, a crowd killed **Major R. L. Bazalgette**, the British Political Agent for the Orissa States, as reported to the British Parliament. Ranpur remembers its local Praja Mandal martyrs, Raghu and Dibakar. After independence the four states merged with Orissa, and they later formed part of Puri district until Nayagarh district was created in 1993.

Nayagarh also has a place in the tradition of [Jagannath](/history/jagannath-temple). According to legend, the deity was first worshipped as Nilamadhaba by the forest chief Viswavasu before King Indradyumna brought him to Puri. Kantilo is one of the places associated with this story, although other places also claim it.

## Places to visit

### Kantilo Nilamadhab

The Nilamadhaba temple at **Kantilo** stands on twin hills above the Mahanadi, about 33 km from Nayagarh and about 100 km from Bhubaneswar, according to the district administration. Its rituals follow the pattern of the Jagannath temple at Puri. A stream of water is said to flow constantly from the deity's feet. A Siddheswar (Shiva) temple stands nearby, and the annual **Magha Saptami** festival draws large crowds.

### Odagaon Raghunath Temple

The temple of Raghunath (Rama) at **Odagaon**, about 27 km from Nayagarh, is built in the Kalinga style and topped by three golden *kalasas*. It is a major pilgrimage centre, especially at **Rama Navami**.

### Maninag Temple, Ranpur

Ranpur, about 28 km from Nayagarh, lies at the foot of the Maninag hills. The temple of Goddess **Maninageswari** (Maninag) on the hilltop is revered as a living Shakti shrine. Ranpur also has the Asurakumari temple near a waterfall and a Jagannath temple whose Ratha Yatra draws many visitors.

### Dakshinakali Temple and Sarankul

The **Dakshinakali** temple, about 1 km from Nayagarh town, houses the town's presiding goddess. The Shiva temple of **Ladukeshwar** at Sarankul, about 13 km away, is crowded at Maha Shivaratri.

### Daspalla and Kuanria Dam

Daspalla, about 40 km from Nayagarh, is a forested area with the **Kuanria dam**, a reservoir popular for picnics, and an adjoining deer park. The district administration recommends November to February for a visit.

### Baisipalli and Satkosia

The Mahanadi forests of Baisipalli form the southern part of the Satkosia Tiger Reserve. Visits are usually arranged through the forest department's nature camps.

## Culture and festivals

The temple festivals of Nayagarh draw pilgrims from across the state: Magha Saptami at Kantilo, Rama Navami at Odagaon, Maha Shivaratri at Sarankul and the [Ratha Yatra](/culture/rath-yatra) in Nayagarh, Ranpur and other towns. Daspalla is known for its **Lankapodi** festival, according to the district administration.

**Kantilo** is known for its **brass and bell-metal** craft. The district shares in Odisha's wider festival calendar, including [Raja Parba](/culture/raja-parba), Durga Puja and [Makar Sankranti](/culture/makar-sankranti-en).

The district is proud of its scholarly tradition: the astronomer [Pathani Samanta](/people/pathani-samanta), who combined traditional Indian astronomy with his own careful observations, was born at Khandapara in 1835.

## Food

- **[Chhena poda](/food/chhena-poda):** the baked, caramelised cheese sweet of Odisha. Local tradition credits its invention to a sweet-maker of Daspalla, usually named as Sudarshan Sahu, and sources differ on the date. Itamati and Machhipada are among the places known for good chhena poda.
- **Kanteimundi brinjal:** a small, thorny local brinjal from villages such as Badabanapur and Ratanpur in Khandapara block and several villages in Bhapur block. It received a Geographical Indication on 2 January 2024, the first brinjal from Odisha to do so (see [Odisha GI tags](/culture/odisha-gi-tags)).

## Economy

Nayagarh is mainly agricultural. Besides paddy, farmers grow vegetables, including the Kanteimundi brinjal, and sugarcane. The **Kanteimundi brinjal** GI was obtained through a farmers' organisation with support from the Krishi Vigyan Kendra, Nayagarh, and the brinjal sells at a premium over ordinary varieties.

Sugarcane farming once supplied the **Nayagarh Sugar Complex**, set up in 1980 to crush about 1.2 lakh tonnes of cane a year. Odisha TV reported in 2023 that the mill had been closed since 2015, leaving cane growers without a local buyer. Other livelihoods include sweet-making (chhena poda is sold across the state), brass and bell-metal work at Kantilo, forest produce and small trade.

## Notable people

- **[Pathani Samanta](/people/pathani-samanta)** (Samanta Chandrasekhar, 1835–1904): astronomer who made his observations with simple instruments and wrote the *Siddhanta Darpana*. He was born at Khandapara, and the planetarium in Bhubaneswar is named after him.

## How to reach

- **By air:** The nearest airport is Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar. The district administration gives Nayagarh as about 90 km from Bhubaneswar.
- **By rail:** **Nayagarh Town** station, opened on 19 June 2017, is on the Khurda Road–Balangir line. Khordha Road junction, about 69 km away, is on the main east-coast line.
- **By road:** NH-57 passes through the district, and regular buses run from Bhubaneswar and other Odisha towns. Kantilo is reached by state highway from Bhubaneswar, and Daspalla lies on the Bhubaneswar–Phulbani route.

For neighbouring districts, see [Khordha](/district/khordha), [Cuttack](/district/cuttack), [Kandhamal](/district/kandhamal) and [Ganjam](/district/ganjam).
