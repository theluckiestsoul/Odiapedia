**Deogarh** (ଦେବଗଡ଼), also spelt Debagarh or Debagada, is a small, hilly and heavily forested district in western Odisha. The district headquarters, Deogarh town, was the capital of the former princely state of **Bamanda (Bamra)**, whose rulers were known for bringing printing, postal services and electricity to the area. Deogarh is also known for the Pradhanpat waterfall just above the town.

## Overview

Deogarh district was created on **1 January 1994**, when it was separated from Sambalpur district. It is one of Odisha's least populous districts: the 2011 Census counted 312,520 people. Only about 7 percent of them lived in towns, making Deogarh overwhelmingly rural.

The district has a single sub-division, Deogarh, and three community development blocks. Its area is 2,940 sq km according to the Census; the district administration and Wikipedia give a smaller figure of about 2,782 sq km. About a third of the people belong to Scheduled Tribes, and Odia is spoken by around nine in ten residents, with Mundari, Kisan and Sadri also in use.

Forests cover a large part of the district, and hills ring Deogarh town. For administration and transport, Deogarh looks mainly to Sambalpur, about 90 km to the west.

## Geography and climate

Deogarh lies east of Sambalpur in the hilly upland of north-western Odisha. The **Brahmani** river flows through the district, and several hill ranges cross it. Wikipedia lists heights for the main ranges, including Kaidanta (about 816 m), Khajuria (about 745 m) and Pradhanpat (about 743 m). Wikipedia also gives forest land in the district as about 1,560 sq km, more than half its area.

The soils are mostly sandy, loamy and red. Farming depends heavily on the monsoon.

## History

According to Wikipedia, the area's recorded history goes back to the Mathara dynasty in about the 5th century CE. Later, a branch of the **Ganga** rulers established the **Bamanda (Bamra)** state, traditionally said to have been founded in 1360 CE by Saraju Gangadeb. Deogarh became its capital. In 1901 the state covered about 5,149 sq km and had 123,378 people. Its forests yielded timber and lac, and the Bengal Nagpur Railway crossed its north-eastern corner, with stations at Bamra Road and Garpos. Like Sambalpur, Bamra was moved between provinces in the colonial period: it was attached to the Central Provinces until 1905, to Bengal until 1912, and to Bihar and Orissa until 1936, before joining the new province of Orissa.

Bamanda is remembered as one of Odisha's most progressive princely states. **Raja Basudeb Sudhal Deb**, who ruled from 1869 to 1903, was a patron of literature and education. He founded a high school at Deogarh and set up the **Jagannath Ballav Press** in 1886. From 1889 the state published the Odia weekly ***Sambalpur Hitaishini***. Wikipedia credits his reign with the state's own postal system (linked to the British system in 1895), a telegraph service, and a long telephone line between Bamra and Barkote opened in 1900.

Wikipedia also records that Bamanda issued its own postage stamps and paper currency, and describes the Bamra–Barkote telephone line as one of the longest in India at the time.

| Ruler | Reign | Remembered for |
|---|---|---|
| Basudeb Sudhal Deb | 1869–1903 | Printing press, *Sambalpur Hitaishini*, school, posts and telegraph |

His successor, **Raja Sachidananda Tribhuban Dev** (ruled 1903–1916), introduced a hydroelectric system at the Kodarkot (Kurudkut) waterfall. It supplied power to the palace and to local industries, including a sugar factory, from around 1908 according to Wikipedia.

On **1 January 1948** the last ruler of Bamanda signed accession to the Indian Union, and the state was merged into Odisha as part of Sambalpur district. Deogarh remained a sub-division of Sambalpur until it became a separate district in 1994.

## Places to visit

### Pradhanpat Waterfall

The Pradhanpat waterfall falls from the hill directly above Deogarh town and is the district's best-known sight. A shrine of **Maa Pradhani** stands near the falls, and the waterfall also supplies drinking water to the town. A local festival, the Pradhanpat Utsav, is held here each year.

### Kurudkut (Kodarkot) Waterfall

This forest waterfall near Deogarh is where the Bamanda rulers set up their early hydroelectric scheme. Spellings vary between sources (Kurudkut, Kodarkot, Kurdkot, Kuddukut). It is best seen after the monsoon and in winter.

### Royal palace and Kailash Palace

The palace of the former Bamanda rulers stands in Deogarh town. OdishaTV also describes a **Kailash Palace**, which it says was built in 1919 by Raja Dibya Sankar Dev and has been developed for eco-tourism. Check access locally, as parts of these buildings may be private.

### Deojharan Waterfall

A waterfall in thick forest with a cool spring where visitors bathe. OdishaTV notes that the site was historically used as a camping ground.

### Gohira Dam

A dam and reservoir in the district that OdishaTV describes as a scenic picnic spot.

### Jagannath Temple

A temple of Lord Jagannath in Deogarh town, built in the Kalinga style and a major place of worship for local people.

## Culture and festivals

Deogarh shares the culture of western Odisha. [Nuakhai](/culture/nuakhai), the festival of the new rice crop, is celebrated in every home. [Rath Yatra](/culture/rath-yatra) and Durga Puja are other major festivals, and the Pradhanpat Utsav celebrates the town's waterfall.

The district's literary heritage is one of its proudest traditions. The Jagannath Ballav Press and the weekly *Sambalpur Hitaishini* made Deogarh an early centre of Odia publishing in the late 19th century.

Adivasi communities, including Munda, Kisan, Oraon and Ho speakers, live in the forested villages and keep their own festivals, songs and dances. Folk forms of the region, grouped under [Sambalpuri dance](/culture/sambalpuri-dance), are performed at festivals.

## Food

Deogarh's food is that of western Odisha. Rice is the staple, and the forests supply seasonal foods such as mushrooms (**ruguda**) and bamboo shoots (**kardi**). Snacks and sweets common across the region, such as **chaula bara** and **rasbaraa**, are also eaten here.

## Economy

Deogarh is mainly an agricultural and forest district. Paddy is the main crop, and many families depend on forest produce for part of their income. There is little large industry. Wikipedia notes several planned industrial projects, but the district has stayed largely rural. Small-scale tourism centres on Pradhanpat and the other waterfalls.

## Notable people

- **Raja Basudeb Sudhal Deb** (ruled 1869–1903): ruler of Bamanda, founder of the Jagannath Ballav Press and the weekly *Sambalpur Hitaishini*, and a noted patron of Odia literature and education.
- **Raja Sachidananda Tribhuban Dev** (ruled 1903–1916): ruler of Bamanda who brought hydroelectric power to Deogarh.

## How to reach

- **Air**: the nearest airport is Veer Surendra Sai Airport, Jharsuguda, about 98 km away. Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, is about 265 km away.
- **Rail**: Deogarh has no railway station of its own. The nearest railheads are Sambalpur (about 90 km), Jharsuguda (about 98 km), Bamra (about 103 km) and Rourkela (about 115 km).
- **Road**: Deogarh is on the old National Highway 6 route, about 90 km east of Sambalpur and about 265 km from Bhubaneswar. Distances are as given by Wikipedia.
