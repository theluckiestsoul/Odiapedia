**Sundargarh** (ସୁନ୍ଦରଗଡ଼) is a district in north-western Odisha, bordering Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand. Sundargarh district is known for the steel city of Rourkela, its mineral wealth, its large Adivasi population and its long record of producing India hockey players.

## Overview

Sundargarh is the second-largest district in Odisha, covering 9,712 sq km, or a little over 6 percent of the state. Its headquarters is the town of Sundargarh. The district's largest city, however, is **Rourkela**, which grew up around the Rourkela Steel Plant from the late 1950s.

The district was formed on **1 January 1948**, when the princely states of **Gangpur** and **Bonai** acceded to India and were merged into Odisha. It has three sub-divisions: Sundargarh, Panposh (which includes Rourkela) and Bonai.

At the 2011 Census, Sundargarh had 2,093,437 people. About half the population (50.75 percent) belonged to Scheduled Tribes, the largest groups being the Oraon, Munda, Kisan, Kharia, Bhuiyan and Gond. Odia is the main language, but Sadri, Mundari, Hindi, Kurukh, Kisan and Kharia are also widely spoken. With more than a third of its people living in towns, Sundargarh is one of the more urbanised districts of Odisha.

## Geography and climate

Sundargarh lies on the northern edge of Odisha. It borders Raigarh and Jashpur districts of Chhattisgarh to the west and north-west, Jharkhand to the north and north-east, and the Odisha districts of Kendujhar, Angul, Deogarh, Sambalpur and Jharsuguda to the east and south.

The landscape is a hilly plateau cut by river valleys and covered in large areas of forest. The **Brahmani** river is formed near Rourkela, where the **Sankh** and **South Koel** rivers meet at Vedvyas. The **Ib**, a tributary of the Mahanadi, drains the western part of the district. The Bonai hills in the east are rich in iron ore and hold the tall Khandadhar waterfall.

## History

The Sundargarh area is generally counted as part of ancient South Kosala. In recent centuries it was divided between two princely states. **Gangpur**, with its capital at Sundargarh, was ruled by the **Sekhar** family, while **Bonai**, with its capital at Bonaigarh, was ruled by a **Kadamba** line. In both cases, tradition says the dynasties were founded with the help of local Bhuyan leaders.

Both states were tributaries of Sambalpur and so came under the Maratha rulers of Nagpur. According to the district administration, they were ceded to the British in 1803, returned to the Marathas in 1806 and finally ceded in 1826. In 1905 they were moved from the control of the Chota Nagpur commissioner to that of Odisha.

On **25 April 1939**, troops opened fire on a gathering of tribal peasants at **Amko Simko**, near Simko village. The crowd had gathered for a no-rent movement led by **Nirmal Munda** against heavy taxes in Gangpur State. The number of people killed is disputed. The Government of Odisha figure is 39, other records give about 49, and Harekrushna Mahtab wrote of a much higher toll. The event is remembered as one of the major tribal protests in Odisha's freedom movement.

After Independence, the rulers of Gangpur and Bonai signed accession on 1 January 1948, and the two states together became Sundargarh district. In the 1950s a public-sector steel plant was built at **Rourkela** with West German collaboration. President Rajendra Prasad lit its first blast furnace on 3 February 1959.

## Places to visit

### Vedvyas

Vedvyas, on the edge of Rourkela, is where the Sankh and South Koel rivers meet to form the Brahmani. Local tradition links the place with the sage Vyasa. Some accounts say he composed the Mahabharata here, while others link it to the story of his birth. A temple at the confluence draws pilgrims.

### Khandadhar Falls

Near Nandapani in the Bonai area, Khandadhar Falls drops about 244 m (801 ft) in a single plunge, fed by the Kora Nala. Wikipedia describes it as the second-highest waterfall in Odisha. Its name comes from its sword-like shape (*khanda* means sword). It is about 104 km from Rourkela via Bonaigarh. It is different from the Khandadhar falls in Kendujhar district.

### Mandira Dam

Mandira Dam on the Sankh river near Kansbahal was built after 1957 to supply water to the Rourkela Steel Plant. The district administration puts it about 26 km from Rourkela by road. Its reservoir is a popular picnic spot.

### Hanuman Vatika

A temple garden in Rourkela known for its very tall statue of Hanuman, one of the city's best-known landmarks.

### Birsa Munda International Hockey Stadium

Opened in Rourkela on 5 January 2023, this stadium seats about 21,800 people. It co-hosted the 2023 Men's FIH Hockey World Cup with Bhubaneswar's Kalinga Stadium. Guinness World Records recognised it as the largest fully seated hockey arena in the world.

## Culture and festivals

Sundargarh's culture is shaped by its many Adivasi communities, among them the Oraon, Munda, Kisan, Kharia, Bhuiyan and Gond. The **Karma** festival, with its group dances and songs, is celebrated widely in the region, as are [Nuakhai](/culture/nuakhai), Durga Puja and [Rath Yatra](/culture/rath-yatra). Sadri, a link language of the Chota Nagpur region, is widely spoken alongside Odia, Mundari and Kurukh.

Rourkela, built around the steel plant, has drawn workers from across India, and Christian, Hindu and Muslim festivals are all marked in the city.

The district's languages reflect this diversity. At the 2011 Census about 44 percent of people gave Odia as their mother tongue, about 15 percent Sadri, and about 10 percent Mundari, with Hindi, Kurukh, Kisan and Kharia each spoken by several percent. Among the Scheduled Tribes, the Oraon made up about 14 percent of the district's population, the Munda about 11 percent, and the Kisan and Kharia about 7 percent each.

**Hockey** is part of the district's identity. Village teams across the tribal belt have produced generations of national players. Two of India's most decorated defenders, Dilip Tirkey and Amit Rohidas, both come from Saunamara village. With the Birsa Munda stadium in Rourkela, the district can now host international tournaments.

## Food

Everyday meals in Sundargarh are rice-based, like the rest of western Odisha. Rural cooking makes good use of forest and seasonal foods such as mushrooms and bamboo shoots, while Rourkela offers food from many parts of India. Dishes common across western Odisha, such as **chaula bara** and bamboo-shoot preparations, are also eaten here.

## Economy

Sundargarh is one of Odisha's main industrial and mining districts.

- **Steel**: the **Rourkela Steel Plant**, run by the Steel Authority of India (SAIL), was India's first integrated steel plant in the public sector. After its 2010–12 expansion it has a capacity of about 4.2 million tonnes of crude steel a year.
- **Mining**: the district has deposits of iron ore, manganese and limestone; iron-ore mining in the Bonai hills has even affected the flow of Khandadhar Falls.
- **Cement and other industry**: Rajgangpur has a large cement plant (now run by Dalmia Cement), and many smaller industrial units operate around Rourkela.
- **Agriculture**: most rural households farm paddy on rain-fed land and collect forest produce.

In 2006 the central government listed Sundargarh among India's 250 most backward districts, a measure that reflects the wide gap between its industrial towns and its rural interior.

## Notable people

- **Dilip Tirkey** (born 1977, Saunamara village): former India hockey captain who played 412 internationals; Padma Shri (2004) and later president of Hockey India.
- **Ignace Tirkey** (born 1981, Lulkidihi): India hockey player, Asia Cup gold medallist; Arjuna Award (2009) and Padma Shri (2010).
- **Amit Rohidas** (Saunamara village): India hockey defender who won Olympic bronze medals in 2020 (Tokyo) and 2024 (Paris).
- **Jual Oram** (born 1961, Kendudihi village): MP for Sundargarh and India's first Union Minister of Tribal Affairs (1999).
- **Nirmal Munda**: leader of the 1939 no-rent movement in Gangpur State, later honoured as a freedom fighter.

## How to reach

- **Air**: Rourkela has a small airport about 6 km from the city, served by regional flights. Veer Surendra Sai Airport at Jharsuguda is another option for the south of the district. Check current schedules before planning.
- **Rail**: Rourkela Junction is on the Howrah–Mumbai main line, with trains to Bhubaneswar, Kolkata, Delhi, Mumbai and other cities. Sundargarh town and other parts of the district are served by smaller stations.
- **Road**: Rourkela is about 328 km from Bhubaneswar by road, according to Wikipedia. The Biju Expressway connects Rourkela with Sambalpur and southern Odisha.
