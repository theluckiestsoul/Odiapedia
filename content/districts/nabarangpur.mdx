## At a Glance
Nabarangpur is predominantly inhabited by tribes. It is an agrarian district and a leading producer of maize. The district comes alive during the **Mondei** festival, a carnival of tribal art and culture.

## The Heritage Diary
In the 4th–5th centuries CE the Nala dynasty, with its seat at Pushkari near Umerkote, controlled the Bastar–Koraput region. The area later shared its history with the Jeypore kingdom. The district has ruins of ancient forts and temples, such as at Podagada.

## Local Landmarks
- **Podagada:** A site with fort ruins and an inscription associated with the Nala dynasty.
- **Papadahandi:** Site of a memorial to the freedom fighters killed on 24 August 1942, when police fired on a procession at the flooded Turi river; 19 people died.

## Taste of the Land
- **Maize products:** With maize being abundant, various local snacks are prepared from it.
- **Mahua:** A drink and food source collected from Mahua flowers.

## Local Heroes
- **Martyrs of Papadahandi:** The 19 people killed in the 1942 police firing are remembered as the district's freedom-struggle martyrs.
