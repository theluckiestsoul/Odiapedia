## Overview

**Nabarangpur** (ନବରଙ୍ଗପୁର), also spelled Nabarangapur or Nowrangpur, is a district in southern Odisha on the border with Chhattisgarh. Nabarangpur is predominantly rural and Adivasi, and is known as Odisha's largest maize-growing district, for the **Mondei** harvest festival, and for the **Papadahandi** police firing of 1942 during the Quit India Movement.

The district headquarters is the town of **Nabarangpur**. Another important town is **Umerkote**, near the ancient fort of Podagada.

Nabarangpur was a sub-division of undivided [Koraput](/district/koraput) district from 1941. On **2 October 1992**, Gandhi Jayanti, the Government of Odisha made it a separate district, as recorded by both the district administration and the district court.

The district covers **5,291 sq km** according to the 2011 Census (Wikipedia gives 5,294 sq km). The 2011 Census counted **1,220,946** people, about 56% of them from Scheduled Tribes. Only about 7% of people live in towns.

## Geography and climate

Nabarangpur lies on a forested plateau in the south-west of Odisha. It borders Koraput and Kalahandi districts in Odisha and the Bastar region of Chhattisgarh. Wikipedia describes hills of up to about 1,000 m in the west, the Eastern Ghats in the east and more open plains in the south.

**Rivers.** The **Indravati**, which rises in Kalahandi, forms part of the boundary with Koraput. The **Turi**, a smaller river, is remembered for the 1942 firing at Papadahandi.

**Climate.** The district has a tropical climate with a hot summer, a wet monsoon and a mild winter.

## History

**Ancient period.** The district administration describes the region as part of the forest country known in early sources as *Atavi* and *Mahakantara*. In the 5th century CE the **Nala** kings ruled the Bastar–Koraput region. Their capital, **Pushkari**, has long been identified with **Podagada**, about 23 km from Umerkote, where an inscription of the Nala king **Skandavarman** records that he built a shrine to Vishnu and repopulated the deserted city of Pushkari. More recent scholarship, summarised on Wikipedia, places Pushkari in Bastar instead, so the identification remains debated. The district administration names the Nala kings Bhavadatta Varman, Arthapati and Skandavarman.

**Jeypore period.** In later centuries the area formed part of the kingdom ruled from Nandapur and then Jeypore. Wikipedia notes that **Vikram Dev I** of Jeypore (ruled 1758–1781) appointed his son Jagannath Dev as the zamindar of Nowrangpur. Under British rule the region was administered from the Madras Presidency, and assistant agents were posted at Nabarangpur from 1860, according to the district administration. It became part of the new province of Orissa with Koraput district in 1936.

**Papadahandi, 1942.** On **24 August 1942**, during the Quit India Movement, a large procession of Congress workers and Adivasi supporters marched towards Papadahandi, and OrissaPOST notes that the arrest of Laxman Nayak was one of the triggers. At the bridge over the flooded **Turi** river, police opened fire. **Nineteen people were killed**, many were injured and about 140 were arrested. OrissaPOST reports that a martyrs' memorial (Sahid Minar) was inaugurated at the site on 24 August 1980, and the martyrs are remembered there every year.

**Resettlement and district formation.** In 1958 refugees from East Pakistan were settled around Umerkote under the Dandakaranya Project, and Bengali is still spoken in parts of the district. Nabarangpur became a district on 2 October 1992.

## Places to visit

### Podagada

The hill fort at Podagada, about 23 km from Umerkote, is the district's main archaeological site. According to the district administration, it has the remains of fortifications, Shiva and Vishnu temples, pottery and the Nala-period stone inscription. Odisha Tourism lists Podagada Fort among the district's attractions.

### Papadahandi martyrs' memorial

The memorial at Papadahandi, beside the Turi river, honours the 19 people killed in the police firing of 24 August 1942. Commemorations are held on national days and on the anniversary of the firing.

### Maa Bhandargharani temple

Odisha Tourism lists the temple of Maa Bhandargharani as one of the principal shrines of Nabarangpur district.

### Natural spots

Odisha Tourism also lists **Hirli Dongri** and **Chandan Dhara** as natural attractions of the district. Little verified information about them is available online, so ask locally before visiting.

### Nearby

Nabarangpur makes a good base for [Koraput](/travel/koraput) attractions such as Gupteswar and Deomali, both of which Odisha Tourism suggests as side trips from the district.

## Culture and festivals

Nabarangpur's culture is shaped by its Adivasi communities, including the **Gond**, **Bhatra**, **Paraja**, **Bhottada**, **Gadaba** and **Kondh**. In the 2011 Census about 69% of people spoke Odia (including the regional **Desia** variety, see [Odia dialects](/language/odia-dialects)) as their first language, followed by Bhatri, Gondi, Bengali and Chhattisgarhi.

**Mondei.** The best-known festival is **Mondei**, celebrated in the harvest season. Communities gather near their village deities to worship the first harvest, sell paddy, maize and vegetables, and perform dances. The district administration and the Culture Department now organise a district-level Mondei at Nabarangpur that brings together tribal dance troupes from the interior and artists from other regions. Dates vary from year to year.

**Crafts.** Odisha Tourism highlights lacquer craft and handmade toys among the district's traditional crafts.

**Rice.** The aromatic **Koraput Kalajeera** rice, which received a Geographical Indication in 2024, is grown in Nabarangpur as well as Koraput. See [GI tags of Odisha](/culture/odisha-gi-tags).

## Food

Maize and millets are central to rural food in Nabarangpur. **Mandia peja**, a gruel of ragi and rice, is a staple of Adivasi households in Nabarangpur and the neighbouring districts of southern Odisha. Rice remains the main grain, and everyday Odia dishes such as [dalma](/food/dalma) are eaten across the district.

## Economy

**Agriculture.** Nabarangpur is an agrarian district and is known as the **largest producer of maize in Odisha**. Millets, sugarcane, cashew nuts and forest produce are other important sources of income.

**Development.** In 2006 the Ministry of Panchayati Raj listed Nabarangpur among India's 250 most backward districts, and it receives support under the Backward Regions Grant Fund. It is part of the KBK (Kalahandi–Balangir–Koraput) region.

## Notable people

- **Sadashiva Tripathy** (born 1910): born in Nabarangpur, he was the fifth Chief Minister of Odisha (1965–1967) and represented Umerkote in the Odisha Legislative Assembly.
- **The Papadahandi martyrs**: the 19 people killed in the police firing of 24 August 1942 are remembered as the district's freedom-struggle martyrs.
