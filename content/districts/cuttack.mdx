## Overview

**Cuttack** (କଟକ) is a district in coastal Odisha, centred on the old city of **Cuttack** at the head of the Mahanadi delta. The city lies between the Mahanadi and Kathajodi rivers. It served as Odisha's main seat of government for centuries, was the capital of the province until 1948, and is still home to the **Orissa High Court**.

The district is known for **tarakasi**, the silver filigree that gives Cuttack the name "Silver City", and for the ruins of **Barabati Fort**. Its festivals include the riverside fair of [Bali Jatra](/culture/bali-jatra) and silver-decorated [Durga Puja](/culture/durga-puja) pandals. The district's western hills hold the Dhabaleswar island temple and the oxbow lake of Ansupa.

Cuttack has been a district since October 1828. In the early 1990s the large old district was split, and **Jajpur**, **Jagatsinghpur** and **Kendrapara** became separate districts. Today Cuttack has three sub-divisions: **Cuttack Sadar**, **Athagarh** and **Banki**.

At the 2011 Census the district had **2,624,470** people, the second-highest of any Odisha district, on **3,932 sq km**, with a literacy rate of 85.50%. In June 2026 the state government revised the official English spelling of the name to **Kataka**, along with 63 other places.

## Geography and climate

The **Mahanadi** cuts across the district from west to east. Below Naraj it splits into distributaries, including the **Kathajodi**, **Birupa** and **Chitrotpala**, which spread across a flat alluvial delta. Wikipedia describes this eastern Sadar area as low, waterlogged and laced with canals.

The Athagarh and Banki sub-divisions in the west are quite different. They are broken hill country, with rounded hills of up to about 2,500 feet near the Hindol border and fertile valleys between them.

The district borders [Angul](/district/angul), [Dhenkanal](/district/dhenkanal), [Nayagarh](/district/nayagarh) and [Khordha](/district/khordha) on the west and south, and [Puri](/district/puri), [Jagatsinghpur](/district/jagatsinghpur), [Kendrapara](/district/kendrapara) and [Jajpur](/district/jajpur) on the east and north.

## History

The name comes from *kataka*, a military camp or seat of government. According to tradition, the city was founded as a fortified camp in the late 10th century. Odisha Tourism dates it to **989 CE** under King Nrupa Keshari, while its Barabati Fort page credits Markata Keshari with founding the fort in 987 CE. Wikipedia describes Cuttack as an administrative centre since the Kesari kings of the 10th century.

Under the **Eastern Gangas** and the Gajapati kings, Barabati became the main royal fort of Odisha. After the defeat of Mukunda Deva in **1568**, the region came under Afghan and later Mughal rule. The coastal plain around Cuttack became known as **Mughalbandi** from the Mughal revenue settlements. The Marathas held Cuttack from about 1750 until the East India Company took it in **1803**.

In October 1828 the Company divided its Odisha territory into three districts, Balasore, Cuttack and Jagannath (Puri). Banki was added in 1840. After independence the princely states of Athagarh, Tigiria, Baramba and Narasinghpur were merged into the district in 1948.

Cuttack was the birthplace of Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose in 1897 and the home of the early Odia nationalist movement led by [Madhusudan Das](/people/madhusudan-das). When Odisha became a separate province on 1 April 1936 (see [Utkal Divas](/culture/utkal-divas)), Cuttack was its capital. The capital moved to Bhubaneswar in 1948.

## Places to visit

### Barabati Fort

The ruins of Barabati Fort stand on the Mahanadi. They include a moat, a gateway and the mound of a palace once said to have had nine storeys. Sources differ on its founding: some credit the Somavamsi king Markata Keshari in the 10th century, others the Eastern Gangas in the 13th to 14th centuries. The fort is a Monument of National Importance. See the [Cuttack travel guide](/travel/cuttack).

### Netaji Birth Place Museum

The family home of Subhas Chandra Bose in the old city is now a museum of his life, with photographs, letters and personal belongings.

### Cuttack Chandi and the city shrines

The **Cuttack Chandi** temple is dedicated to the city's presiding goddess and is the focus of its Durga Puja. The **Qadam-e-Rasool** is a historic Muslim shrine visited by people of several faiths. The **Odisha State Maritime Museum**, opened in 2013, tells the story of Odia seafaring; see [Odisha's maritime history](/history/odisha-maritime-history).

### Dhabaleswar

This Shiva temple stands on an island in the Mahanadi about 27 km from Cuttack, near Athagarh. It is dated to the 10th century under the Somavamsis. A wire-rope suspension footbridge from Mancheswar, described as Odisha's first, gives access to the island.

### Ansupa Lake

Ansupa is a horseshoe-shaped oxbow lake of about 231 hectares on the left bank of the Mahanadi, opposite Banki and about 40 km from Cuttack. It lies below the Saranda hills, draws migratory birds in winter, and was designated a Ramsar site in 2021.

### Charchika and Bhattarika temples

At **Banki**, the Charchika temple houses an image of Chamunda dated to the 9th or 10th century, in a temple rebuilt in the 19th century. **Bhattarika** temple at Sasanga, on the Mahanadi in Baramba area, is another major shrine of the goddess.

### Other protected monuments

Besides Barabati, the Archaeological Survey of India protects several lesser-known sites in the district as Monuments of National Importance. They include a ruined fortress at **Choudwar**, the Churangarh (Sarangarh) fort at **Dadhapatna**, the remains of a Buddhist temple at **Bandareswar** and a monolithic pillar at **Sriapur**. Few have visitor facilities, so ask locally before you go.

### Naraj and Deojhar

**Naraj**, where the Mahanadi branches near Cuttack, is a local pilgrimage site, and **Deojhar** waterfall lies in the western hills.

## Culture and festivals

[Durga Puja](/culture/durga-puja) in Cuttack is famous for its *chandi medha*, silver backdrops and ornaments made by filigree workers for the goddess. [Bali Jatra](/culture/bali-jatra) opens on Kartika Purnima on the Mahanadi bank near Barabati Fort. It recalls the voyages of Odia merchants to Bali and Java and has become one of the largest open-air fairs in India. Dawn [Boita Bandana](/culture/boita-bandana) launches of toy boats mark the same morning. The Cuttack district administration announced the 2026 fair for 24 November to 1 December.

Cuttack's crafts have two registered Geographical Indications. **Cuttack Rupa Tarakasi (Silver Filigree)** was registered in 2024; read more on our [tarakasi](/culture/tarakasi-silver-filigree) page. **Khandua Saree and Fabrics**, the silk ikat of **Nuapatna and Maniabandha**, was registered in 2010 and is traditionally linked to Lord Jagannath; see [Khandua silk](/culture/khandua-silk).

## Food

Cuttack is known across Odisha for street food. Its signature dish is [dahibara aloodum](/food/dahibara-aloodum): lentil fritters soaked in spiced yoghurt water, served with spicy potato curry. **Thunka puri**, a large puffed puri, is sold at the Bali Jatra fair. **Salepur**, in the east of the district, is a well-known [rasagola](/food/rasagola) centre.

## Economy

Cuttack is often called Odisha's commercial capital. Wikipedia notes its wholesale textile trade, its silver filigree workshops and its industrial belt, which includes **Choudwar** (ferro-alloys and power), the **Jagatpur** industrial estate and units around **Athagarh**. The High Court, SCB Medical College and many educational institutions anchor a large services sector.

The ICAR **National Rice Research Institute** (formerly the Central Rice Research Institute) is at Cuttack. In the delta, paddy farming supported by canals and river water remains the main rural livelihood.

## Notable people

- **Subhas Chandra Bose** (born 1897): nationalist leader and head of the Indian National Army, born in Cuttack.
- **[Madhusudan Das](/people/madhusudan-das)** (1848–1934): Utkal Gourab, lawyer and architect of the movement for a separate Odisha, born at Satyabhamapur in Cuttack district.
- **[Biju Patnaik](/people/biju-patnaik)** (1916–1997): aviator and twice Chief Minister of Odisha, born in Cuttack.
- **[Rama Devi](/people/rama-devi)** (1899–1985): freedom fighter and social worker, born in Cuttack.
- **[Gopinath Mohanty](/people/gopinath-mohanty)** (1914–1991): novelist and Jnanpith Award winner, born at Nagabali near Cuttack.

## How to reach

- **By air:** Biju Patnaik International Airport in Bhubaneswar is the nearest airport, about 35 km from Barabati Fort according to Odisha Tourism.
- **By rail:** Cuttack railway station is on the main Howrah–Chennai line, about 5 km from Barabati Fort.
- **By road:** National Highway 16 (Kolkata–Chennai) passes through the district. Cuttack is about 22 to 28 km from Bhubaneswar, depending on the route, with frequent buses and taxis between the twin cities.
