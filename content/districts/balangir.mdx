**Balangir** (ବଲାଙ୍ଗୀର), also spelt Bolangir, is a district in western Odisha. It was once the heart of the Chauhan-ruled **Patna State**. Balangir district is known for the Yogini temple at Ranipur Jharial, the Harishankar temple on the Gandhamardan hills, Sambalpuri handloom and the Kosli (Sambalpuri) language and culture.

## Overview

The district takes its name from its headquarters town. According to the district administration, the name comes from **Balaramgarh**, a fort built in the 16th century by **Balaram Deo**, the Chauhan prince who also founded the Sambalpur kingdom.

Balangir covers 6,575 sq km and had 1,648,997 people at the 2011 Census. It is largely rural: only about 12 percent of people lived in towns. It has three sub-divisions (Balangir, Patnagarh and Titlagarh) and 14 blocks. Scheduled Tribes make up about a fifth of the population and Scheduled Castes a little under a fifth.

The present district took shape after the princely states of **Patna** and **Sonepur** merged with Odisha on **1 January 1948**. Sonepur was later separated as **Subarnapur district** on 1 April 1993.

Balangir is part of Odisha's drought-prone western uplands. Seasonal migration for work has long been a feature of rural life, and in 2006 the central government listed it among India's 250 most backward districts.

## Geography and climate

Balangir lies in the basin of the **Tel**, a major tributary of the Mahanadi. Other rivers include the Suktel, Ang, Jira and Saleshing. The **Gandhamardan** hills, rising to about 1,005 m (3,296 ft), lie on the district's border with Bargarh and are known for their medicinal plants. Other high points include Butel and Chahdli.

The Gandhamardan range covers about 190 sq km across Balangir and Bargarh. The Botanical Survey of India has recorded about 220 plant species of medicinal value there, and two Ayurvedic colleges and hospitals stand on either side of the hills. The hills also hold bauxite. A lease granted to the state-owned company BALCO in 1983 led to a long local protest movement, and the mining project was abandoned in the late 1980s. In March 2023 the range was declared a Biodiversity Heritage Site, according to Down To Earth. Sources differ slightly on its highest point, giving about 1,005 m or 1,060 m.

The district is famous for its harsh summers. Titlagarh is regularly among the hottest places in India and has recorded 50.1 °C.

## History

The area was part of ancient **South Kosala**. In the medieval period, according to tradition, descendants of Prithviraj Chauhan settled in the region after his defeat by Muhammad of Ghor. **Ramai Deo** is regarded as the founder of Chauhan rule at **Patnagarh**, usually dated to about 1360 CE. Patnagarh was the capital of a confederacy of hill forts known as the *Athara Garh* (eighteen forts).

In the 16th century **Balaram Deo** founded the Sambalpur kingdom, and the Balaramgarh fort gave Balangir its name. Over time the capital of Patna State moved from Patnagarh to Balangir, and Patnagarh declined after the 16th century.

The last ruler of Patna State, **Maharaja Rajendra Narayan Singh Deo** (1912–1975), took full powers in 1933. After the state merged with Odisha in 1948, he led the Ganatantra Parishad and served as **Chief Minister of Odisha from 1967 to 1971**, the first Chief Minister of the state from outside the Congress.

## Places to visit

### Ranipur Jharial

Ranipur Jharial, near Titlagarh and Kantabanji, is one of Odisha's most important early temple sites. Its best-known monument is a circular, roofless (hypaethral) **Chausath Yogini** temple, about 50 feet across, dated to the 9th or 10th century CE. A shrine with a dancing Shiva stands at the centre. Sources differ on how many of the 64 Yogini images survive; counts range from about 48 to 62. The site also has a large brick temple and several smaller stone temples. Ranipur Jharial was the first Yogini temple to be described by a modern scholar, Major-General John Campbell, in 1853.

### Harishankar

Harishankar lies on the southern slope of the **Gandhamardan** hills, about 81 km from Balangir according to Odisha Tourism. The temple is dedicated to both Hari (Vishnu) and Shankar (Shiva) and was built in the 14th century at the wish of Queen Durlabha Devi, wife of the Chauhan king Vaijjal Dev. A stream tumbles over granite beside the temple, and pilgrims bathe in its pools. A trail across the hill links Harishankar with the Nrusinghanath temple on the northern slope, in Bargarh district. A dancing Ganesha image of about the 12th century has been found at the site, and some scholars link the plateau between the two temples with Parimalagiri, a Buddhist monastery mentioned by the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang.

### Patnagarh

Patnagarh, about 36 km from Balangir, was the first capital of the Chauhan kings. The **Patneswari Temple**, of the goddess worshipped by the Patna royal family, stands here.

### Balangir town

The town has the **Sailashree Palace** of the former rulers and several temples, including shrines of Samaleswari and Patneswari.

### Titlagarh

Titlagarh is a railway junction and market town, best known for its extreme summer heat; visit between November and February.

## Culture and festivals

Balangir is one of the heartlands of the Kosli or Sambalpuri language. In the 2011 Census about a fifth of the people returned Sambalpuri as their mother tongue, with most of the rest returning Odia.

Sambalpuri, also called Kosli or Western Odia, is written in the Odia script. The 2011 Census counted about 2.63 million native speakers, and Balangir holds about an eighth of them. Speakers often regard it as a language of its own, while linguists usually class it as a variety of Odia; a survey in 2006 found that it shares about three-quarters of its basic vocabulary with standard Odia. Supporters campaign for its inclusion in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution.

- **[Nuakhai](/culture/nuakhai)**, the harvest festival of new rice in Bhadraba (August–September), is the district's biggest family festival.
- **[Sambalpuri dance](/culture/sambalpuri-dance)** forms such as Dalkhai and Rasarkeli are performed at festivals and weddings.
- **Handloom**: Balangir is one of the main weaving districts for the GI-registered [Sambalpuri saree](/culture/sambalpuri-saree) and bandha fabrics. See [Odisha's GI tags](/culture/odisha-gi-tags).
- **Temple festivals**: Harishankar draws large crowds at Shivaratri, Makar Sankranti and during the Magha fair.

## Food

Balangir's food belongs to the western Odisha tradition. **Chaula bara**, crisp fried rice-flour fritters served with green chillies and tamarind soup, is a popular snack in Balangir and nearby towns. **Gupchup**, the local name for pani puri, is a favourite street food. Mutton curry (**mansa jhol**) is a feature of feasts such as Chhadkhai. Sweets include **rasbaraa** and **tikhri**, and bamboo-shoot dishes made with **kardi** and **hendua** are common.

## Economy

Agriculture is the mainstay of Balangir, with paddy grown largely on rain-fed land. Because rainfall is uneven and droughts are frequent, many rural workers migrate seasonally to other states. Handloom weaving provides important additional income in weaving villages. The district has an Indian Ordnance Factory, and Balangir town is a trading centre for the surrounding area. Bhima Bhoi Medical College and Hospital, inaugurated in 2018, has strengthened the town's role as a health and education centre.

## Notable people

- **Rajendra Narayan Singh Deo** (1912–1975): last Maharaja of Patna State and Chief Minister of Odisha from 1967 to 1971.
- **Ramai Deo**: traditionally regarded as the founder of Chauhan rule at Patnagarh in the 14th century.
- **Balaram Deo**: Chauhan prince credited with the Balaramgarh fort that gave Balangir its name, and with founding the Sambalpur kingdom.
- **Rasanara Parwin**: cricketer from Balangir who has played for the India women's team.

## How to reach

- **Air**: Veer Surendra Sai Airport, Jharsuguda, is the nearest airport. Biju Patnaik International Airport in Bhubaneswar is farther away.
- **Rail**: Balangir is on the Jharsuguda–Sambalpur–Titlagarh line, and Titlagarh is a junction for the Raipur and Vizianagaram lines. The Khurda Road–Balangir railway project, built in stages, is designed to give the district a more direct rail link with Bhubaneswar.
- **Road**: Balangir town is about 327 km from Bhubaneswar by road, according to Wikipedia. Harishankar is about 335 km from Bhubaneswar, according to Odisha Tourism.
