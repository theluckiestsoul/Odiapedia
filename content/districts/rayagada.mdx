## Overview

**Rayagada** (ରାୟଗଡ଼ା) is a district in southern Odisha, bordering Andhra Pradesh, with its headquarters at the town of Rayagada on the **Nagavali** river. Rayagada is known for the **Niyamgiri hills** and their **Dongria Kondh** people, for the **Majhighariani temple**, and for being an industrial and railway centre of southern Odisha.

The district has two sub-divisions, **Rayagada** and **Gunupur**, and 11 blocks. The Rayagada sub-division includes the Rayagada, Kashipur, Kalyansingpur and Kolnara blocks; the Gunupur sub-division includes Gunupur, Gudari, Padmapur, Ramanaguda, Bissam Cuttack, Muniguda and Chandrapur.

Rayagada was part of undivided [Koraput](/district/koraput) district until **2 October 1992**, when it was made a separate district.

The district covers **7,073 sq km** according to the 2011 Census (other sources, including Wikipedia, give about 7,585 sq km). The 2011 Census counted **967,911** people. About 56% belong to Scheduled Tribes, and the 2011 sex ratio was 1,051 females per 1,000 males.

## Geography and climate

Rayagada lies in the Eastern Ghats in the south of Odisha. Its neighbours include Koraput, Kalahandi and Gajapati districts and the state of Andhra Pradesh.

**Rivers.** The **Nagavali** flows past Rayagada town and on into Andhra Pradesh. The **Vamsadhara** is the district's other major river; at **Minajhola** it is joined by the Chauladhua and Phalaphalia streams.

**Hills and forests.** The **Niyamgiri** range lies about 40 km from Rayagada according to Odisha Tourism, and continues into Kalahandi. Much of the district is forested; Wikipedia gives about 4,785 sq km of forest area.

**Climate.** Like the rest of southern Odisha, Rayagada receives most of its rain from the south-west monsoon. Odisha Tourism recommends visiting from October to March.

## History

**Early history.** Rayagada lay within the ancient region of Kalinga. A 7th-century inscription at the Nilakantheswar temple of **Padmapur** indicates that the nearby Jagamanda hill once housed the monastery of the Buddhist logician **Dharmakirti**.

**Founding of Rayagada.** According to tradition recorded by Odisha Tourism and Wikipedia, **Vishwanath Dev Gajapati** of the Suryavamsi kings of Nandapur (who ruled in the 16th century; sources give his reign as beginning in 1527) built a fort and capital on the bank of the Nagavali and named it Rayagada. He is also credited with building the **Majhighariani temple** inside his palace, which gave the goddess her name, "the one seated in the central room".

**Jeypore and British rule.** The area remained part of the Jeypore kingdom, which became a feudatory of the Golconda sultans in 1571. The Nabarangpur district administration's history records that the British officer Captain Richard Mathews captured Rayagada on 15 January 1775. Under British rule Rayagada was part of the Jeypore estate in the Madras Presidency and joined the new province of Orissa as part of Koraput district in 1936.

**Railways and industry.** The Parvatipuram–Raipur railway through Rayagada was completed in **December 1931**, and the Koraput–Rayagada rail link followed on **31 December 1998**.

**Niyamgiri.** From the late 1990s a proposal to mine bauxite in the Niyamgiri hills for Vedanta's refinery at Lanjigarh in Kalahandi met strong opposition from the Dongria Kondh. In 2013 the Supreme Court of India ordered that the local village councils be consulted; all twelve councils consulted rejected mining, and the Union environment ministry refused clearance in January 2014.

## Places to visit

### Maa Majhighariani temple

The temple of **Maa Majhighariani**, on the bank of the Nagavali in Rayagada town, is the guardian shrine of the area. The district administration notes that devotees come from across southern Odisha and Andhra Pradesh, and from Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh. Odisha Tourism names Chaitra Parba, Durga Puja and Shivaratri as its main festivals. According to local belief, the goddess rides a horse at night to protect the town.

### Chatikona

Chatikona, about 48 km from Rayagada, lies at the foot of the Niyamgiri hills, which surround it on three sides. It has a temple of Lord Shiva (Pataleswar) and a waterfall fed by streams from the hills. Maha Shivaratri draws large crowds.

### Minajhola

Minajhola, about 134 km from Rayagada, is a Shiva shrine at the meeting of the Vamsadhara, Chauladhua and Phalaphalia rivers, surrounded by dense forest. Odisha Tourism notes that Maha Shivaratri brings many pilgrims.

### Hanging bridge at Chekaguda

This 151 m suspension bridge across the Nagavali at Chekaguda was built in 2011–2012 to connect tribal villages to Rayagada town, cutting a 25 km detour to about 5 km. Odisha Tourism lists it among the district's picturesque attractions.

### Laxminarayan temple, Therubali

At Therubali, about 21 km from Rayagada, the Laxminarayan temple stands in the township of the IMFA ferro-alloy plant and houses Laxminarayan, Hanuman and the Jagannath trinity.

### Padmapur

Padmapur, about 67 km from Rayagada, has the hilltop **Nilakantheswar** temple, with its 7th-century inscription linking the area to the Buddhist philosopher Dharmakirti.

### Other sites

Odisha Tourism also lists the **Hatipathar** waterfall. The Wikipedia district article mentions the **Maa Markama** temple at Bissam Cuttack and the Pataleswar temple at Paikapada.

## Culture and festivals

Rayagada's population is largely Adivasi. The main communities are the **Kondh** (including the **Dongria Kondh** and Kutia Kondh) and the **Saora** (Sora). In the 2011 Census, about 42% of people spoke Odia as their first language, about 33% **Kui**, about 10% Telugu and about 8% Sora.

**Dongria Kondh.** About 8,000 Dongria Kondh live in some 100 villages of the Niyamgiri hills, which span Rayagada and Kalahandi. They practise horticulture and shifting cultivation and revere **Niyam Raja**, the god of the hills. Odisha Tourism notes that Dongria women are recognisable by their facial tattoos, multiple ear-rings, a trio of nose rings and silver neck bands. They are recognised as a Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Group, so visitors should travel with licensed guides, ask before photographing anyone, and respect local rules.

**GI-registered crafts.** In January 2024 two crafts from Rayagada received Geographical Indication tags:

| Craft | Makers | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Kapadaganda shawl | Dongria Kondh women | Hand-embroidered shawl gifted as a mark of respect and affection |
| Lanjia Saura painting (idital) | Lanjia Saura community | White wall paintings on a red-brown background, traditionally on house walls |

See [GI tags of Odisha](/culture/odisha-gi-tags) for the full list.

**Festivals.** Major religious festivals include Chaitra Parba at the Majhighariani temple, [Durga Puja](/culture/durga-puja), Maha Shivaratri at Chatikona and Minajhola, and [Rath Yatra](/culture/rath-yatra), which the town of Kashipur celebrates on a large scale.

## Food

Rice and finger millet (*mandia*) are the staples of rural Rayagada. **Mandia peja**, a gruel of ragi and rice, is a daily food of Adivasi households in Rayagada and the neighbouring districts of southern Odisha. **Salap**, a palm sap, is drunk fresh or fermented in many tribal villages.

## Economy

**Agriculture.** The main crops are paddy, ragi, maize, pulses such as green gram and black gram, groundnut and sweet potato. Forest produce supports many Adivasi families.

**Industry and minerals.** The district has deposits of bauxite. Major industrial units include:
- **JK Paper** at Jaykaypur near Rayagada.
- **Indian Metals and Ferro Alloys (IMFA)**, whose main ferro-alloy plant is at Therubali.
- **Utkal Alumina International** (Hindalco) at Doraguda in Kashipur block, an alumina refinery near Kucheipadar village; the project faced local protests, notably in 2004.

**Railways.** On **1 June 2026** the new **Rayagada railway division** of East Coast Railway began operations, with Rayagada as its headquarters, covering lines in Rayagada, Koraput, Malkangiri, Nabarangpur, Gajapati and Kandhamal districts.

## Notable people

- **Giridhar Gamang** (born 1943): born at Dibirisingi in Rayagada district; Chief Minister of Odisha in 1999 and long-serving Lok Sabha member for Koraput.
- **Raghunath Panigrahi** (1932–2013): Odissi vocalist from Gunupur, famous for singing the *Gita Govinda* of [Jayadeva](/people/jayadeva); Padma Shri 2010.
- **Tapan Misra** (born 1961): ISRO scientist born in Rayagada; Director of the Space Applications Centre (2015–2018), known for the RISAT-1 radar.
- **Ritesh Agarwal** (born 1993): entrepreneur born at Bissam Cuttack; founder of OYO.
