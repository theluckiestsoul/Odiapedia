**Bhadrak** (ଭଦ୍ରକ) is a coastal district in north-eastern Odisha, on the Bay of Bengal, with its headquarters at Bhadrak town. Bhadrak district takes its name from the goddess **Bhadrakali**. It is known for the Shiva temple of Akhandalamani at Aradi, the deep-water **Dhamra Port** and the martyrs' memorial at **Eram**.

## Overview

Bhadrak is one of Odisha's smaller but more densely populated districts. Bhadrak town itself had 107,463 residents in 2011, up from about 18,600 in 1901. It covers 2,505 sq km and had 1,506,337 people in the 2011 Census, about 600 per sq km. Literacy was high at 82.78 percent, and about 12 percent of people lived in towns.

The district was carved out of the old Balasore district on **1 April 1993**, under a state government notification of 27 March 1993. It has seven blocks, with Bhadrak and Basudevpur as its main towns and Dhamra, Chandbali, Dhamnagar and Agarpada as smaller urban centres.

Bhadrak is mainly an agricultural district on the fertile coastal plain. Its coastline, the port at Dhamra and fishing harbours give it a maritime side. Bhadrak lies on NH-16 and the Howrah–Chennai railway.

## Geography and climate

Bhadrak lies on the northern Odisha coastal plain, with the Bay of Bengal to the east. Its neighbours are Balasore, from which it was separated, Kendujhar, Jajpur and Kendrapara.

The **Salandi** river flows past Bhadrak town. The **Baitarani** passes Chandbali and, together with the Brahmani, reaches the sea at the **Dhamra** estuary. Mangrove creeks near Chandbali lead towards Bhitarkanika in neighbouring Kendrapara district. Offshore lies **Dr. Abdul Kalam Island** (formerly Wheeler Island), which the DRDO uses as its Integrated Test Range for missiles. The island, about 2 km long and some 390 acres in area, lies roughly 10 km off the coast and was renamed on 4 September 2015. Lights there are dimmed and tests restricted during the turtle nesting season because of nearby Gahirmatha.

## History

The district administration notes two explanations for the name. The usual one links it to the goddess **Bhadrakali**. The 19th-century administrator John Beames suggested *Balabhadra Kshetra*, land sacred to Balabhadra, the elder brother of Jagannath and a god linked with farming.

According to the district administration, the 7th-century Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang passed through the area on his way to the Buddhist centres of Lalitgiri and Ratnagiri. Under the Eastern Ganga kings (1211–1436), Bhadrak was one of the administrative divisions known as *dandapats*. During the reign of Prataprudra Deva, **Sri Chaitanya** is said to have stopped at the villages of Kuansh and Santhia. The district administration adds that he stayed for two days and left behind an old piece of bedding, still worshipped at the Madan Mohan temple. Wikipedia notes that Bhadrak served as a provincial administrative centre in the Mughal period.

Bhadrak was active in the freedom movement. Bhadrak High School, founded in 1882, was among the area's early modern schools; Harekrushna Mahtab studied there. Mahatma Gandhi visited in 1921 and again in 1934, and the area took part in the Civil Disobedience Movement of 1930. On **28 September 1942**, during the Quit India Movement, police fired on a peaceful gathering at **Eram**. Twenty-eight people died on the spot and one more later, for a total of 29. Eram is remembered as Rakta Tirtha, and the district administration describes it as a "second Jallianwala Bagh".

Bhadrak remained a sub-division of Balasore district until it became a separate district in 1993.

## Places to visit

### Maa Bhadrakali Temple

The shrine of the district's presiding goddess stands on the bank of the **Salandi** at Aharapada, on the outskirts of Bhadrak. Sources differ on the distance from the town: the district administration gives about 5 km and Wikipedia about 8 km.

### Akhandalamani Temple, Aradi

This Shiva temple stands on the bank of the **Baitarani** at Aradi, about 37 km east of Bhadrak. According to the temple's legend, a ploughman struck a black stone that seemed to bleed. A local ruler, Raja Niladri Samara Singha Mohapatra, then built a wooden shrine about 350 years ago. The present temple was built later by the Kanika king Harihara Bhanja and Queen Satyabhama Patadei, using stone from Lalitgiri. Shivaratri (the *Jagara mela*) and the month of Shravana, when *bol bam* pilgrims carry water to the deity, draw large crowds.

### Eram (Rakta Tirtha)

Eram is about 16 km from Basudevpur. It is bounded by the sea on one side and by rivers on the others. Memorials at the site record the names of the 29 people killed in the police firing of 1942.

### Biranchi Narayan Temple, Palia

The temple of Biranchi Narayan, a form of the Sun god, is at Palia, about 15 km south of Bhadrak on the road to Chandbali, according to the district administration.

### Chandbali and Dhamra

**Chandbali**, a river port on the Baitarani, is a gateway for boat trips into the mangroves of [Bhitarkanika National Park](/travel/bhitarkanika-national-park). Odisha Tourism runs a Panthanivas there. **Dhamra**, near the river mouth, has the port and the shrine of **Maa Dhamarai**. Local folklore says she was one of five sisters.

## Culture and festivals

Bhadrakali and Akhandalamani are the focus of the district's religious life, with large gatherings at Shivaratri and during Shravana. The district administration notes several local customs. In Basantia village, **Panchuka Purnima** is marked by boat races on a village pond in memory of the Odia merchants who once sailed to the islands of Indonesia, similar to [Boita Bandana](/culture/boita-bandana). Melas in Bonth, Basantia and Basudevpur blocks feature *jatra* (opera) troupes. [Pana Sankranti](/culture/pana-sankranti) and [Dola Purnima](/culture/dola-purnima) are widely celebrated. The district site also mentions the old **Prasanna Khemeswar** Shiva temple at Brahmangan village, where Durga Puja, the Jagar festival and a Holi *melana* are held.

Odia is spoken by about 91 percent of people and Urdu by about 7 percent, with smaller Bengali- and Santali-speaking communities (2011). The local speech belongs to the northern Baleswari variety of Odia (see [Odia dialects](/language/odia-dialects)).

## Food

Odisha Tourism's guide to Bhadrak lists:

- **Palua ladu**, a sweet made with arrowroot (*palua*), sold in local markets.
- Coastal seafood such as **chingudi jhola** (prawn curry) and [machha besara](/food/machha-besara) (fish in mustard sauce).
- Everyday [pakhala bhata](/food/pakhala-bhata) and [dalma](/food/dalma), and chhena-based sweets.

## Economy

Agriculture is the mainstay, with paddy grown across the coastal plain. Fishing and marine services are important along the coast, with harbours at Dhamra, Kasia and Chudamani.

**Dhamra Port**, about 7 km from Dhamra town, is the main modern industry. The port was first conceived in 1998. Larsen and Toubro and Tata Steel developed it as a joint venture, and it received its first vessel on 8 February 2010 and its first commercial vessel on 10 April 2011. It has an approach channel about 18 km long and a draft of 17.5 m, deep enough for large bulk carriers. Adani Ports took control in 2014. Wikipedia reports annual cargo of about 43 million tonnes in 2023–24, mainly coal and iron ore. Environmental groups have raised concerns about the port's closeness to the olive ridley turtle nesting grounds of Gahirmatha.

## Notable people

- **Harekrushna Mahtab** (1899–1987), born at Agarpada in present-day Bhadrak district. He was Chief Minister of Odisha in 1946–1950 and 1956–1961 and Governor of Bombay, played a key role in merging the princely states into Odisha, and won a Sahitya Akademi Award for *Gaon Majlis*.
- **Pari Bewa**, one of the 29 killed at Eram in 1942, whom the district administration describes as the only woman martyr of Odisha.

## How to reach

- **By air:** Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, is about 132 km away, according to Odisha Tourism.
- **By rail:** Bhadrak railway station, at Charampa about 3 km north of the town, is on the Howrah–Chennai main line and has regular express services. A railway line to Dhamra serves the port.
- **By road:** Bhadrak is on **NH-16**, roughly 125–130 km from Bhubaneswar. NH-316A and several state highways connect it to Chandbali, Dhamra and Kendujhar.

October to March is the most comfortable time to visit. See also [Chandipur](/travel/chandipur) in neighbouring Balasore and [how to reach Odisha](/travel/how-to-reach-odisha).
