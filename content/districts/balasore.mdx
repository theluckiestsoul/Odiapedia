## Overview

**Balasore** (ବାଲେଶ୍ୱର), officially spelled **Baleshwar** since June 2026, is a coastal district in the far north of Odisha. It lies between the Bay of Bengal and the hills along the Mayurbhanj border, and touches West Bengal to the north-east. The district headquarters is Balasore town.

Balasore is known for **Chandipur**, where the sea retreats for kilometres at low tide, and for the DRDO **Integrated Test Range** nearby. Other attractions are the hill shrine of **Panchalingeswar**, the **Khirachora Gopinath** temple at Remuna and the quiet beach at **Talasari**. It is also the home district of [Fakir Mohan Senapati](/people/fakir-mohan-senapati), the father of modern Odia fiction. Fakir Mohan University and Fakir Mohan Medical College in Balasore town are named after him.

Balasore became a district in **October 1828** under the Bengal Presidency, and it joined the new province of Odisha in 1936. [Bhadrak](/district/bhadrak), formerly a sub-division, became a separate district in 1993. Today Balasore has two sub-divisions, **Balasore** and **Nilagiri**, and 12 blocks.

At the 2011 Census the district had **2,320,529** people, with a literacy rate of 79.79%. Sources differ on the area: the Census-based figure used here is 3,806 sq km, while the district administration gives 3,634 sq km.

## Geography and climate

Broadly, the district has three belts: a **coastal belt** of sandy beaches, creeks and mudflats; a fertile central **alluvial plain**; and, in the north-west around **Nilagiri**, a belt of **forested hills** that run into the Similipal massif. Wikipedia gives the coastline as about 137 km.

The **Subarnarekha** enters the district from the north and reaches the sea there. The **Budhabalanga** flows from Mayurbhanj to the coast near Chandipur and Balaramgadi.

The **Kuldiha Wildlife Sanctuary**, declared on 4 January 1984, covers about 273 sq km of hill forest. It is linked to Similipal through the Sukhupada and Nato hills and forms part of the Mayurbhanj Elephant Reserve.

The climate is hot and humid. The monsoon arrives in mid-June, and the district administration says about three-quarters of the rain falls between June and December. The coast is exposed to cyclones, so check India Meteorological Department warnings when travelling.

## History

The district administration describes the region as part of ancient **Kalinga** and later of the Toshali and Utkal regions. Its harbours made it an early meeting point with European traders. Wikipedia records an English East India Company factory at Balasore from **1633**, and Odisha Tourism points to Dutch tombs and an old British cemetery in the town as reminders of that period. Other European companies also traded here.

The Marathas held the area until the **Treaty of Deogaon in 1803** brought it under the Company. In October 1828 Balasore became one of three districts of the Odisha division under Bengal. Inland, the hill state of **Nilagiri**, ruled according to tradition by a Nagabanshi line, remained a separate princely state. Its last Raja signed the instrument of accession on 1 January 1948 after agitation by local freedom fighters, and the state was absorbed into Balasore district in 1949. It joined the new province of Odisha on 1 April 1936.

Balasore figures prominently in the freedom struggle. On **9 September 1915** the Bengali revolutionary **Jatindranath Mukherjee (Bagha Jatin)** and four companions fought police and troops at Chashakhand near the Budhabalanga. Bagha Jatin died of his wounds in Balasore hospital the next day.

On **13 April 1930** volunteers broke the salt law at **Inchudi**, about 19 km from Balasore. Leaders included Acharya Harihar Das, and women such as [Rama Devi](/people/rama-devi) took part. The event is remembered as the "Dandi of Odisha".

## Places to visit

### Chandipur beach

At Chandipur the sea recedes for several kilometres at low tide, so visitors can walk out on the seabed and watch the water return. It is not a swimming beach. See the [Chandipur travel guide](/travel/chandipur).

### Panchalingeswar

On a hillock near the Nilagiri hills, about 28 km south-west of Balasore town, five Shiva lingas sit in the bed of a perennial stream that flows over them. Worshippers lie flat on the rocks to reach and touch the lingas. According to tradition, Sita installed the lingas during the exile of the Ramayana; another account links them to the demon king Banasura. Odisha Tourism places Panchalingeswar about 32 km from Chandipur.

### Khirachora Gopinath, Remuna

This Vaishnava shrine at Remuna, which the district administration also calls "Remuna Gupta Vrindaban", is linked to the legend of Gopinath stealing *khira* (sweetened milk) for the saint Madhavendra Puri, which gave the deity the name Khirachora, the milk thief. Wikipedia dates the temple to about 1300 CE, and Janmashtami is its chief festival.

### Talasari and Udaypur beaches

These long, quiet beaches near the West Bengal border are known for red crabs and casuarina groves. Odisha Tourism puts Talasari about 85 km from Chandipur.

### Nilagiri

**Nilagiri**, the former capital of the Nilagiri princely state, is the gateway to the district's hill belt. Odisha Tourism lists its Jagannath temple among the sights near Chandipur, and Panchalingeswar and the Kuldiha forest lie within easy reach.

### Chandaneswar

The Shiva temple at Chandaneswar is known for its **Chadak Mela** festival, according to the district administration.

### Kuldiha Wildlife Sanctuary

This forest sanctuary, about 30 km from Chandipur, has elephants, leopards, gaur and giant squirrels, along with hornbills and many orchids.

### Balaramgadi and Balasore town

**Balaramgadi**, where the Budhabalanga meets the sea about 3 km from Chandipur, is a busy fishing harbour. In Balasore town, the Dutch tombs and a newly built Jagannath temple, about 7 km from the headquarters, are local landmarks.

## Culture and festivals

Balasore shares the main festivals of coastal Odisha: [Rath Yatra](/culture/rath-yatra), [Durga Puja](/culture/durga-puja), [Raja Parba](/culture/raja-parba) and [Makar Sankranti](/culture/makar-sankranti-en). Large fairs on Makar Sankranti are held at the Makara Muni temple. The **Chadak Mela** of Chandaneswar is a distinctive local fair.

Because the district borders Bengal, its culture shows both Odia and Bengali influences. Wikipedia's analysis of the 2011 Census reports that about 88% of residents speak Odia, followed by Santali, Urdu and Bengali. The Santal and other Adivasi communities of the hill belt keep their own languages, music and festivals.

## Food

Seafood dominates Balasore's table, especially fish, crabs and prawns landed at Balaramgadi and other harbours. A mustard-based fish curry such as [machha besara](/food/machha-besara) is typical. **Khira** (milk-rice pudding) has a special place because of its link to the Khirachora Gopinath temple at Remuna.

## Economy

The district administration names **industry, agriculture, fishing and tourism** as Balasore's four main sources of income. Paddy is the chief crop of the central plain. Marine fishing supports the coast, and the administration lists tyre, paper and pharmaceutical units among the larger employers.

The **Integrated Test Range** of the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO), set up in 1982, is a major centre for trials of rockets, missiles and airborne weapon systems. Its Launch Complex-III is at Chandipur, while a second launch complex is on Abdul Kalam Island off the coast of neighbouring Bhadrak district. Parts of the shore near Chandipur are restricted on test days, so visitors should follow local instructions. The district is also an education hub, with Fakir Mohan University and Fakir Mohan Medical College and Hospital.

## Notable people

- **[Fakir Mohan Senapati](/people/fakir-mohan-senapati)** (1843–1918): novelist and father of modern Odia fiction, author of *Chha Mana Atha Guntha*, born at Mallikashpur in Balasore.
- **[Radhanath Ray](/people/radhanath-ray)** (1848–1908): a leading Odia poet of the modern era, born at Kedarpur in present-day Balasore district.
- **Bagha Jatin** (Jatindranath Mukherjee): the Bengali revolutionary was not from the district, but his last stand near Balasore in September 1915 is commemorated locally.

## How to reach

- **By air:** The nearest major airport is Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar.
- **By rail:** Balasore station is on the main Howrah–Chennai line, with frequent trains from Kolkata and Bhubaneswar.
- **By road:** National Highways 16 and 18 pass through Balasore. The town is about 215 km from Bhubaneswar and 240 km from Kolkata. Chandipur is about 16 km from the town.

See [how to reach Odisha](/travel/how-to-reach-odisha). Balasore is a convenient stop on the way to [Similipal National Park](/travel/simlipal-national-park) in neighbouring [Mayurbhanj](/district/mayurbhanj).
