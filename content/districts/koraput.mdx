## Overview

**Koraput** (କୋରାପୁଟ) is a hill district in southern Odisha, set on the Eastern Ghats plateau along the borders with Andhra Pradesh and Chhattisgarh. Koraput is best known for Deomali, the highest peak in Odisha, for waterfalls and reservoirs such as Duduma and Kolab, and for being, in the words of its district administration, the heartland of Odisha's tribal communities.

The district headquarters is the hill town of **Koraput**, which sits at roughly 870 to 890 m above sea level. The largest town is **Jeypore**, the former seat of the Jeypore rulers. The district has two sub-divisions, Koraput and Jeypore, and 14 community development blocks.

Koraput district was created on **1 April 1936**, when the province of Orissa was formed and the area was transferred from the Madras Presidency. In **October 1992** it was divided into four districts: Koraput, [Rayagada](/district/rayagada), [Nabarangpur](/district/nabarangpur) and [Malkangiri](/district/malkangiri). The district administration gives the date as 1 October 1992, while several other official sources give 2 October 1992.

Koraput covers **8,807 sq km**, which Wikipedia lists as the third-largest district area in Odisha. The 2011 Census counted **1,379,647** people. About half the population (50.6%) belongs to Scheduled Tribes, and literacy stood at 49.21% in 2011.

## Geography and climate

Koraput lies in the south-west of Odisha. Within the state, it borders Rayagada, Nabarangpur and Malkangiri districts; to the south and east lies Andhra Pradesh, and to the west the Bastar region of Chhattisgarh. Much of the district is an upland of two broad plateaus, at average heights of roughly 3,000 feet and 2,000 feet, cut by valleys and forested ridges.

**Rivers.** The **Indravati** forms part of the boundary with Nabarangpur. The **Kolab** rises near the Andhra Pradesh border and is dammed to form the Kolab reservoir; further downstream the same river is known as the Sabari. The **Machkund**, known lower down as the Sileru, forms part of the Odisha–Andhra Pradesh boundary and plunges over **Duduma** waterfall.

**Hills.** **Deomali** is the highest point in Odisha at about **1,672 m** (5,486 ft), according to Odisha Tourism. The Panchpatmali range near Damanjodi holds large bauxite deposits.

**Climate.** Koraput is cooler than coastal Odisha because of its height. The monsoon brings most of the rain between June and September, and winter nights on the plateau can be cold.

## History

**Early kingdoms.** The district administration's history notes that the **Nala** kings ruled this region in the early centuries CE, with their capital at **Pushkari**. Historians differ on where Pushkari lay: older studies placed it at Podagada near Umerkote (now in Nabarangpur district), while more recent work identifies it with a site in the Bastar region.

**Nandapur and Jeypore.** In medieval times **Nandapur** became the capital of the Silavamsi kings. In the mid-15th century, according to the district administration, **Vinayak Deo** founded the line later known as the Jeypore Raj family and inherited the Silavamsi kingdom. In 1571 the kingdom became a feudatory of the Qutb Shahis of Golconda. In the mid-17th century the ruler Vir Vikram Deo moved the headquarters from Nandapur to **Jeypore**, which remained the seat of the family.

**British period.** Under British rule the Jeypore estate was part of the Madras Presidency. The British chose Koraput town as an administrative centre in **1870** because of its healthier climate. When Orissa became a separate province, Koraput district was formed on **1 April 1936**, with R. C. S. Bell as its first District Magistrate and Collector.

**Freedom movement.** The Quit India Movement of 1942 was strongly felt in the Koraput hills. **Laxman Nayak**, born at Tentuligumma in 1899, led a procession of tribal protesters to the Mathili police station (now in Malkangiri district) in August 1942. Police opened fire, and Laxman Nayak was later tried and sentenced to death; he was hanged at Berhampur Jail on **29 March 1943**. In the same month of August 1942, police fired on a procession at Papadahandi, now in Nabarangpur district, killing 19 people. The district administration also notes the role of Maharaja Vikram Deo of Jeypore in the movement for a united Odisha.

**After independence.** Koraput is a Fifth Schedule (Scheduled Area) district, and in 2006 it was listed among India's 250 most backward districts for the Backward Regions Grant Fund. It is part of the KBK (Kalahandi–Balangir–Koraput) region targeted by special development programmes.

## Places to visit

For a traveller's guide with seasons and practical tips, see our [Koraput travel guide](/travel/koraput) and the [Koraput tribal heritage itinerary](/travel/koraput-tribal-heritage-itinerary).

### Deomali

Deomali is Odisha's highest peak, at about 1,672 m. The summit area has open grassland and wide views over the Eastern Ghats, and is visited for trekking, sunrise and photography. Odisha Tourism lists it as open year-round; sources give different road distances from Koraput town, so check locally.

### Duduma waterfall

Duduma is formed by the Machkund river on the Odisha–Andhra Pradesh border, about 92 km from Koraput town according to Wikipedia. Sources differ on its height: figures of about 157 m and 175 m are both quoted. The Machkund hydroelectric project, a joint venture of Odisha and Andhra Pradesh, lies nearby.

### Gupteswar

Gupteswar is a limestone cave shrine to Shiva near the Kolab river, roughly 55 km from Jeypore. Visitors climb a flight of steps to the cave, which holds a natural linga. According to tradition, Rama found the linga while passing through the Dandakaranya forest, and the nearby hill is called Ramagiri. Maha Shivaratri draws very large crowds from Odisha, Andhra Pradesh and Chhattisgarh.

### Sabara Srikhetra

Sabara Srikhetra is the Jagannath temple of Koraput town. Incredible India describes it as the southern abode of Lord Jagannath, built in 1972, and notes that it holds an annual chariot festival with tribal participation, an echo of the [Rath Yatra](/culture/rath-yatra) at Puri.

### Kolab reservoir and Sunabeda

The **Kolab** reservoir, close to Koraput town, is a popular spot for sunset views and has a botanical garden nearby. **Sunabeda**, about 20 km from Koraput, is a planned township built around the aero-engine factory of Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL) and is also home to the Central University of Odisha.

### Nandapur and Subai

**Nandapur**, the old capital of the Silavamsi and early Jeypore rulers, preserves the **Batrisa Simhasana** ("throne of 32 steps") among other relics, according to the district administration. **Subai**, about 34 km from Koraput, has the remains of a Jain temple complex with rare images of Tirthankaras; the Government of Odisha describes it as a Jain monastery of the medieval period, though some researchers date it earlier.

### Jeypore

Jeypore, the district's largest town, was established as the seat of the rulers in the mid-17th century. It has the old fort and palace area, Vikram Dev University, and the region's airport.

## Culture and festivals

Koraput is home to many Adivasi communities. The largest are the **Paraja**, followed by the **Kondh**; the **Bhottada**, **Gadaba** and **Bhumia** are also prominent. In the 2011 Census, Odia was the first language of about 54% of the people, followed by **Desia** (the local variety of Odia used across communities, see [Odia dialects](/language/odia-dialects)), Kui, Paraja, Telugu, Kuvi and Gadaba.

**Festivals.** The annual **Parab** festival, highlighted by Incredible India, brings together tribal dance, music and crafts. The Rath Yatra at Sabara Srikhetra is the largest Jagannath festival of the region.

**Crafts and GI products.**

**Literature.** The Koraput hills are the setting of *Paraja* (1945) by [Gopinath Mohanty](/people/gopinath-mohanty), who spent much of his government service in undivided Koraput.

## Food

The everyday food of rural Koraput is built on rice and finger millet, called *mandia* (ragi) in Odia. **Mandia peja**, a gruel of ragi and rice, is a staple of Adivasi households across Koraput and the neighbouring districts. Incredible India's food guide to Koraput lists other millet dishes such as *mandia tampa* (ragi porridge cooked with broken rice), *mandia ruti* (flatbread) and *mandia sijha pitha* (steamed dumplings wrapped in banana leaf), along with dishes cooked in bamboo shoot or wrapped in sal leaves.

The aromatic **Kalajeera** rice is the district's best-known rice, and Koraput also grows coffee (see Economy).

## Economy

**Agriculture.** Most people depend on farming: paddy in the valleys, and millets, pulses and vegetables on the uplands. In 2012 the UN Food and Agriculture Organization recognised **Koraput traditional agriculture** as a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System, for the rice diversity conserved by tribal farmers.

**Coffee.** Coffee was first planted experimentally at Bichalkota around 1930 under the Jeypore rulers, and the Coffee Board expanded it from 1958. The Better India reported in 2026 that about 3,000 hectares of shade-grown Arabica are cultivated by roughly 3,200 growers, marketed under the **Koraput Coffee** brand of the Tribal Development Co-operative Corporation of Odisha.

**Industry and mining.** Major units include:
- **HAL Sunabeda**, which makes aero-engines, including the AL-31 engines for the Indian Air Force's Su-30MKI fleet.
- **NALCO** at Damanjodi, an alumina refinery fed by the bauxite of the Panchpatmali hills.
- The **Kolab** and **Machkund** hydroelectric projects.

The district also has deposits of limestone, china clay and other minerals.

## Notable people

- **Laxman Nayak** (1899–1943): tribal freedom fighter from Tentuligumma, hanged at Berhampur Jail on 29 March 1943 after the Mathili protest of 1942.
- **Maharaja Vikram Deo of Jeypore**: ruler of Jeypore who, according to the district administration, played an important role in the movement that created the province of Odisha.

| Mode | Details |

| Air | Jeypore Airport, about 3 km from Jeypore, has had IndiaOne Air flights to Bhubaneswar and Visakhapatnam since 31 October 2022. Visakhapatnam International Airport is roughly 170 km from Koraput, according to Odisha Tourism |
| Rail | Koraput Junction is on the Kottavalasa–Kirandul line; Jeypore, Kotpad Road, Damanjodi and Laxmipur Road are other stations. | Road | NH 26 runs through Pottangi, Koraput, Jeypore and Nabarangpur; NH 63 branches west at Borigumma towards Jagdalpur; NH 326 links Koraput with Rayagada via Laxmipur. Odisha Tourism gives the distance from Bhubaneswar as approximately 500 km |
