**Subarnapur** (ସୁବର୍ଣ୍ଣପୁର), better known by the name of its headquarters, **Sonepur**, is a district in western Odisha at the meeting point of the Mahanadi and Tel rivers. Subarnapur district is famous for its many temples, which have earned Sonepur the popular title "Second Varanasi", and for its Bomkai and Sonepuri handloom saris.

## Overview

The name Subarnapur means "city of gold". Wikipedia notes that in the 8th century the area was known as **Swarnapur** (Suvarnapura), and that it was also called **Paschima Lanka** (Western Lanka), a name echoed in the cult of its guardian goddess, Lankeswari.

The district was created on **1 April 1993**, when it was separated from Balangir district. Before that, the area had been the princely state of **Sonepur**, which merged with Odisha on 1 January 1948. The district headquarters is Sonepur town.

Subarnapur covers 2,337 sq km and had 610,183 people at the 2011 Census. It is overwhelmingly rural: only about 8 percent of the people lived in towns. Its blocks include Sonepur, Binika, Tarbha, Dunguripali, Birmaharajpur and Ullunda. Sambalpuri (Kosli) is the most common mother tongue, spoken by about 60 percent of people at the 2011 Census, followed by Odia. Subarnapur has two sub-divisions, Sonepur and Birmaharajpur.

## Geography and climate

Subarnapur lies in the middle Mahanadi valley. The **Mahanadi** flows through the district, and the **Tel**, one of its largest tributaries, joins it at Sonepur town. The confluence is the reason Sonepur grew up as a sacred site. River plains along the Mahanadi and Tel make up much of the farmland.

## History

Finds of punch-marked coins point to settlement in the Sonepur area between about the 3rd century BCE and the 1st century CE. According to the Wikipedia history of Sonepur, the Somavamsi king **Janmejaya I** (about 850–885 CE) took the region from the Bhanjas and founded the Suvarnapura kingdom. In the 10th and 11th centuries Suvarnapura was one of the two capitals of the **Somavamsi** dynasty.

Later rulers included the Telugu Chodas, from about 1070 CE, and the Kalachuris, from about 1113 CE. The Eastern Ganga king Anangabhima Deva III conquered the area in the early 13th century.

The Chauhan **Sonepur State** was founded in the 17th century, when a prince of the Patna (Balangir) Chauhan line took the area from the Bhanja rulers of Boudh and gave it to **Madan Gopal**. Sources differ on the date, giving either 1640 or 1650. The state signed an agreement with the East India Company in 1803 and came under British protection after 1817. Sonepur covered about 2,347 sq km in 1901, close to the present district's area. It was a nine-gun salute state.

The ruler of Sonepur acceded to India on **1 January 1948**, and the state became part of Balangir district. It was separated again as Subarnapur district in 1993.

## Places to visit

### Lankeswari Temple

The shrine of the goddess **Lankeswari**, also called Nikumbhila, stands on a rock in the bed of the Mahanadi at Sonepur. Lankeswari was the principal deity of the old Paschima Lanka, and she is worshipped by the Kaibarta community of Sonepur and by people across the district. The shrine can be seen from the road between Ulunda and Birmaharajpur.

### Subarnameru Temple

A Shiva temple on the bank of the Tel in Sonepur town. Its main festivals are Shivaratri and Kartik Purnima.

### The Mahanadi–Tel confluence

The meeting of the two rivers at Sonepur is the town's sacred centre. Many of its temples cluster along the riverbanks, which gives the town its "Second Varanasi" reputation.

### Patali Srikhetra, Kotsamlai

Patali Srikhetra lies at the foot of Trikut hill at Kotsamlai, in the Birmaharajpur sub-division. According to tradition recorded in the Madala Panji, the temple chronicle of Puri, the images of Jagannath, Balabhadra and Subhadra were hidden in the caves of Trikut to protect them from invaders, and were later recovered by the Somavamsi king Yayati Keshari. This gives the site its name, Patali Srikhetra ("underground Srikhetra"). The site is also important to Shakta and Buddhist traditions.

### Khaliapali

Khaliapali is the site of the ashram of the poet-saint [Bhima Bhoi](/people/bhima-bhoi), set up around 1877 according to the Odia Virtual Academy. It is a place of pilgrimage for followers of Mahima Dharma. Bhima Bhoi, who was raised in a Kondh (Kandha) Adivasi family, composed devotional works such as *Stuti Chintamani* and many bhajans that are still sung in western Odisha. Popular tradition remembers him as blind, though the Odia Virtual Academy says he was not born blind.

## Culture and festivals

Subarnapur is one of Odisha's leading handloom districts.

- **Bomkai saree**: the [Bomkai saree](/culture/bomkai-saree), registered as a Geographical Indication ("Bomkai Saree and Fabrics"), is today woven mainly by the **Bhulia** weavers of Subarnapur, which is why it is often called Sonepuri Bomkai. Its thick extra-weft borders and pallus often combine with ikat. According to accounts in Odisha's handloom histories, the Bomkai style reached Sonepur from Ganjam during the rule of a king of Patna state. Weavers add extra threads to raise motifs such as fish, temple spires and lotuses, and borders in a contrasting colour are woven with a three-shuttle method known locally as *muhajorha*.
- **Sambalpuri bandha**: Subarnapur is also a major centre for the GI-registered [Sambalpuri saree](/culture/sambalpuri-saree). See [Odisha's GI tags](/culture/odisha-gi-tags).
- **Festivals**: [Nuakhai](/culture/nuakhai) is the main family festival. Shivaratri and Kartik Purnima are celebrated at the riverside temples.
- **Folk performance**: [Sambalpuri dance](/culture/sambalpuri-dance) forms such as Dalkhai are part of festival life.
- **Language**: Subarnapur is a heartland of Sambalpuri (Kosli), which is written in the Odia script. About one in seven of the language's 2.63 million native speakers counted in 2011 live in the district. Speakers campaign for its inclusion in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution.
- **Mahima Dharma**: through Bhima Bhoi's ashram at Khaliapali, the district has a lasting link with the Mahima Dharma movement and its devotional songs.

## Food

Subarnapur's food is that of western Odisha. Rice is the staple, and fresh fish from the Mahanadi and Tel is common in local cooking. **Chaula bara**, crisp rice-batter fritters served with tamarind soup, is a popular snack in Sonepur and across the region. Bamboo-shoot dishes (**kardi** and **hendua**) and festival sweets such as **rasbaraa** and **tikhri** are also part of the local table.

## Economy

Agriculture is the main occupation. Paddy is the main crop of the river valleys. Handloom weaving is the district's best-known industry: households of Bhulia and other weaving communities produce Bomkai and bandha saris, which are also sold through Odisha's state handloom cooperatives. Wikipedia notes that Sonepur is also known for its river prawns.

## Notable people

- **[Bhima Bhoi](/people/bhima-bhoi)** (c. 1850–1895): poet-saint of Mahima Dharma who set up his ashram at Khaliapali in the former Sonepur state. Sources differ on his birthplace.
- **Chaturbhuj Meher** (born in Sonepur): master ikat weaver, awarded the Padma Shri in 2005, who trained thousands of weavers.
- **Kailash Chandra Meher** (born in Sonepur): painter known for tussar pattachitra and contemporary art, awarded the Padma Shri in 2013.

## How to reach

- **Air**: the nearest airport is Veer Surendra Sai Airport, Jharsuguda, about 142 km from Sonepur according to Wikipedia. Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, also serves the district by road.
- **Rail**: Sonepur is on the Khurda Road–Balangir railway project, which is being opened in stages. The established railheads nearby are Balangir and Sambalpur. Check current train services before travel.
- **Road**: National Highways 57 and 126A and State Highways 15 and 41 connect Sonepur with Sambalpur, Balangir, Boudh and Bhubaneswar.
