**Kendujhar** (କେନ୍ଦୁଝର), also spelled **Keonjhar**, is a hilly, forested district in northern Odisha whose headquarters is Kendujhar town. Kendujhar district is one of India's main iron ore regions and is also known for the Maa Tarini shrine at Ghatagaon, the source of the Baitarani river at Gonasika and the ancient paintings at Sitabinji.

## Overview

Kendujhar lies on the northern plateau of Odisha, bordering Jharkhand. It covers 8,303 sq km and had a population of 1,801,733 in the 2011 Census. Scheduled Tribes formed about 45.5 percent of the population. The largest groups are the Ho, Bhuyan, Munda and Santal, and the district is the main home of the **Juang**, a Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Group.

The district was formed from the former princely state of Keonjhar, which merged with India on **1 January 1948**. It has three sub-divisions: **Kendujhar**, **Champua** and **Anandapur**, with 13 blocks.

Kendujhar's economy is dominated by mining. The Joda–Barbil belt in the north is among the most important iron ore and manganese areas in India. The rest of the district is largely rural, with forests, hills and many waterfalls.

## Geography and climate

According to the district administration, Kendujhar is bounded by Mayurbhanj and Bhadrak to the east, Jajpur to the south, Dhenkanal and Sundargarh to the west, and West Singhbhum district of Jharkhand to the north.

The district is hilly, with several peaks above 900 m:

| Hill | Height (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Mankadnacha | 3,639 ft |
| Gandhamardan | 3,477 ft |
| Gonasika | 3,219 ft |
| Thakurani | 3,003 ft |

The **Baitarani** river rises in the Gonasika hills. Its source is a rock shaped like a cow's nose, which gives the place its name. The river first flows north, then turns east and briefly forms the boundary with Jharkhand. Tropical deciduous forest covers much of the district.

Kendujhar has hot summers and a strong monsoon. Wikipedia gives an average annual rainfall of about 1,910 mm for the district, with summer highs of over 43°C and cold winter nights. Kendujhar town has recorded extremes of about 47.4°C in May and 2.2°C in winter, and receives about 1,419 mm of rain a year, most of it between June and September.

## History

According to Wikipedia's summary of the district's history, the **Nagas of Vindhyatabi** ruled the area from about the 2nd to the 4th century CE. An inscription at Sitabinji names a king, Maharaja Sri Disabhanja, and is dated by scholars to around the 4th–5th century CE.

The district administration records that Keonjhar became a separate state in the first half of the 12th century, when **Jyoti Bhanja**, of the Bhanja family of Khijjinga (Mayurbhanj), became its ruler; Wikipedia adds that he was installed with the support of local Bhuyan clans. In the late 15th century **Govinda Bhanja** greatly enlarged the state. During the reign of Pratap Balabhadra Bhanja (1764–1792), the small tracts of Tillo and Jujhpada were added. They were recognised as part of Keonjhar in the sanad that the East India Company granted to Raja Janardan Bhanja in **1804**.

In **1891** the Bhuyan people and other communities rose against Raja Dhanurjaya Bhanja over forced labour and other burdens. The revolt, remembered as the **Bhuyan Meli** or Dharani Meli, was led by **Dharanidhar Naik**. For a time the king fled to Cuttack, but British forces eventually suppressed the uprising, and Dharanidhar was imprisoned in Cuttack until 1897. After his release he withdrew from public life and lived at a hermitage at Ali until his death in 1914.

In 1931 the state covered about 8,019 sq km and had 460,609 people, according to Wikipedia. The last ruler, Balabhadra Narayan Bhanj Deo, reigned from 1926. Keonjhar merged with India on **1 January 1948** and became a district of Odisha.

## Places to visit

### Maa Tarini Temple, Ghatagaon

The shrine of **Maa Tarini** at Ghatagaon is one of the most visited pilgrimage centres in northern Odisha. It is about 45 km from Keonjhar town and 180 km from Bhubaneswar. Devotees offer coconuts to the goddess, and the district administration notes that tribal priests continue to serve here. Maha Bisuba Sankranti is celebrated with special festivities.

### Gonasika

Gonasika, in the hills where the Baitarani rises, is a sacred place with the **Brahmeswar Mahadev** temple. The district administration says the Baruni Jatra here in Chaitra draws about 30,000 pilgrims.

### Sanaghagara and Badaghagara waterfalls

These two waterfalls lie close to Keonjhar town. The district administration places **Sanaghagara** about 8 km from the town on the national highway. Odisha Tourism describes **Badaghagara** as the larger fall, dropping roughly 100 ft through forest.

### Khandadhar Waterfall

This Khandadhar, not to be confused with the waterfall of the same name in Sundargarh district, is described by the district administration as one of the most beautiful waterfalls in the state. The administration puts it 50–60 km from Keonjhar town.

### Sitabinji

The Sitabinji group of rock shelters, about 30 km south of Keonjhar town near the Sita river, is protected by the Archaeological Survey of India. On a boulder called **Ravana Chhaya** is a painting of a royal procession with a king on an elephant. Painted in tempera and usually dated to the 4th–5th century CE, it is regarded as one of the earliest Indian paintings on a non-religious subject.

### Nomira pillow lava

Near Nomira village, about 18 km south of Joda, rare **pillow lava** formed when molten lava erupted and cooled under water. OrissaPOST reports that the Geological Survey of India declared it a national geological monument in 1976 and dates the rocks to about 2.8 billion years.

### Other sites

- **Kushaleswar temple, Deogaon**: a Shiva shrine that the district administration dates to about 900 CE.
- **Murga Mahadev temple**: at the foot of the Thakurani hills, beside a perennial spring.
- **Kanjipani Ghati**: a scenic mountain road with wide views; the district administration says the ghat trail runs for about 20 km.
- **Keshari Kunda**: two natural holes in the bed of the Baitarani where pilgrims take a holy dip.
- **Bhimkund**: a pool and waterfall on the Baitarani, set among rocks and forest.
- **Hadagarh reservoir**: a dam on the Salandi river in the Anandapur sub-division.
- **Handibhanga** and **Gundichaghagi**: forest waterfalls; Gundichaghagi is a series of cascades on the Musala river.

## Culture and festivals

The district administration describes a wide festival calendar. It includes [Nuakhai](/culture/nuakhai), [Raja](/culture/raja-parba) and [Makar Sankranti](/culture/makar-sankranti-en), Shivaratri at the Shiva temples, Bodam in the month of Pousha, and tribal festivals such as **Sohrai** and **Karma**. During **Chaitra Parba**, Chhau is performed.

Dance is part of community life. The district site mentions **Changu** dance with drums, the **Juang dance**, Ho dances during the Magh festival and [Chhau](/culture/chhau-dance).

The **Juang** live mainly in Banspal block. The state's tribal research institute (SCSTRTI) notes that the name means "man" in their own Munda-family language. They traditionally centre village life on the **mandaghar**, a youth dormitory. A Juang Development Agency was set up at Gonasika in 1978–79.

Odia is the main language, spoken by about 79 percent of people in 2011, followed by Ho and Santali.

## Economy

Mining is the backbone of the district's economy. Iron ore, manganese and chromite are worked around **Joda**, **Barbil**, **Banspani** and **Barjamda** by the Odisha Mining Corporation, Tata Steel and other companies. Wikipedia's summary states that Kendujhar supplies about 30 percent of Odisha's iron ore and most of its manganese.

Outside the mining belt, most people depend on farming and forest produce. In 2006 the Ministry of Panchayati Raj listed Kendujhar among India's backward districts receiving the Backward Regions Grant Fund.

## Notable people

- **Dharanidhar Naik** (1864–1914), born at Kusumita, leader of the 1891 Bhuyan uprising. Keonjhar's college, now **Dharanidhar University** (university status 2023), was renamed after him in 1983–84.
- **Mohan Charan Majhi**, born in 1972 at Raikala village, MLA for Keonjhar, and Chief Minister of Odisha from 12 June 2024.

## How to reach

- **By air:** The district administration gives the distance to Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, as about 230 km.
- **By rail:** **Kendujhargarh** station, on the Padapahar–Jakhapura line, has had passenger services since 2009, including MEMU trains towards Bhubaneswar. Jajpur Keonjhar Road on the Howrah–Chennai main line is another option, and Harichandanpur is the nearest station for Ghatagaon.
- **By road:** National highways NH-49, NH-20, NH-215, NH-520 and NH-220 run through the district, connecting it with Bhubaneswar, Cuttack, Jamshedpur and Kolkata.
