## Overview

**Bargarh** (ବରଗଡ଼) is a district in western Odisha, bordering Chhattisgarh. Its headquarters is the town of Bargarh, on the Jira river. Odisha Tourism now uses the Odia-based spelling **Baragada**, following the state's 2026 revision of English place names.

The district is known for its farms, its looms and its theatre. Water from the **Hirakud dam** irrigates the northern plains, which grow large paddy harvests and earn the district the popular title of Odisha's "rice bowl". Bargarh is a major centre of **Sambalpuri handloom**, home to the large **Sambalpuri Bastralaya** cooperative. Every winter the whole town becomes the stage for **[Dhanu Jatra](/culture/dhanu-jatra)**, which is often described as the world's largest open-air theatre.

Bargarh was carved out of [Sambalpur](/district/sambalpur) district on **1 April 1993**. It has two sub-divisions, **Bargarh** and **Padampur**, with 12 tahasils and 12 blocks.

At the 2011 Census the district had **1,481,255** people on **5,837 sq km**, a density of about 254 per sq km. The literacy rate was 74.62%, and the district had a high sex ratio of 977 females per 1,000 males.

## Geography and climate

Bargarh is mainly an open plain broken by small hill ranges. It borders the Chhattisgarh districts of Mahasamund and Raigarh to the north-west, [Jharsuguda](/district/jharsuguda) to the north, [Sambalpur](/district/sambalpur) to the east, [Subarnapur](/district/subarnapur) and [Balangir](/district/balangir) to the south, and [Nuapada](/district/nuapada) to the west.

The north of the district is watered by canals from the Hirakud reservoir on the **Mahanadi**. The **Jira** river flows past Bargarh town. In the south-west rise the **Gandhamardan hills**, shared with [Balangir](/district/balangir) district, whose highest point reaches about 1,060 m. The Nrusinghanath shrine stands on their northern slope. In Hindu tradition these are the hills from which Hanuman carried healing herbs for Lakshmana in the Ramayana. The Botanical Survey of India has documented about 220 plant species of medicinal value here, and local communities have long campaigned to protect the hills from bauxite mining.

The **Debrigarh Wildlife Sanctuary**, about 347 sq km of dry deciduous forest, lies on the shore of the Hirakud reservoir. The reservoir bounds it on the east and north.

Summers are very hot, as in much of western Odisha. Most rain falls in the south-west monsoon, and winters are cool and dry.

## History

Bargarh lay in the ancient region of **Dakshina Kosala**. Wikipedia's district history traces a series of ruling powers here. The Sarabhapuriyas rose to prominence in the 6th century CE, followed by the Somavamshis. A Kalachuri copper plate of about 1131 CE mentions **Vagharakotta**, possibly an early name of Bargarh. The Eastern Gangas drove out the Kalachuris around 1211.

From the late 16th century the area was ruled by the **Chauhan** kings of Sambalpur. According to Wikipedia, the Chauhan king **Balaram Dev** made Bargarh a fortified headquarters near the Jira river. Wikipedia also records an older name, **Bahgar Kota**, which Balaram Dev changed to Bargarh.

The Marathas took the Sambalpur kingdom in 1800. After the Third Anglo-Maratha War, Sambalpur became British territory in 1817, and it was annexed under the Doctrine of Lapse in 1849. During the rebellion of 1857 and its long aftermath, **[Veer Surendra Sai](/people/veer-surendra-sai)** and his allies fought the British across this region. The area took part in the Odia language movement of the 1890s; see [history of Sambalpur](/history/sambalpur-history).

After independence the area remained part of Sambalpur district until Bargarh district was formed in 1993.

## Places to visit

### Nrusinghanath temple

At the foot of the Gandhamardan hills near **Paikmal**, about 35 km west of Padampur and 110 km from Bargarh town, stands a temple to Narasimha built by King **Baijal Singh Dev** of Patna State in the early 15th century. The temple is about 45 feet tall. A big fair is held on Nrusimha Chaturdashi in Vaishakha (around May). Odisha Tourism describes a hilltop trekking route of about 16 km over the Gandhamardan hills from Nrusinghanath to the Harishankar temple on the southern slope, in [Balangir](/district/balangir) district.

### Debrigarh Wildlife Sanctuary

Debrigarh's hills and forests run down to the Hirakud reservoir, which draws migratory waterfowl in winter. Odisha Tourism lists leopards, elephants, gaur, deer and wild boar. It recommends visiting from October to March and offers jeep safaris, trails and boat rides. The sanctuary is about 25 km from Hirakud Dam and about 40 km from the Samaleswari temple in Sambalpur, according to Odisha Tourism; see the [Hirakud Dam travel guide](/travel/hirakud-dam).

### Bargarh town during Dhanu Jatra

During [Dhanu Jatra](/culture/dhanu-jatra), the streets of Bargarh become Mathura and Ambapali village across the Jira becomes Gopapura. The actor playing **Kansa** holds court and issues mock orders. Kalinga TV reported about 22 stages within roughly 7 km of the town for the 2025–26 edition.

### Barpali

**Barpali**, a tahasil town in the south of the district, is the birthplace of the poet [Gangadhar Meher](/people/gangadhar-meher). It is also known for its own style of Sambalpuri saree.

## Culture and festivals

Bargarh belongs to the Sambalpuri-speaking region of western Odisha. Wikipedia's analysis of the 2011 Census reports that about 76% of residents name Sambalpuri as their mother tongue, followed by Odia.

The biggest festival is **[Dhanu Jatra](/culture/dhanu-jatra)**. It runs for about 11 days in the month of Pausha and ends on Pausha Purnima. Sources date its beginning to 1947 or early 1948. **[Nuakhai](/culture/nuakhai)**, the new-rice festival held in August or September, is the most important family festival of the region. **[Sitala Sasthi](/culture/sitala-sasthi)** celebrations have spread from Sambalpur to Bargarh, Padampur and Barpali.

Folk forms such as **Dalkhai** and other [Sambalpuri dances](/culture/sambalpuri-dance) are performed at these festivals to the dhol, nisan and mahuri.

Bargarh is one of the main weaving districts of the **[Sambalpuri saree](/culture/sambalpuri-saree)**, whose bandha (ikat) yarn is tie-dyed before weaving. "Sambalpuri Bandha Saree and Fabrics" is a registered Geographical Indication. The **Sambalpuri Bastralaya** at Bargarh, founded in **1954**, describes itself as one of the country's largest primary weavers' cooperatives. It reports more than 5,600 weavers, 90 affiliated primary societies, and about a quarter of Odisha's cooperative handloom production.

## Food

Bargarh shares the cuisine of western Odisha. Regional food writing lists **chaula bara**, a crisp fried rice-batter snack sold in Bargarh, Barpali and nearby towns. It also describes **ambil** (a sour vegetable soup), and bamboo-shoot dishes such as **kardi** (fresh shoots) and **hendua** (dried or fermented shoots; descriptions vary). Pakhala (fermented rice) is a hot-weather staple across Odisha; see [pakhala bhata](/food/pakhala-bhata).

## Economy

Agriculture is the backbone of the economy. The Hirakud Dam, which Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru inaugurated on 13 January 1957 in neighbouring Sambalpur district, transformed farming here by feeding a network of irrigation canals. Wikipedia notes that all-season irrigation from Hirakud makes the northern half of the district rich in agriculture, mostly paddy, and Bargarh town has many rice mills. Its article on the town puts the district's annual rice output at about 600,000 tonnes. Cement and sugar mills also operate in the district. Handloom weaving, organised largely through cooperatives such as the Sambalpuri Bastralaya, is the other major source of income.

## Notable people

- **[Gangadhar Meher](/people/gangadhar-meher)** (1862–1924): *Swabhaba Kabi*, the poet of nature, author of *Tapaswini*, born at Barpali into a weaving family.
- **Parbati Giri** (1926–1995): freedom fighter jailed during the Quit India Movement and later a social worker, called the "Mother Teresa of Western Odisha", born at Samlaipadar in present-day Bargarh district.
- **Kunja Bihari Meher** (1928–2008): master ikat weaver from Bargarh district, awarded the Padma Shri in 1998 for his contribution to Sambalpuri handloom.

## How to reach

- **By air:** The nearest airport is Veer Surendra Sai Airport at Jharsuguda. Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, has the widest choice of flights.
- **By rail:** Bargarh Road station, about 4 km from the town, is on the Jharsuguda–Titlagarh line of the East Coast Railway, with trains to Bhubaneswar, Raipur, Visakhapatnam and Kolkata. Sambalpur is the nearest major junction.
- **By road:** Bargarh lies on National Highway 53 (the Kolkata–Mumbai corridor), and National Highway 26 begins here and runs south.

From the coast, most travellers come via [Sambalpur](/district/sambalpur). See [how to reach Odisha](/travel/how-to-reach-odisha).
