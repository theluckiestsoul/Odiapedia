## Overview

**Boudh** (ବୌଦ୍ଧ), also spelled Baudh, is a small district in central Odisha on the south bank of the **Mahanadi**. Its headquarters is Boudh town, the old capital of the princely state of Baudh, which stands on the river.

The district's name is generally linked to Buddhism. Boudh town still has a large Buddha image of about the 8th century in the earth-touching posture. It also preserves a remarkable group of early medieval Shiva temples built on a star-shaped plan. Boudh has been an important seat of Buddhism, Shaivism and the Shakti cult, and it remains a quiet, largely rural district of farms and forests.

Boudh became a separate district on **2 January 1994**, when it was carved out of the former Boudh-Kandhamal district. It has two assembly constituencies, **Boudh** and **Kantamal**, and forms part of the [Kandhamal](/district/kandhamal) Lok Sabha seat.

At the 2011 Census the district had **441,162** people, among the smallest populations in Odisha, on **3,098 sq km**. That gives a density of about 142 per sq km. The sex ratio was a high 991 females per 1,000 males, and the literacy rate was 71.61%. The district administration gives a larger area of about 3,445 sq km.

## Geography and climate

Boudh lies in the middle course of the Mahanadi. Boudh falls in Odisha's Southern revenue division, headquartered at Berhampur. According to the district administration, it is bounded by the Mahanadi and [Angul](/district/angul) district to the north, [Kandhamal](/district/kandhamal) to the south, [Nayagarh](/district/nayagarh) to the east, and the **Tel river** and [Subarnapur](/district/subarnapur) district to the west.

In the north-east, the forests around **Padmatola** form part of the wider [Satkosia](/travel/satkosia) landscape along the Mahanadi gorge.

## History

Archaeological evidence suggests that the Boudh area was an important Buddhist centre. A Government of Odisha *Orissa Review* survey by historian Byomakesh Tripathy describes a colossal Buddha image of about the 8th century near the palace in Boudh town. It also records bronze images of Maitreya, Lokesvara, Tara and Heruka, as well as votive stupas and broken Buddha images at nearby Syamsundarpur and Pargalpur.

By the mid-8th century the region formed part of **Khinjali Mandala** under the early **Bhanja** rulers. The **Somavamsi** kings later displaced them. Wikipedia identifies **Jagati** in Boudh with Yayatinagara, a Somavamsi capital of Yayati I. The Rameswar temples and the twin Chari Sambhu temples belong to this rich early medieval period. The Eastern Gangas followed.

Around the early 14th century, a ruling Brahmin chief adopted **Ananga Bhanja**, a prince of the Keonjhar Bhanja house, founding the line that ruled **Baudh State** for more than 600 years. In the 17th century the Chauhan king Balabhadra Deva of Sambalpur took the Sonepur region from Boudh, and Sonepur became a separate state in 1640.

After the British conquest of Odisha in 1803, **Raja Biswambar Dev** of Boudh accepted British sovereignty by a treaty of **3 March 1804**. Baudh remained a tributary state until its last ruler signed the accession that merged it with Orissa on **1 January 1948**. The former state later became part of Boudh-Kandhamal district, and Boudh became a district of its own in 1994.

## Places to visit

### Buddha image, Boudh town

Near the old palace in Boudh town stands a large seated Buddha in the earth-touching (*bhumisparsha*) posture. *Orissa Review* dates it to about the 8th century and assigns it to the Vajrayana tradition.

### Rameswar group of temples

Inside the modern Rameswar temple complex in Boudh town stand three early Shiva temples, which the district administration calls the Rameswara or Ramanatha temples. Sources differ on their names and date. An *Odisha Review* study by Ramesh Meher names them **Bhubanesvara**, **Kapilesvara** and **Swapnesvara** (also called Sidhesvara) and dates them to the 10th century under the Somavamsis. The district administration names them Paschima Somanatha, Bhubaneswar and Dhabaleswara and dates them to the 8th century. Each has an unusual star-shaped (stellate) plan formed by two intersecting squares, which Ramesh Meher measures at about 11 feet each. Loose sculptures in the compound include Buddhist and Jain images that probably came from other buildings, and *Orissa Review* also records bronze Buddhist images kept in the temple premises. The Archaeological Survey of India protects the Paschima Somanatha, Bhubanesvara and Kapilesvara temples in Boudh town as Monuments of National Importance.

### Chari Sambhu temples, Gandharadi

At **Gandharadi**, on the Mahanadi about 16 km from Boudh town, stand twin temples of **Nilamadhava** (Vishnu) and **Sidheswara** (Shiva). They date from about the 8th or 9th century, and sources differ on the exact period. These Monuments of National Importance are maintained by the Archaeological Survey of India.

### Bhairabi temple, Purunakatak

The goddess **Bhairabi** is worshipped at **Purunakatak**, about 30 km from Boudh on the road to Daspalla. According to tradition the site is one of the Shakti pithas. The main festival is a 16-day Durga Puja (Sodasa Puja) involving surrounding villages. *Orissa Review* records that the present shrine was built in 1969 with donations from devotees, replacing a modest older structure.

### Padmatola and the Satkosia gorge

The Padmatola forests, about 80 km from Boudh via Charichhak, belong to the landscape of the 22 km Satkosia gorge of the Mahanadi. The district administration lists tiger, leopard, elephant, spotted deer, sloth bear, gharial and mugger crocodile among the wildlife of the area. It describes the gorge as a meeting point of Chhotanagpur plateau forests, dry deciduous forests and moist peninsular sal forests, and records 38 mammal, 128 bird and 27 reptile species. A gharial research and breeding facility was set up at Tikarpada, on the Angul side of the gorge, in 1995. See the [Satkosia travel guide](/travel/satkosia).

### Other sites

The district administration also lists these sites:

- **Marjakud**, an island in the Mahanadi opposite Boudh town.
- The **Rajbati**, or **Jogindra Villa** palace, of the former rulers in Boudh town.
- **Dambarugada** mountain, about 20 km from Boudh on the road to Balangir.
- **Nayakpada cave**, about 12 km from Boudh. Local tradition links it to an ascetics' ashram and to a story that Jagannath's images were once hidden underground (*patali*) nearby to protect them from an invader, which is why the place is also called Patali Srikhetra.

## Culture and festivals

Both Odia and Sambalpuri are spoken in Boudh. Wikipedia's analysis of the 2011 Census reports that about 79% of residents speak Odia and about 21% Sambalpuri. Boudh was also recorded as the district with the highest share of Hindus in India, about 99%.

Festivals include [Nuakhai](/culture/nuakhai), Rath Yatra, Laxmi Puja, Shivaratri, Dussehra, Dola Jatra and the local **Chuda Khai Jatra**. Traditional dances include **Karma**, **[Danda Nata](/culture/danda-nacha)** and **Dalkhai**, part of the wider family of [Sambalpuri dances](/culture/sambalpuri-dance).

Boudh is also one of the weaving districts of the bandha (ikat) tradition behind the [Sambalpuri saree](/culture/sambalpuri-saree).

## Economy

Boudh is overwhelmingly rural: only about 4.6% of its people lived in towns at the 2011 Census, according to Wikipedia. Farming on the plains of the Mahanadi and Tel is the main livelihood, and handloom weaving in the bandha tradition adds to village incomes.

## Notable people

Few nationally known figures from Boudh appear in published sources. The people most closely tied to the district's documented history are its rulers:

- **Ananga Bhanja** (early 14th century): founder of the long-ruling Bhanja line of Baudh State, adopted from the Keonjhar royal house.
- **Raja Biswambar Dev**: ruler who signed the 1804 treaty accepting British sovereignty.
- **Raja Narayan Dev**: the last ruler of Baudh State before its merger with Orissa in 1948.

## How to reach

- **By air:** Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, is the usual gateway. Veer Surendra Sai Airport at Jharsuguda is an alternative from the west.
- **By rail:** Rairakhol, about 30 km from Boudh, is the nearest railway station according to the district administration.
- **By road:** Boudh is about 220 to 230 km from Bhubaneswar via Nayagarh (the district administration's pages give both figures), or about 240 km via Angul. The Khurda–Balangir highway passes through the district via Charichhak and Purunakatak.
