**Jajpur** (ଯାଜପୁର) is a district in coastal Odisha whose headquarters, Jajpur town, is an ancient pilgrimage centre on the **Baitarani** river known as **Biraja Kshetra**. Jajpur district combines old temples and Buddhist monasteries with one of Odisha's largest industrial zones at **Kalinganagar** and the chromite mines of **Sukinda**.

## Overview

Jajpur district was formed on **1 April 1993**, when the old Cuttack district was divided. It covers 2,899 sq km in the 2011 Census, while the district administration gives 2,887.69 sq km. Its population in 2011 was 1,827,192. The district is densely settled and largely rural, with only about 7 percent of people in towns.

Jajpur town itself is small: its municipality had 37,458 residents in 2011. Over the centuries it has been known as Viraja, Viranja, Varanja-nagara and Yajanagara before the present name, according to Wikipedia.

Unusually, Jajpur has a single sub-division, **Jajpur**, divided into 10 tahasils and 10 community development blocks. Its municipalities are Jajpur and Vyasanagar (Byasanagar), the town that grew up around Jajpur Road station.

The district is known for the **Biraja temple**, revered as a Shakti Peetha. It is also home to the Buddhist sites of **Ratnagiri** and **Udayagiri**, the steel plants of Kalinganagar and India's main chromite belt in the Sukinda valley.

## Geography and climate

According to Wikipedia, Jajpur is bounded by Kendujhar and Bhadrak to the north and north-east, Kendrapara and Cuttack to the south, and Dhenkanal to the west. The **Baitarani** runs along the eastern side of the district past Jajpur town, and the **Brahmani** crosses its south. The west rises into hills: the Sukinda valley lies between the Mahagiri and Daitari ranges, rich in chromite and iron ore.

## History

The district administration records that the area was known in ancient times as **Viraja** and as **Baitarani Tirtha**. In tradition it is one of the Shakti Peethas, where the navel of Sati fell, which is why it is also called **Nabhi Gaya**. The Mahabharata tells of the Pandavas bathing at Viraja with the sage Lomasha.

From the 7th century the **Bhaumakara** kings ruled much of Odisha from Guheswar Pataka, a capital near Viraja. Buddhism flourished in this period, and monasteries such as Ratnagiri and Udayagiri were active for centuries. According to the district administration, the town of Jajpur was founded in the early 10th century by the Somavamsi king **Yayati (Jajati) Keshari**. Tradition says he performed the *Dasaswamedha* sacrifice here, inviting 10,000 Brahmins from northern India. The Eastern Ganga king Anangabhima Deva later built a Jagannath temple at Jajpur.

The district administration says a battle at Gohira Tikiri, involving Mukunda Deva Harichandan, opened the way for Afghan rule in Odisha in the 16th century. Under the British, the Bengali novelist **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay** served as deputy magistrate at Jajpur from 1882 to 1884; the district administration says he wrote his novel *Devi Chaudhurani* during this posting. In 1934 Mahatma Gandhi inaugurated the Sevaghar ashram founded by [Rama Devi](/people/rama-devi) at Bari, in present-day Jajpur district.

Jajpur became a separate district in 1993. On **2 January 2006**, police fired on tribal people protesting against land acquisition for industry at Kalinganagar; 13 protesters were killed and more than 40 wounded. The protesters said their land had been taken cheaply by the state industrial corporation and resold to companies, and the firing became a landmark in debates over industrial land acquisition in Odisha.

## Places to visit

### Biraja Temple

The temple of **Goddess Biraja** (Viraja) is the heart of Jajpur town. The goddess is shown as a two-armed Durga, spearing the demon Mahishasura with one hand and pulling his tail with the other. The main festival is the autumn [Durga Puja](/culture/durga-puja), observed here over 16 days, along with a chariot festival in which the goddess rides a chariot called *Simhadhwaja*, whose flag bears a lion. Navaratri, Raja and the harvest festival of Navanna are also observed. The temple is built in the Kalinga style, and the nearest railway stations are Jajpur Keonjhar Road and Cuttack. The goddess also gives her name to the [Biraja Panjika](/culture/biraja-panjika), a widely used Odia almanac.

### Dasaswamedha Ghat and the Baitarani

The **Dasaswamedha Ghat** on the Baitarani is a place for holy dips and ancestral rites. The district administration also lists the **Varaha temple** and the **Trilochaneswar temple** among Jajpur's important shrines.

### Ratnagiri and Udayagiri

These two excavated Buddhist sites, with Lalitgiri in neighbouring Cuttack district, form Odisha's **Diamond Triangle**. **Ratnagiri**, excavated by the Archaeological Survey of India in 1958–61 under Debala Mitra, flourished from about the 5th to the 13th century CE. It has a large monastery with richly carved doorways, a main stupa surrounded by hundreds of votive stupas, and an ASI site museum. **Udayagiri** is the largest of the three sites and the least excavated; inscriptions name its two monasteries Madhavapura Mahavihara and Simhaprastha Mahavihara. See our [Diamond Triangle guide](/travel/diamond-triangle-ratnagiri-udayagiri-lalitgiri) and the [Odisha Buddhist trail](/travel/odisha-buddhist-trail-itinerary).

### Langudi Hill

The Buddhist remains on Langudi hill, discovered in the 1990s, have led some scholars to question the older identification of Ratnagiri with Puspagiri, the monastery described by Xuanzang.

### Chandikhol

The **Chandi temple** at Chandikhol is named after the goddess worshipped there by the monk Baba Bhairabananda, according to the district administration.

### Ashokjhar Waterfall

A waterfall in Sukinda block, about 8 km from the block headquarters. The nearest railway station is Sukinda Road.

## Culture and festivals

Jajpur's religious calendar centres on Biraja and the Baitarani. Pilgrims come year-round for ritual baths and ancestral rites. The district administration highlights the women's floor and wall art of the area in three forms, *jhoti*, *chita* and *muruja*, made with rice paste, powdered stone, dried leaves and marigold petals. Women honour the goddess Lakshmi at harvest time in the month of Margasira (see [Manabasa Gurubara](/culture/manabasa-gurubara)), and make muruja designs beside the tulasi plant during Kartika.

Jajpur also has a literary tradition. The 18th-century poet and musician **Abhimanyu Samanta Singhara**, author of *Bidagdha Chintamani*, was born at Balia, and the district administration also names Upendra Tripathy and Govind Das among Jajpur's writers. Odisha Tourism mentions tussar weaving and golden grass craft as crafts visitors can see.

Odia is spoken by about 90 percent of people, with Urdu and Ho the next most common languages (2011).

## Food

No dish is officially registered to Jajpur. Odisha Tourism's guide to the district suggests everyday Odia food: [pakhala bhata](/food/pakhala-bhata), [dalma](/food/dalma), [machha besara](/food/machha-besara), and sweets such as [chhena poda](/food/chhena-poda) and [rasagola](/food/rasagola).

## Economy

The district administration describes agriculture and mining as the backbone of Jajpur's economy. Paddy is grown on the river plains of the Baitarani and Brahmani.

- **Kalinganagar** is a planned industrial complex with steel plants of Tata Steel, Jindal, VISA Steel, the public-sector Neelachal Ispat Nigam and other companies.
- **Sukinda valley**, a strip of about 50 sq km between the Mahagiri and Daitari hills with around a dozen mines, holds most of India's chromite. Wikipedia states that Odisha has about 98 percent of the country's proved reserves, about 97 percent of them in this valley. The open-cast mines have caused serious water pollution, and in 2007 the Blacksmith Institute listed Sukinda among the world's most polluted places, an assessment local officials disputed.
- **Daitari** has iron ore mines, which the district administration says earn substantial mineral revenue for the state.

## Notable people

- **Yayati (Jajati) Keshari**, the Somavamsi king credited by the district administration with founding Jajpur in the early 10th century.
- **Abhimanyu Samanta Singhara** (1760–1806), born at Balia, Odia poet and Odissi musician, author of *Bidagdha Chintamani*.
- **Dutee Chand**, born in 1996 at Chaka Gopalpur, sprinter, Indian 100 m record holder (11.24 s, 2016), double silver medallist at the 2018 Asian Games and Arjuna Award recipient.

## How to reach

- **By air:** Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, about 102 km away, according to Odisha Tourism.
- **By rail:** **Jajpur Keonjhar Road** station, at Byasanagar on the Howrah–Chennai main line, serves Jajpur town and Kalinganagar. Cuttack is the nearest large junction.
- **By road:** **NH-16** runs through the district, linking the district with Cuttack and Bhubaneswar to the south and Bhadrak to the north.

Odisha Tourism recommends October to March and about two days for the district. See also [how to reach Odisha](/travel/how-to-reach-odisha).
