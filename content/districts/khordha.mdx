## Overview

**Khordha** (ଖୋର୍ଦ୍ଧା), also spelled Khurda or Khurdha, is a district in coastal Odisha that contains **Bhubaneswar**, the state capital. The district headquarters, however, is the historic town of **Khordha**, about 25 km south-west of Bhubaneswar.

Khordha is best known for the temple city of Bhubaneswar, the Kalinga War site at Dhauli, the Jain caves of Udayagiri and Khandagiri, and its role in Odisha's resistance to British rule: Khurda was the seat of the last independent Odia kingdom, and the centre of the [Paika Rebellion](/history/paika-rebellion) of 1817.

The district came into existence on **1 April 1993**, when the old Puri district was split into Puri, Khordha and Nayagarh districts. Wikipedia notes that the name was standardised as Khordha in 2000. It has two sub-divisions, **Khordha** and **Bhubaneswar**.

At the 2011 Census, Khordha had **2,251,673** people on **2,813 sq km**, with about 800 people per sq km. It is Odisha's most urbanised district: about 48% of residents lived in towns, and literacy stood at 86.88%.

## Geography and climate

Khordha lies on the edge of the Mahanadi delta between the coastal plain and the low, forested hills of the Eastern Ghats. It borders Cuttack district to the north, Puri district to the east, Nayagarh to the west and Ganjam to the south-west. In the south the district touches the north-western shore of **Chilika Lake**, around Balugaon, Barkul and Tangi.

The **Daya** and **Kuakhai** rivers flow through the district. Dhauli stands on the bank of the Daya. Wikipedia gives the district's forest area as about 619 sq km. The forests of the **Chandaka-Dampara Wildlife Sanctuary**, notified in December 1982 and covering about 176 sq km of the Khurda uplands, lie on the north-western edge of Bhubaneswar and are known for their elephants.

The climate is tropical. Summers from March to June are very hot, the south-west monsoon brings most of the rain, and winters from November to February are mild and dry.

## History

People have lived and built in this area for well over two thousand years. **Sisupalgarh**, an early fortified city on the eastern edge of Bhubaneswar, is protected by the Archaeological Survey of India. **Dhauli** hill on the Daya is widely believed to be the site of the [Kalinga War](/history/kalinga-war-en), and it bears rock edicts of the Mauryan emperor Ashoka. In the 1st century BCE the Mahameghavahana king [Kharavela](/history/kharavela) is associated with the rock-cut Jain caves of [Udayagiri and Khandagiri](/history/udayagiri-khandagiri).

Between roughly the 7th and 13th centuries, successive dynasties built the temples of Bhubaneswar's Old Town. The [Lingaraj Temple](/history/lingaraj-temple), the largest of them, dates from the late 11th century.

After the defeat of Mukunda Deva in 1568, **Ramachandra Deva I** of the Bhoi dynasty set up a new power centre at **Khurda**. The Khurda kings retained control of the [Jagannath Temple](/history/jagannath-temple) at Puri, which gave them standing as the traditional rulers of Odisha even after the Marathas took most of the region in the 18th century. Wikipedia describes Khurda as the seat of Odisha's kings from 1568 to 1803.

The East India Company took Odisha in 1803. After a failed revolt by King **Mukunda Deva II**, the Company annexed Khurda in 1804. His minister and royal priest **Jayee Rajguru** was executed at Midnapore on 6 December 1806, and many in Odisha remember him as one of the earliest martyrs of resistance to British rule.

In 1817 the Paikas, the traditional militia of Khurda, rose against the Company's land and salt policies under **Buxi Jagabandhu**. The [Paika Rebellion](/history/paika-rebellion) spread to Puri and beyond before it was suppressed. Jagabandhu held out until 1825.

In the 20th century Bhubaneswar was chosen as the capital of the new state, replacing Cuttack in 1948. Bhubaneswar and Khurda remained sub-divisions of Puri district until Khordha district was created in 1993.

## Places to visit

### Bhubaneswar Old Town temples

Around the Bindu Sagar tank stand the [Lingaraj Temple](/history/lingaraj-temple), Mukteswar, Parasurameswar and Rajarani temples, among many others. They make the city one of the best places in India to see the evolution of the Kalinga style of temple architecture. Entry to the Lingaraj sanctum is restricted to Hindus. See the [Bhubaneswar travel guide](/travel/bhubaneswar).

### Udayagiri and Khandagiri

These twin sandstone hills on the western edge of Bhubaneswar hold rock-cut chambers made for Jain monks. They include the Hathigumpha inscription of Kharavela. Read more about [Udayagiri and Khandagiri](/history/udayagiri-khandagiri).

### Dhauli

Dhauli hill, about 8 km south of Bhubaneswar, has Ashokan rock edicts and a white Peace Pagoda (Shanti Stupa), which was built in 1972 with Japanese Buddhist support.

### Nandankanan Zoological Park

Nandankanan was inaugurated on 29 December 1960 and spreads over about 437 hectares around Kanjia Lake, between Bhubaneswar and Cuttack. It is known for breeding white tigers since 1980, and in 2009 it became the first Indian zoo to join the World Association of Zoos and Aquariums. The park is closed on Mondays.

### Atri hot spring

Atri, about 15 km west of Khordha town, has a perennial hot spring of about 57 °C and the temple of **Hatakeswar** (Shiva). The ENVIS eco-heritage centre records a large fair here on [Makar Sankranti](/culture/makar-sankranti-en).

### Barunei hill

The hill near Khordha town has a shrine to the twin goddesses **Barunei and Karunei** and a hill stream, the Swarna Ganga. [Raja Parba](/culture/raja-parba) is the main festival at the temple.

### Mangalajodi and Chilika

**Mangalajodi**, in Tangi block on the northern edge of Chilika, is a community-protected wetland. Former bird hunters now guide visitors by boat, and peak winter counts have topped 150,000 birds. Barkul, near Balugaon, is another base for boat trips on the lake. See the [Chilika Lake travel guide](/travel/chilika-lake-travel-guide).

### Banapur

Banapur, near Chilika in the south of the district, is known for its **Bhagabati temple**, which is regarded as a Shakti pitha. The town also celebrates **Panchudola**, a festival held five days after Dola Purnima in which deities from nearby villages are carried in procession. Banapur was also a centre of the 1817 rising.

## Culture and festivals

Khordha's festival calendar centres on Bhubaneswar's temples. The Lingaraj Temple celebrates **Maha Shivaratri**, **Ashokastami** (the Rukuna Rath chariot procession) and a 22-day **[Chandan Yatra](/culture/chandan-yatra)** at Bindu Sagar. Each winter the city hosts the Mukteswar Dance Festival, which features [Odissi](/culture/odissi-dance), and the Rajarani Music Festival.

[Durga Puja](/culture/durga-puja) in Bhubaneswar is known for its themed pandals and for the burning of Ravana effigies on Dussehra. [Raja Parba](/culture/raja-parba) and [Makar Sankranti](/culture/makar-sankranti-en) are widely observed in the villages. The Ekamra Haat market in Bhubaneswar is the easiest place to find handloom and crafts from across Odisha, including [Sambalpuri sarees](/culture/sambalpuri-saree) and [Pipili appliqué](/culture/pipili-applique).

## Food

As the capital district, Khordha has restaurants serving food from across Odisha, including [dalma](/food/dalma), [pakhala bhata](/food/pakhala-bhata) and [dahibara aloodum](/food/dahibara-aloodum). **Pahala**, a roadside sweet market on National Highway 16 between Bhubaneswar and Cuttack, is well known for [rasagola](/food/rasagola), [chhena gaja](/food/chhena-gaja) and [chhena poda](/food/chhena-poda). Kora khai, a sweet of puffed paddy and jaggery, is commonly associated with Bhubaneswar's temple fairs.

## Economy

Bhubaneswar dominates the district economy as Odisha's administrative, education and services centre, with a growing information-technology sector. **Khurda Road** is the headquarters of an East Coast Railway division. Wikipedia lists brass utensils, cottage industries and cable manufacturing among the district's traditional and modern industries, along with large food and beverage plants. Outside the city, farming (mainly paddy) remains important, and families near Chilika depend on fishing and, increasingly, on nature tourism.

## Notable people

- **Buxi Jagabandhu Bidyadhar**: military commander of the Khurda kingdom and leader of the [Paika Rebellion](/history/paika-rebellion) of 1817.
- **Godabarish Mishra** (1886–1956): poet, educationist and politician, born at Srinibaspur Sasan in present-day Khordha district. He belonged to the circle of [Gopabandhu Das](/people/gopabandhu-das) at the Satyabadi school.
- **Ranganath Misra** (born 1926): born at Banapur, he was Chief Justice of India from 1990 to 1991.

Jayee Rajguru, the Khurda minister executed in 1806, was born near Puri; see the [Puri district](/district/puri) page.

## How to reach

- **By air:** Biju Patnaik International Airport is in Bhubaneswar itself, inside the district.
- **By rail:** Bhubaneswar and Khurda Road Junction are on the main Howrah–Chennai line, with the branch to Puri leaving from Khurda Road.
- **By road:** National Highway 16 (Kolkata–Chennai) runs through Bhubaneswar and Khordha town. Khordha town is about 25 km from Bhubaneswar, and Tangi, the access town for Mangalajodi, is about 60 km.

For connections from other states see [how to reach Odisha](/travel/how-to-reach-odisha), and for nearby districts see [Puri](/district/puri), [Cuttack](/district/cuttack) and [Nayagarh](/district/nayagarh).
