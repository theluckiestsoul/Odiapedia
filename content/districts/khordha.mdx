## At a Glance
Khordha is the administrative heart of Odisha, housing the state capital, **Bhubaneswar**. It is the most urbanized district of Odisha (about 48% urban in Census 2011). The Khurda fort is remembered as the "last independent fort" to hold out against British rule.

## The Heritage Diary
Khordha became the capital of the Bhoi king Ramachandra Deva in the late 16th century, and its rulers long resisted British control. The **Paika Bidroha** (1817), led by Buxi Jagabandhu, started here and is considered by many as the First War of Indian Independence. Bhubaneswar, the "Temple City," has a history spanning over 2,000 years with Kalinga architecture at its zenith.

## Local Landmarks
- **Bhubaneswar:** Home to Lingaraj Temple, Mukteswar, Rajarani, and hundreds of ancient sandstone temples. Also the hub of IT and education.
- **Dhauli:** The site of the Kalinga War where Emperor Ashoka renounced violence. It has a Shanti Stupa (Peace Pagoda).
- **Chilika Lake (Mangalajodi):** The northern sector of Chilika, famous as a birdwatcher's paradise.
- **Nandankanan:** A famous zoo and botanical garden, known for its White Tigers.
- **Atri:** A hot sulfur spring with a Temple of Lord Hatakeswar.
- **Barunei Hill:** A historic hill fort associated with the Paika rebellion.

## Taste of the Land
- **Dalma:** The state dish, widely available here.
- **Kora Khai:** A traditional sweet of popped rice and jaggery, associated with Bhubaneswar's temple traditions.
- **Matti Handi Mutton:** Mutton cooked in earthen pots is popular in Bhubaneswar.

## Local Heroes
- **Buxi Jagabandhu:** The commander of the Paika rebellion.
- **Jayee Rajguru:** Royal priest and chief minister of the Khurda king, executed by the British in 1806; he is often described as one of the earliest martyrs of resistance to British rule (he was born near Puri).
- **Godabarish Mishra:** Born near Banapur; one of the "Panchasakha" (five friends) of Gopabandhu Das's Satyabadi group, a poet and freedom fighter.
