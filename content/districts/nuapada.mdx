## Overview

**Nuapada** (ନୂଆପଡ଼ା) is a district on the western edge of Odisha, bordering the state of Chhattisgarh. Nuapada is known for the **Sunabeda** plateau and wildlife sanctuary, for the **Yogeswar temple** and Upper Jonk reservoir at **Patora**, for the sacred spring of **Patalganga**, and for the old town of **Khariar**, once the seat of a Chauhan ruling family.

The district headquarters is the town of **Nuapada**. The district has a single sub-division and five blocks: Nuapada, Khariar, Sinapali, Boden and Komna. Its urban centres include Nuapada, Khariar and Khariar Road.

Nuapada was part of [Kalahandi](/district/kalahandi) district from 1 November 1949, when the Nuapada sub-division was added to it. On **27 March 1993** it was carved out as a separate district.

The 2011 Census gives the district's area as **3,852 sq km**; Wikipedia gives a smaller figure of about 3,408 sq km. The 2011 Census counted **610,382** people. Scheduled Tribes made up about 34% of the population and Scheduled Castes about 13%, and literacy was 57.35%.

## Geography and climate

Nuapada lies on the western border of Odisha. The Mahasamund district of Chhattisgarh borders it on the north, west and south, and Bargarh, Balangir and Kalahandi districts lie to the east.

**Plateau and hills.** The district has hills and plateaus forming an extension of the Eastern Ghats, with plateau tops at around 4,000 feet according to Wikipedia. The best known is the **Sunabeda plateau**, with valleys, gorges and waterfalls.

**Rivers.** The **Jonk** drains the Sunabeda plateau and is dammed at Patora for the Upper Jonk irrigation project. The **Udanti** and the **Indra** are other important rivers; the Lower Indra irrigation project has its dam at Tikhali, near Khariar.

**Climate.** Nuapada has a hot summer, a monsoon season and a cool winter. Wikipedia gives average annual rainfall of about 1,230 mm. Odisha Tourism suggests visiting from October to March.

## History

**Ancient and early medieval period.** The district administration's history traces the region to **Mahakantara**, the forest kingdom named in Samudragupta's 4th-century inscription, followed by the **Nala** kings (up to about 500 CE) and later the **Somavamsis**. It also notes an ancient irrigation work and heritage site at **Manikgarh** near Patora that it dates to the pre-Christian era.

**Khariar and the Chauhans.** The **Chauhan** family of Khariar descended from the Chauhan rulers of Patna (Balangir). The district administration dates the founding of the Khariar line by **Gopal Ray** to about 1590 CE, while Wikipedia gives about 1600 CE. The Khariar zamindari came under the Marathas in 1741, and after the British defeated the Marathas it came under British occupation in 1818.

**Colonial and modern period.** In 1906 Khariar was attached to the Mahasamund tahsil, according to the district administration. In 1936 it was added to the newly formed province of Orissa. After independence, the area joined Kalahandi district as the Nuapada sub-division on 1 November 1949, and became a separate district on 27 March 1993.

**Development challenges.** Nuapada is part of the KBK (Kalahandi–Balangir–Koraput) region identified for special development programmes, and Wikipedia notes that it has often been in the news for reported starvation deaths.

## Places to visit

### Patora and the Yogeswar temple

Patora, about 18 to 22 km from Nuapada, lies on the Jonk river among green hills. The **Yogeswar (Jogeswar) temple** houses an ancient Shiva linga, which Wikipedia describes as dating back to the 6th century, and a joint image of Shiva and Parvati. The dam of the **Upper Jonk Irrigation Project** forms a reservoir where the district administration notes scope for boating, and the site also has a garden called Rajiv Udyan. Odisha Tourism lists the Patora Nature Camp as an eco-stay.

### Sunabeda Wildlife Sanctuary

Sunabeda Wildlife Sanctuary, declared in **1988**, covers about **600 sq km** of plateau, valleys, gorges and waterfalls adjoining Chhattisgarh. Wikipedia records leopard, Indian gaur, sloth bear, barking deer and nilgai, and about 200 species of birds; tigers have been reported, though poaching has reduced wildlife numbers. A larger tiger reserve including Sunabeda has received in-principle approval but has not been notified.

### Patalganga

Patalganga, about 22 km from Khariar, is a spring regarded as sacred. According to local tradition, when Sita grew thirsty during the exile, Lakshmana pierced the ground with an arrow and the spring burst forth. People bathe here in large numbers during solar and lunar eclipses.

### Khariar

Khariar, the former seat of the Chauhan zamindars, has temples such as the Maa Samaleswari and Jagannath temples. Wikipedia lists the Tikhali dam of the Lower Indra project, about 17 km away, and Ramgarh hill among nearby sights.

### Godheswar waterfall

Odisha Tourism lists a waterfall it calls **Godheswar** among the district's natural attractions. Ask locally about access, especially during the monsoon.

## Culture and festivals

**Language.** In the 2011 Census about 58% of the people spoke Odia, about 24% Sambalpuri and about 15% Chhattisgarhi as their first language, reflecting the district's position between western Odisha and Chhattisgarh.

**Festivals.** **[Nuakhai](/culture/nuakhai)**, the festival of the new rice, is the most important festival of the district, as it is across western Odisha. Shiva shrines such as Yogeswar draw pilgrims on Maha Shivaratri, and Patalganga attracts bathers on eclipse days.

**Communities.** About a third of the population belongs to Scheduled Tribes. The **Chuktia Bhunjia** live in the hills of the Sunabeda plateau in Komna block and keep their distance from outsiders; their festivals include Dussehra, Chaval Dhona and Naya Khana (the new-harvest festival).

## Economy

Nuapada is mainly agricultural. Rice is the main crop, followed by maize, cotton and onion. Three irrigation projects, the **Upper Jonk**, the **Sundar dam** and the **Lower Indra** project, serve farmland in the district.

## How to reach

| Mode | Details |
|---|---|
| Air | The nearest airport is Swami Vivekananda Airport, Raipur; Odisha Tourism gives the distance as about 117 km, while the district administration gives about 142 km from Patora |
| Rail | Khariar Road station, about 10 km from Nuapada, is on the Raipur–Vizianagaram line of East Coast Railway, about 110 km from Raipur; Nuapada also has its own station. Kantabanji, in Balangir district, is the nearest station to Khariar town |
| Road | National and state highways link Nuapada with Raipur, Sambalpur and Bhubaneswar. Wikipedia gives the road distance from Khariar to Bhubaneswar as about 430 km |
