**Jharsuguda** (ଝାରସୁଗୁଡ଼ା) is a district in western Odisha bordering Chhattisgarh. Jharsuguda district is a major centre of coal mining, power generation and aluminium, and is often called the "Powerhouse of Odisha". Its town is also home to Veer Surendra Sai Airport, the main airport for western Odisha.

## Overview

Jharsuguda is one of Odisha's smaller districts, covering 2,081 sq km. At the 2011 Census it had 579,505 people. With almost 40 percent of its people living in towns, it is among the more urbanised districts in the state. Its main towns are Jharsuguda, Brajrajnagar and Belpahar.

The district was carved out of Sambalpur district in **1994**; local sources give the date as 1 January 1994. Jharsuguda had earlier been made a sub-division of Sambalpur in 1979. It has five community development blocks: Jharsuguda, Kirmira, Kolabira, Laikera and Lakhanpur.

The district's growth is tied to two things: the railway, which reached Jharsuguda in the 1880s and turned it into an important junction, and coal, which was found in the Ib valley in 1900. Today Jharsuguda is a fast-growing industrial district with mines, power stations and metal plants, though agriculture and forests remain important in its villages.

Scheduled Tribes make up about 30 percent of the population. Sambalpuri is the most widely spoken home language, followed by Odia, Hindi and Kisan.

## Geography and climate

Jharsuguda borders Raigarh district of Chhattisgarh to the west, Sundargarh to the north, Sambalpur to the east and Bargarh to the south. The district lies in the Mahanadi basin. The **Ib** river, a tributary of the Mahanadi, flows through the coal-bearing Ib valley, and the **Bheden** drains the southern part.

Much of the land is gently rolling upland with scattered hills and patches of sal and mahua forest, especially around Belpahar and Lakhanpur.

## History

The Jharsuguda area has traces of very early human activity. The **Vikramkhol (Bikramkhol)** rock shelter near Belpahar carries painted and incised marks covering an area of about 35 by 7 feet. They were first studied in the 1930s by the historian K. P. Jayaswal, who saw them as an early script. Later scholars disagree: some see links with Brahmi or Indus signs, while others regard them as non-writing marks. The marks remain undeciphered.

In the medieval period the area was part of the **Chauhan** kingdom of Sambalpur, founded by Balaram Dev in the 16th century. Local zamindaris such as Rampur, Kolabira and Padampur held land under the Sambalpur kings, and the area of present-day Jharsuguda town was then a small forest settlement.

The region took part in the revolt led by **Veer Surendra Sai** against British rule in Sambalpur, which lasted until 1862. According to a local history, Karunakar Naik, the zamindar of Kolabira, was hanged at Sambalpur for his part in the struggle of 1857–1863.

Modern Jharsuguda grew with the **Bengal Nagpur Railway**, which opened a station here in the 1880s. Coal was discovered by accident in 1900 during bridge building on the Ib, and the first mine in the Ib valley opened in 1909. During the last years of the Second World War the Allies built a chain of airfields in the region, including one at Jharsuguda, to guard against a possible Japanese attack from the north-east. The airstrip was abandoned after Independence and was later rebuilt as today's Veer Surendra Sai Airport.

After Independence, Jharsuguda remained part of Sambalpur district. Its first municipal council chairman was elected in 1952, the town became a sub-division headquarters in 1979, and the district was created in 1994. Coal mining in the Ib valley was nationalised in the 1970s. It passed to Western Coalfields in 1973, to South Eastern Coalfields in 1986, and to Mahanadi Coalfields in 1992.

## Places to visit

### Koilighugar Waterfall

Near Kushmelbahal village in Lakhanpur block, the Ahiraj stream falls about 61 m (200 ft) through sal and mahua forest. A Shiva shrine called Maheswarnath stands at the waterfall, and a fair is held at Shivaratri. Koilighugar is about 26 km from Jharsuguda by road, according to Wikipedia.

### Ramchandi Temple, Rampur

A temple of the goddess Ramchandi on the bank of the Ib river at Rampur, near Brajrajnagar, and a place of local pilgrimage.

### Vikramkhol rock shelter

The Vikramkhol shelter lies in the Belpahar reserved forest, about 12 km from Belpahar. It is valued for its undeciphered ancient marks. The site is not fully protected and has suffered from weathering and vandalism, so visitors should not touch the rock surface.

### The Ib river

The Ib valley is at its most scenic during and just after the monsoon, when the river is in spate and the countryside turns green.

## Culture and festivals

Jharsuguda shares the culture of western Odisha. [Nuakhai](/culture/nuakhai), the festival of the new rice harvest, is the most important festival in the district. Durga Puja, Ganesh Puja, Diwali and [Rath Yatra](/culture/rath-yatra) are also celebrated widely. [Sitala Sasthi](/culture/sitala-sasthi), the festival of Shiva and Parvati's wedding, has spread from Sambalpur to Jharsuguda and other towns.

The everyday language of much of the district is Sambalpuri (Kosli), which about 42 percent of people gave as their mother tongue in 2011, ahead of Odia at about 27 percent. Hindi, Kisan, Mundari and Kharia are also spoken.

Folk forms such as Dalkhai and other [Sambalpuri dances](/culture/sambalpuri-dance) are performed at festivals. The district's Adivasi communities include the Kisan, Oraon (Kurukh), Munda, Bhuiyan, Sabar and Santal, and each keeps its own traditions of song, dance and worship. Because industry has drawn workers from across India, the towns are culturally mixed.

## Food

Jharsuguda's food is the food of western Odisha. Rice is the staple, eaten both freshly cooked and as pakhala. Popular local items include **chaula bara**, a crisp rice-batter snack, and dishes made with **kardi** (tender bamboo shoot) and **hendua** (dried bamboo shoot). During the monsoon, wild mushrooms (**ruguda**) gathered from the forests are a seasonal delicacy.

## Economy

Industry dominates Jharsuguda's economy.

- **Coal**: the **Ib Valley Coalfield**, covering about 1,375 sq km, is run by Mahanadi Coalfields Limited (MCL). According to the Geological Survey of India it holds the third-largest coal reserves in India, about 22.3 billion tonnes. Large open-cast mines include Lajkura, Samaleswari and Lilari. Output rose from about 0.55 million tonnes in 1972–73 to about 15.5 million tonnes in 2002–03, according to Wikipedia, and an MCL-funded rail line is planned to move more coal.
- **Power**: plentiful coal has attracted several thermal power plants, which is why the district is called the Powerhouse of Odisha.
- **Aluminium**: Vedanta operates a large aluminium smelter at Jharsuguda.
- **Steel and other units**: sponge iron and steel plants operate across the district.
- **Agriculture**: paddy is the main crop in the villages.

In 2006 Jharsuguda was listed by the central government among India's 250 most backward districts, a status that reflects the gap between its industrial towns and rural areas.

## Notable people

- **Karunakar Naik**: zamindar of Kolabira, executed at Sambalpur for his part in the 1857–1863 rising, according to local histories.

The district is also closely linked with **Veer Surendra Sai** (born at Khinda in Sambalpur district), whose name the airport carries.

## How to reach

- **Air**: Veer Surendra Sai Airport, about 5 km north-east of the town, was inaugurated on 22 September 2018 and has flights to several Indian cities. Check current schedules.
- **Rail**: Jharsuguda Junction is on the Howrah–Nagpur–Mumbai main line and is the start of the line to Sambalpur, Titlagarh and Vizianagaram, with direct trains to Bhubaneswar and many other cities.
- **Road**: National Highway 49 and the Biju Expressway pass through the district. Sambalpur is about 60 km away. Bhubaneswar is roughly 330 to 365 km away by road via Sambalpur; estimates vary with the route.
