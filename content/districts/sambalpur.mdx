**Sambalpur** (ସମ୍ବଲପୁର) is a district in western Odisha whose headquarters, Sambalpur city, is the main urban and cultural centre of the region. Sambalpur district is known for the Samaleswari Temple, the Hirakud Dam on the Mahanadi, and the Sambalpuri bandha handloom tradition.

## Overview

Sambalpur district lies in the Mahanadi basin in north-western Odisha. Its headquarters city takes its name from the goddess Samalei, or Samaleswari, who is regarded as the presiding deity of the region. In older times the area was also known as Hirakhanda, a name linked to the diamonds once traded here.

The district has three sub-divisions: Sambalpur, Kuchinda and Rairakhol. It covers 6,624 sq km according to the 2011 Census (some official sources give a slightly larger figure) and had a population of 1,041,099 in 2011. About 30 percent of the people lived in towns, mainly in the Sambalpur urban area, and Scheduled Tribes made up roughly a third of the population.

Sambalpur was once a far larger district. After Independence it also included what are now Bargarh, Jharsuguda and Deogarh. Bargarh was separated in 1993, and Jharsuguda and Deogarh followed in 1994, leaving the present district.

Today Sambalpur is a hub for education, administration and railways in western Odisha. It is home to Sambalpur University, Veer Surendra Sai University of Technology at Burla, VIMSAR medical college and IIM Sambalpur. Sambalpur is also the headquarters of the Sambalpur division of East Coast Railway.

## Geography and climate

Sambalpur district borders Sundargarh to the north, Deogarh to the east, Angul and Subarnapur to the south, and Bargarh and Jharsuguda to the west. The **Mahanadi**, Odisha's longest river, enters from the north-west, where it is held back by the **Hirakud Dam**. Smaller streams in the district include the Maltijor, Harrad, Kulsara, Bheden and Phuljharan.

The land falls into three broad zones: hilly country around Bamra and Kuchinda in the north, plateau ridges near Rairakhol in the south-east, and the more open valleys and plains of the Sambalpur sub-division. Large parts of the north and south-east are forested. Sources differ widely on the exact forest cover, depending on how forest is measured.

The district has two wildlife sanctuaries of its own. **Ushakothi (Badrama) Wildlife Sanctuary**, notified in 1962, covers about 304 sq km. **Khalasuni Wildlife Sanctuary**, set up in 1982, covers about 116 sq km and is part of an important elephant corridor. Debrigarh Wildlife Sanctuary lies on the far shore of the Hirakud reservoir, in neighbouring Bargarh district.

## History

The Sambalpur region has a long recorded history. The Roman-era geographer Ptolemy, writing in the 2nd century CE, mentions a place called Sambalaka. It is usually identified with Sambalpur, which was known for exporting diamonds. In the early medieval period the area came under the **Somavamsi** kings (around the 9th century) and later the **Eastern Gangas**, before passing to the Gajapati kings.

The defining era began with the **Chauhan** rulers. A branch of the Chauhans first ruled the Patna kingdom (around present-day Balangir), and in the 16th century **Balaram Dev** founded a separate kingdom at Sambalpur. Odisha Tourism says Balaram Dev made Samaleswari the state's chief goddess in about 1542 CE, while other accounts date the founding of the kingdom to around 1570. The Chauhans ruled Sambalpur for nearly three centuries and built many Shiva temples.

The **Marathas of Nagpur** took Sambalpur in 1800. After the Anglo-Maratha wars it came under British control in 1817, and in **1849** the British annexed it under the doctrine of lapse after Raja Narayan Singh died without a male heir.

Resistance to British rule centred on **Veer Surendra Sai** (1809–1884), a Chauhan claimant to the throne. He took up arms in 1827, was imprisoned at Hazaribagh, was freed by rebels in 1857 and led a guerrilla campaign in the forests until he surrendered in 1862. Re-arrested in 1864, he died at Asirgarh fort in 1884 after spending about 37 years in British prisons.

Under British rule Sambalpur moved between provinces several times: to the Central Provinces in 1862, back to Bengal in 1905, then to Bihar and Orissa in 1912, and into the new Orissa province in 1936. On 1 January 1948 the princely state of **Rairakhol** acceded to India and became part of Sambalpur district, and the Bamra state around Deogarh was also absorbed. The **Hirakud Dam** was inaugurated by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru on 13 January 1957. Read more in [Sambalpur history](/history/sambalpur-history).

## Places to visit

### Samaleswari Temple

The temple of Maa Samaleswari in Sambalpur city, on the bank of the Mahanadi, is the region's most important shrine. Odisha Tourism notes that its main priests trace their descent to the Chauhan family. Major festivals here include Nuakhai, Mahavisuba Sankranti and the nine-day Durga Puja.

### Hirakud Dam

Hirakud is a large earthen dam across the Mahanadi near Sambalpur. Its main dam is about 4.8 km long, and with its dykes the whole structure runs to about 25.8 km. It is often described as the longest earthen dam in the world. Gandhi Minar and Jawahar Minar, towers at either end, give wide views over the reservoir, which has been a Ramsar wetland since 2021. See the [Hirakud Dam travel guide](/travel/hirakud-dam).

### Huma Leaning Temple

At Huma, about 23 km south of Sambalpur on the Mahanadi, stands the leaning temple of Vimaleswara (Bimaleswar), a form of Shiva. The main temple and the smaller shrines around it tilt at different angles, and Odisha Tourism suggests the lean may have been deliberate. Odisha Tourism dates the temple to around the mid-16th century; other accounts credit Chauhan kings of the 17th century with rebuilding it. Visitors feed the fish in the temple pond, and a fair is held at Shivaratri.

### Ghanteswari Temple and Chiplima

The Ghanteswari temple near Chiplima is known for the many bells hung there by devotees. According to Wikipedia, Chiplima also has a natural waterfall about 24 m high.

### Budharaja Temple

A Shiva temple on a hillock within Sambalpur city, one of several Shiva shrines built in the Chauhan period.

### Ushakothi and Khalasuni sanctuaries

These forested sanctuaries in the north of the district shelter elephants, leopards and many forest birds. Visitor facilities are limited, so check with the forest department before going.

## Culture and festivals

Sambalpur is the centre of a distinct western Odisha culture, with its own language variety, music and dance. Many people speak Sambalpuri (also called Kosli) at home, alongside standard Odia. The district is also home to communities such as the Kisan, Munda and Oraon.

- **[Nuakhai](/culture/nuakhai)** is the most important festival of western Odisha. Families eat the first rice of the new harvest together, and the goddess Samaleswari receives the first offering.
- **[Sitala Sasthi](/culture/sitala-sasthi)** celebrates the wedding of Shiva and Parvati in Jyeshtha (May–June). In Sambalpur, local families act as the bride's parents, and Shiva's wedding procession passes through the city.
- **[Sambalpuri dance](/culture/sambalpuri-dance)**: folk forms such as Dalkhai and Rasarkeli are performed to the dhol, nisan and mahuri at festivals.
- **Handloom**: the **[Sambalpuri saree](/culture/sambalpuri-saree)**, woven with the bandha (tie-dye) technique, is a registered Geographical Indication as "Sambalpuri Bandha Saree and Fabrics". Weaving is spread across the western districts. See [Odisha's GI tags](/culture/odisha-gi-tags).
- **Crafts**: a central government district profile lists bamboo work, [dhokra](/culture/dhokra-craft) metal casting and lac bangles among the district's craft clusters.

## Food

Sambalpur shares the food culture of western Odisha. **Chaula bara**, a crisp fried snack of rice batter, is sold in Sambalpur and nearby towns, often with green chillies and tamarind soup. Other regional favourites include dishes made with **kardi** (tender bamboo shoot) and **hendua** (dried bamboo shoot), and **lethaa**, a sour vegetable soup also called ambil. Festival sweets include **rasbaraa** and **tikhri**. Fish from the Mahanadi and the Hirakud reservoir is common in local cooking.

## Economy

Agriculture and forestry are the traditional base of Sambalpur's economy. Water from the Hirakud project supports paddy farming in the plains, and the district has many rice mills. Kendu leaf, used to roll bidis, is an important forest product and is often called the "green gold" of Odisha.

Industry has grown around power, aluminium and steel. A government district profile names Hindalco, sponge iron and steel units, and Mahanadi Coalfields among the large establishments. Minerals found in the district include bauxite, coal, dolomite, graphite and quartz. Handloom weaving, government services, education and the railway division are also major employers. In 2006 the central government listed Sambalpur among India's 250 most backward districts, making it eligible for the Backward Regions Grant Fund.

## Notable people

- **Veer Surendra Sai** (1809–1884): freedom fighter, born at Khinda, about 40 km north of Sambalpur; Jharsuguda's airport is named after him.
- **[Bhima Bhoi](/people/bhima-bhoi)**: the 19th-century poet-saint of Mahima Dharma. Some accounts place his birth in the Rairakhol area, though sources differ.
- **Shriyanka Sadangi** (born 1995 in Sambalpur): rifle shooter, gold medallist at the 2019 South Asian Games.

## How to reach

- **Air**: Veer Surendra Sai Airport, Jharsuguda, is the nearest airport, about 60 km by road. Biju Patnaik International Airport in Bhubaneswar is roughly 300 to 325 km away.
- **Rail**: Sambalpur is a railway junction and the headquarters of a division of East Coast Railway, with trains to Bhubaneswar, Puri, Rourkela, Raipur and other cities.
- **Road**: National Highways 53 and 55 pass through Sambalpur, and the Biju Expressway links it with Rourkela and Bargarh. The road journey from Bhubaneswar is roughly 290 to 325 km, depending on the route. Odisha Tourism gives 287 km to nearby Huma.
