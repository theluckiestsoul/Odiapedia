## Overview

**Kalahandi** (କଳାହାଣ୍ଡି) is a district in western Odisha, with its headquarters at **Bhawanipatna**. Kalahandi is a land of plains, plateaus and forested hills drained by the Tel and Indravati rivers, and is known for the ancient fort of **Asurgarh**, the **Manikeswari temple** and its Chhatar Jatra, the **Ghumura** drum dance, **Habaspuri** weaving and the waterfalls around Bhawanipatna.

The district has two sub-divisions, **Bhawanipatna** and **Dharamgarh**, and 13 blocks. Bhawanipatna sub-division includes the Bhawanipatna, Kesinga, Lanjigarh, Narla, Karlamunda, Madanpur Rampur and Thuamul Rampur blocks; Dharamgarh sub-division includes Dharamgarh, Junagarh, Koksara, Jaipatna, Kalampur and Golamunda.

The district grew out of the former **princely state of Kalahandi**, whose ruler signed the accession to India on **1 January 1948**. On 1 November 1949 the Nuapada sub-division was added to the district, and on 27 March 1993 it was separated again as [Nuapada](/district/nuapada) district.

Kalahandi covers **7,920 sq km**, according to the 2011 Census. The 2011 Census counted **1,576,869** people. Scheduled Tribes made up about 28.5% and Scheduled Castes about 18.2% of the population, and literacy was 59.22%.

## Geography and climate

Kalahandi lies in the south-west of Odisha. Its neighbours include Nuapada, Balangir, Rayagada and Nabarangpur districts. Wikipedia describes a landscape of plains, hills and mountains, with about a third of the area under dense forest.

**Rivers.** The **Tel**, a tributary of the Mahanadi, is the main river of the district, with tributaries such as the **Utei**, **Hati**, **Sagada**, **Udanti** and **Rahul**. The **Indravati** rises in the hills of **Thuamul Rampur** in the south of the district and flows south towards Nabarangpur and Bastar. The Upper Indravati project at **Mukhiguda** dams the river for power and irrigation.

**Hills and forests.** The district includes the high plateau of Thuamul Rampur, the **Ampani** hills and part of the **Niyamgiri** range, which it shares with Rayagada. **Karlapat Wildlife Sanctuary**, close to Bhawanipatna, protects a block of forest, and Wikipedia records leopard, sambar, nilgai, barking deer and mouse deer there.

**Climate.** Kalahandi has a hot summer, a monsoon season from June to September, and a dry, cool winter. Historically the district was prone to drought, though large areas are now irrigated.

## History

**Ancient period.** Kalahandi is identified with **Mahakantara** ("great forest"), a forest kingdom named in Samudragupta's Allahabad pillar inscription of the 4th century CE; the district administration's history notes a ruler called Vyaghraraja in this period. Near Narla, the fortified site of **Asurgarh** covers about 24 hectares and has four gates and a large reservoir. Excavations there found coins of the pre-Mauryan, Mauryan and Gupta periods. Sources differ on its age: some date the settlement to the second half of the first millennium BCE, while the ASI is quoted as placing its beginnings earlier. The old name **Karunda Mandal** recalls the region's gemstones.

**Early dynasties.** The Nala kings ruled the region until about 500 CE, followed by the kingdom of Tustikara in the 6th century and then the Somavamsis. The district administration's history records an invasion by Rajendra Chola in 1022 CE and the later rise of the Eastern Gangas.

**Naga dynasty.** According to local tradition, the **Naga** rulers of Kalahandi began their rule in **1005 CE**, though many historians place the rise of the Nagas in the 15th century, after the decline of the Ganga empire. Their capital was at **Junagarh** before it moved to Bhawanipatna. The district administration notes that the earliest known use of the name "Kalahandi" is in the **Dadhivaman temple inscription of 1718**. The Bhonslas of Nagpur held the region in the mid-18th century, and it came under British authority in 1853. Kalahandi was recognised as a princely state in 1874 and was a 9-gun salute state.

**Accession and after.** The last ruler, **Pratap Keshari Deo**, signed the accession to the Indian Union on 1 January 1948. In the 1980s Kalahandi became known across India for drought and starvation deaths. Since the completion of the **Indravati irrigation project** in the late 1990s the district has changed markedly: ThePrint reported in 2024 that its kharif rice output had risen from about 2.38 lakh tonnes in 1995 to about 8.8 lakh tonnes in 2023.

## Places to visit

### Manikeswari temple, Bhawanipatna

The Manikeswari temple in Bhawanipatna is the shrine of the presiding goddess of the former royal family. Its foundation was laid by Udit Narayan Deo and it was completed in 1947 by Brajamohan Deo. The **Chhatar Jatra** is held on the navami (ninth day) of Dussehra and draws large crowds.

### Phurlijharan

Phurlijharan is a perennial waterfall on the Sagada river, about 15 km from Bhawanipatna. The district administration gives its height as about 30 feet, while Wikipedia gives about 16 m. Sunlight often makes rainbows in the spray.

### Rabandhara

Rabandhara, about 10 to 13 km from Bhawanipatna, is a waterfall in a gorge of rock and forest. Odisha Tourism runs an eco-tourism site with cottages overlooking the Pengsur reservoir, from which guided treks and nature trails are offered.

### Karlapat Wildlife Sanctuary

Karlapat, about 15 km from Bhawanipatna, is a sanctuary of about 175 sq km. Its forests shelter leopard and several deer species, and birds such as the Malabar trogon and great eared nightjar have been recorded, according to Odisha Tourism.

### Junagarh

Junagarh, about 26 km from Bhawanipatna, was the old capital of the Kalahandi rulers. It has a fort, temples such as the Lankeswari and Dadhibaman temples, Odia inscriptions and **sati pillars** recording a practice of medieval times.

### Asurgarh

The ruins of Asurgarh near Narla, with their ramparts and reservoir, are the district's most important archaeological site (see History).

### Belkhandi

Belkhandi, about 67 km from Bhawanipatna at the meeting of the Tel and Utei rivers, has temples and the ruins of 12th-century monuments, including sculptures of the Seven Mother Goddesses.

### Ampani

The Ampani hills, about 77 km from Bhawanipatna, offer wide views and forest wildlife, according to the district administration.

### Dokarichanchara

Dokarichanchara, in Koksara block, is a waterfall with a Rama temple where Ram Navami is celebrated over nine days. The Gudahandi hills nearby have prehistoric rock paintings.

### Thuamul Rampur

The high plateau of Thuamul Rampur, in the south of the district, is surrounded by waterfalls and gorges and is the source area of the Indravati. Odisha Tourism has described it as Kalahandi's best-kept secret.

## Culture and festivals

**Language.** About 95% of people speak Odia as their first language, much of it in the regional variety known as **Kalahandia**.

**Dance and music.** **[Ghumura](/culture/ghumura-dance)** is the signature folk dance of Kalahandi, performed with a drum made from an earthen pot. Once associated with war music, it was later patronised by the courts of Bhawanipatna and Jayapatna.

**Festivals.** The **Chhatar Jatra** of the Manikeswari temple is the district's best-known festival. Like the rest of western Odisha, Kalahandi celebrates **[Nuakhai](/culture/nuakhai)**, the festival of the new rice. At Lanjigarh, the **Jhami Yatra**, a fire-walking festival, is held in the month of Chaitra (March–April).

**Communities.** Adivasi communities include the Kondh, among them the Dongria Kondh of the Niyamgiri hills.

## Food

Kalahandi's food is based on rice, now the district's leading crop, with finger millet (*mandia*) eaten in porridge and cakes. The new rice of the season is first eaten at Nuakhai.

## Economy

**Agriculture.** Rice is the main crop. ThePrint reported in 2024 that cotton output had also grown many times over since 2000, and that more than 90% of villages now have all-weather roads. Forest produce such as mahua flowers and kendu leaves remains important.

**Irrigation and power.** The **Upper Indravati** project at Mukhiguda provides hydropower and irrigation and is credited with the district's agricultural turnaround. The last maharaja, Pratap Keshari Deo, had championed the project.

**Minerals and industry.** Kalahandi has deposits of bauxite and graphite and historical sources of gemstones. Vedanta operates an alumina refinery at **Lanjigarh**.

**Education.** Kalahandi University in Bhawanipatna grew out of Kalahandi Science College, founded in 1960.

## Notable people

- **Pratap Keshari Deo** (1919–2001): last ruler of Kalahandi (1939–1947) and Lok Sabha member for Kalahandi (1957–1980).
- **Bhubaneswar Behera** (1916–2001): engineer, educationist and Odia writer, born at Kashibahal; Principal of REC Rourkela and Vice-Chancellor of Sambalpur University.
- **Kishen Pattnaik** (born 1930): socialist thinker and writer, born in Bhawanipatna; Lok Sabha member (1962–1967) who raised the issue of starvation in Kalahandi.
