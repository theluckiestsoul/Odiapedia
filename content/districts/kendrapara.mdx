## Overview

**Kendrapara** (କେନ୍ଦ୍ରାପଡ଼ା) is a coastal district in the delta of the Brahmani, Baitarani and Mahanadi rivers in eastern Odisha, with the Bay of Bengal on its east. Its headquarters is the town of **Kendrapara**, home to the temple of **Baladevjew** (Balabhadra), which is why the town is also called **Tulasi Kshetra**. The district is best known for the mangroves and saltwater crocodiles of **Bhitarkanika** and for the olive ridley turtle beaches of **Gahirmatha**.

Kendrapara district was carved out of [Cuttack district](/district/cuttack) on **1 April 1993**. It forms a single sub-division with nine tehsils and blocks: Aul, Derabish, Garadpur, Kendrapara, Mahakalapada, Marshaghai, Pattamundai, Rajkanika and Rajanagar.

The district covers 2,644 sq km and had 1,440,361 people at the 2011 Census. Only about 6% lived in towns, and literacy stood at 85.15%. Odia is spoken by about 91% of residents, Bengali by about 5% and Urdu by about 3%. The district's five Assembly seats all belong to the Kendrapara Lok Sabha constituency, which also includes two seats in Cuttack district.

## Geography and climate

Kendrapara is flat, fertile delta land crossed by many rivers and creeks. It borders Bhadrak to the north, Jajpur to the north-west, Cuttack to the west, Jagatsinghpur to the south and the Bay of Bengal to the east. According to Wikipedia, its coastline runs for about 48 km, from Dhamra Muhan in the north to Batighar in the south.

The Ministry of MSME's district profile lists the **Birupa, Brahmani, Luna, Paika, Chitrotpala and Gobari** among the district's rivers, along with many tributaries and canals. In the north-east the Brahmani and Baitarani reach the sea through a maze of tidal creeks lined with mangroves, the heart of Bhitarkanika.

Two major protected areas lie on the coast:

| Protected area | Key facts |
|---|---|
| Bhitarkanika Wildlife Sanctuary | Declared 22 April 1975; about 672 sq km of mangroves and wetland |
| Bhitarkanika National Park | Declared 16 September 1998; about 145 sq km, the core of the sanctuary |
| Bhitarkanika Mangroves (Ramsar site) | Designated 19 August 2002 |
| Gahirmatha Marine Sanctuary | Notified 27 September 1997; about 1,435 sq km, mostly sea |

The coast is low and exposed to cyclones and sea erosion. The **super cyclone of 29 October 1999** devastated coastal Kendrapara as well as neighbouring Jagatsinghpur. Erosion has swallowed most of **Satabhaya**, once a group of seven villages: Down To Earth reported in 2024 that six had been lost to the sea. In 2018 the administration moved 571 families to a rehabilitation colony at Bagapatia, about 12 km inland. The climate is hot and humid, with heavy monsoon rain from June to September and mild winters.

## History

According to the district administration, the name Kendrapara is linked to the legendary figure Kandarasura. The coast had a long maritime past. The administration notes that some historians identify a port of Ptolemy's time with Mayuri gaan (formerly Maipur) in the old Kanika estate. Places such as Pattamundai and Chandol appear in old records as trading centres (see [Odisha's maritime history](/history/odisha-maritime-history)).

In the medieval and early modern periods the area was divided among local estates. The district administration names three main ones: **Kujang** (Sandha family), **Aul** (Dev family) and **Kanika** (Bhanja family). According to Wikipedia, the original Baladevjew temple was destroyed in 1661 by a Mughal official under Aurangzeb. The present temple was built in 1761, during Maratha rule, by the Raja of Kujang, Gopal Sandha, and the zamindar of Chhedara, Srinivas Narendra Mahapatra.

After the British conquest of Odisha in 1803, the area became part of Cuttack district. The **Kendrapara municipality**, established on 10 March 1869, is described by the administration as the oldest in Odisha. Schools opened from 1844, and the Frazer Girls' School followed in 1893.

In the freedom movement, local leaders such as **Jadumani Mangaraj** and **Narayan Birabar Samanta** were prominent, and the district administration records that Mahatma Gandhi made a padayatra (walking tour) through Kendrapara. After independence, [Biju Patnaik](/people/biju-patnaik) represented the Kendrapara Lok Sabha seat from 1977 to 1985. Kendrapara became a separate district in 1993.

## Places to visit

### Baladevjew Temple

The temple of **Baladevjew** (Balabhadra, elder brother of Jagannath) at Ichhapur in Kendrapara town is the district's most important shrine. Balabhadra is worshipped as the main deity, with Jagannath and Subhadra beside him, and a separate shrine is dedicated to Tulasi. The temple's Ratha Yatra in the month of Ashadha (June–July) is famous for Baladevjew's large chariot, the **Brahma Taladhwaja**. The sweet **Rasabali** is one of its best-known offerings.

### Bhitarkanika National Park

Bhitarkanika's tidal creeks and mangrove forests are one of India's main strongholds of the **saltwater crocodile**. The January 2024 census counted 1,811 crocodiles, up from 1,192 in 2000, as reported by Down To Earth. The park is also rich in birds; Wikipedia records about 320 species. Visitors explore it by boat from Khola (reached via Rajnagar) or from Chandbali in Bhadrak district. The park is closed every year from 1 May to 31 July for the breeding season. See the [Bhitarkanika travel guide](/travel/bhitarkanika-national-park).

### Gahirmatha

The beaches of Gahirmatha, which separate the Bhitarkanika mangroves from the sea, are one of the world's most important mass-nesting sites for **olive ridley turtles**. The beach is inside a protected marine sanctuary, so check with the forest department before planning a visit.

### Pentha and the eroding coast

OrissaPOST reported in 2023 that Pentha beach was the second-worst eroded beach in Odisha after Satabhaya. Geosynthetic tubes have been laid there to slow the sea, and the site shows clearly how a changing shoreline affects coastal villages.

### Aul

Aul was the seat of the old Aul estate. The district administration notes the Barahajew (Varaha) temple there, and the Ministry of MSME lists Aul among the district's main tourist places.

## Culture and festivals

Kendrapara's religious life centres on Baladevjew. The district celebrates the [Ratha Yatra](/culture/rath-yatra) on a grand scale, and the town's identity as Tulasi Kshetra comes from the local tradition that Tulasi is Balabhadra's consort. Coastal festivals such as [Boita Bandana](/culture/boita-bandana), [Raja Parba](/culture/raja-parba) and [Durga Puja](/culture/durga-puja) are widely observed.

According to the district administration, the musician **Gokul Srichandan** gathered well-known musicians at Bada Kotha and promoted Odia song, and he made a recording of an Odia song in 1899. The Odia writer **Binapani Mohanty**, winner of the Sahitya Akademi Award, came from a family of Chandol near Kendrapara.

Local crafts listed in the Ministry of MSME's district profile include **golden grass** work, bamboo craft and terracotta.

## Food

- **[Rasabali](/food/rasabali):** fried discs of chhena (fresh cheese) soaked in thickened, sweetened milk. It is offered as *bhoga* at the Baladevjew temple, and Kendrapara Rasabali received a Geographical Indication in October 2023 (see [Odisha GI tags](/culture/odisha-gi-tags)).

## Economy

Kendrapara's economy rests on **agriculture and fishing**. The Ministry of MSME's profile records no large or medium industries in the district. It counted 961 registered small units, led by food-processing businesses such as rice mills, and eight artisan clusters. Fishing and crab catching support many coastal villages.

Tourism to Bhitarkanika and the Baladevjew temple brings visitors, especially between November and February. Sea erosion and cyclones are continuing challenges for coastal livelihoods, and they have led to the relocation of whole villages.

## Notable people

- **[Biju Patnaik](/people/biju-patnaik)**: former Chief Minister of Odisha, Member of Parliament for Kendrapara from 1977 to 1985.
- **Jadumani Mangaraj** and **Narayan Birabar Samanta**: leaders of the freedom movement in Kendrapara, according to the district administration.
- **Gokul Srichandan**: musician who promoted Odia song in the late 19th century, according to the district administration.
- **Binapani Mohanty** (1936–2022): Odia fiction writer, Sahitya Akademi Award (1990) and Padma Shri (2020), whose family came from Chandol.

## How to reach

- **By air:** The nearest airport is Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, about two and a half hours by road from Kendrapara. The Kendrapara district site gives about 155 km from Bhubaneswar to Bhitarkanika.
- **By rail:** The usual railheads are Cuttack, about 54 km from Kendrapara town, and Bhadrak, about 55 km from Chandbali for the northern entry to Bhitarkanika.
- **By road:** Kendrapara is linked to Cuttack by State Highway 9A and to NH-16 towards Bhubaneswar. Rajnagar and the Khola gate of Bhitarkanika are reached by road from Kendrapara, and Khola is about 30 km from Rajnagar.

For neighbouring districts, see [Jagatsinghpur](/district/jagatsinghpur), [Cuttack](/district/cuttack), [Jajpur](/district/jajpur) and [Bhadrak](/district/bhadrak).
