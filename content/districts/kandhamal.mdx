## Overview

**Kandhamal** (କନ୍ଧମାଳ) is a hill district in central Odisha. It is known for forested ranges, waterfalls, the cool uplands of Daringbadi and the culture of the **Kandha** (Kondh) people, from whom it takes its name. The headquarters is **Phulbani**, a small town on the Salunki river.

Kandhamal was created on **1 January 1994**, when the old Phulbani district was divided into Kandhamal and [Boudh](/district/boudh). It has two sub-divisions, **Phulbani** and **Baliguda**, and 12 blocks: Baliguda, Chakapada, Daringbadi, G. Udayagiri, Khajuripada, Kotagarh, Nuagaon, Phiringia, Phulbani, Raikia, Tikabali and Tumudibandha.

The district covers 8,021 sq km, the sixth-largest area in Odisha. However, it had only 733,110 people at the 2011 Census, and fewer than one in ten lived in towns. Scheduled Tribes made up 53.58% of the population and Scheduled Castes 15.76%. About 53% of residents named Odia as their first language and about 46% Kui, the language of the Kandhas.

Kandhamal is famous across India for **Kandhamal Haladi**, an organically grown turmeric that received a Geographical Indication tag in 2019. It is also one of the state's least developed districts and ranked 29th of 30 districts on Odisha's Human Development Index.

## Geography and climate

Kandhamal lies on a broken plateau in the Eastern Ghats, with ranges, deep valleys and narrow river basins. Altitudes range from about 300 m to 1,100 m. Phulbani stands at about 485 m, and Daringbadi at about 915 m. The district borders Boudh, Nayagarh, Ganjam, Gajapati, Rayagada and Kalahandi.

Forests cover about 5,709 sq km, roughly 71% of the district. Sal is the main tree, along with species such as bija, asan and sandalwood. The forests yield bamboo, kendu leaf, tamarind, mahua flowers and sal seeds.

The **Salunki** and **Pilasalunki** rivers flow near Phulbani. The **Rushikulya** rises in the Rushimala hills near Daringbadi and flows south-east into Ganjam. Other streams include the Bhrutanga. Waterfalls are common: Putudi (about 18 km from Phulbani), Urmagarh (about 21 km) and Katramal (about 31 km) are among the best known.

The **Kotagarh Wildlife Sanctuary** (399.05 sq km, notified in 1981) protects elephants, gaur, sambar, leopards and sloth bears, and is noted for the four-horned antelope (*chousingha*).

Daringbadi and nearby uplands are among the coldest places in Odisha in December and January, and frost is common on winter mornings.

## History

According to the district administration, in the Mauryan period (3rd century BCE) this hill country was part of the unconquered *Atavika* (forest) lands. In later centuries the surrounding plains were held by the principalities of Boudh, Ghumusar and Khemundi, while the hill Kandhas kept a large measure of independence under their own chiefs. The district administration notes that the Bhanjas held the area until 1835 and that the British gradually brought it under control between about 1830 and 1880.

The Kandha hills of Ghumusar and Boudh drew British attention in the 1830s and 1840s. After the fall of the Ghumusar Bhanjas in 1835–1836, Kandha leaders resisted Company rule. Officers such as Samuel Charters Macpherson were then posted to the hill tracts. In November 1845 Macpherson was made the government's agent for suppressing the **meriah**, the human sacrifice once practised by some Kandha groups. Kandha resistance in the hills continued for some years afterwards.

According to the district administration, the Kandhamal area was part of Boudh until 1855, after which British administration separated it from Boudh. The old Phulbani district was later divided, and **Kandhamal district** came into being on 1 January 1994.

In 2008 the district suffered serious communal violence, in which at least 39 people were killed and thousands of homes were damaged or destroyed.

## Places to visit

### Daringbadi

Daringbadi, at about 915 m, is often called the "Kashmir of Odisha" for its cool winters, pine plantations and coffee gardens. Odisha Tourism lists a coffee garden about 3 km from the town, Midubanda waterfall about 40 km away and the Mandasaru valley about 35 km away. See the [Daringbadi travel guide](/travel/daringbadi).

### Belghar

Belghar is a high, forested area of the district with a nature camp, grasslands and streams. It is home to the **Kutia Kandha**, a Kandha group that still practises shifting cultivation. Visitors should respect village customs and seek permission before photographing people.

### Phulbani and nearby waterfalls

Phulbani is a quiet hill town and a base for the Putudi, Urmagarh and Katramal waterfalls and the Pilasalunki reservoir. Putudi, about 18 km away, is the most visited of these.

### Balaskumpa

The shrine of Goddess **Barala Devi** at Balaskumpa, near Phulbani, is one of the district's main temples. Its biggest festival is held at Dussehra.

### Kotagarh Wildlife Sanctuary

Kotagarh, with Baliguda as its nearest town, is one of Odisha's less-visited sanctuaries. It is known for the four-horned antelope and for forest waterfalls such as Ludu.

## Culture and festivals

The **Kandha** (Kondh) are the largest community in Kandhamal. Many Kandhas speak **Kui**, a Dravidian language; Britannica notes that Kandhas in the plains increasingly speak Odia, while Kui remains strong in the remote hills. Most Kandhas are now rice farmers. Some groups, such as the Kutia Kandha, keep their traditional shifting (swidden) cultivation. Tribal festivals mentioned in accounts of the district include Sisaa Laka, Maranga Laka, Bora Laka and Kedu Laka.

[Danda Nacha](/culture/danda-nacha), the Chaitra ritual devoted to Shiva, is performed for 13 days in parts of Kandhamal. Hindu festivals such as Dussehra, Holi, Diwali, Shivaratri, Ram Navami and Janmashtami are widely observed. The Odia spoken around Phulbani, sometimes called Phulbani Odia, borrows words from Kui and from neighbouring dialects (see [Odia dialects](/language/odia-dialects)).

According to the Ministry of MSME's district profile, local crafts include a terracotta cluster at Ganjuguda and leaf-plate making at Baliguda. The district's registered Geographical Indication is **Kandhamal Haladi** (see [Odisha GI tags](/culture/odisha-gi-tags)).

## Food

Rice is the staple food and main crop. The district's signature ingredient is **Kandhamal Haladi**, a turmeric grown mainly by Kandha farmers using traditional methods without chemical fertilisers. It is used in everyday Odia cooking and is sold across India and abroad.

## Economy

Agriculture and forest produce support most households. Rice is the main crop, and the uplands are used for dry crops, horticulture, sericulture and floriculture.

**Turmeric** is the most important cash crop. The Kandhamal Apex Spices Association for Marketing (KASAM), set up in 2000, applied for the GI tag, which was granted on 1 April 2019. It was Odisha's first turmeric GI. At that time ANI reported that turmeric was grown on about 13,000 hectares and that the district produced about 25,000 tonnes of dry turmeric a year. KASAM fixed a benchmark price for farmers to reduce dependence on middlemen.

Other activities include collection of minor forest produce, a turmeric and spices processing cluster at Phulbani, small agro-based industries and some graphite mining. The district has no railway line, which limits industrial growth.

## How to reach

- **By air:** The nearest major airport is Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, about 211 km from Phulbani by road. There is also an airstrip at Gudari, about 5 km from Phulbani.
- **By rail:** There is no railway in the district. The usual railheads are Brahmapur (Berhampur), about 165 km from Phulbani and roughly 120–150 km from Daringbadi depending on the route, and Rairakhol, about 100 km from Phulbani.
- **By road:** Buses connect Phulbani with Bhubaneswar, Brahmapur, Cuttack and Boudh. Daringbadi is usually reached by road from Brahmapur.
