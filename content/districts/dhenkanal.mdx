**Dhenkanal** (ଢେଙ୍କାନାଳ) is a district in central Odisha with its headquarters at Dhenkanal town. Dhenkanal district is known for the hilltop Shiva temple of **Kapilas**, **Joranda**, the main seat of the Mahima Dharma faith, its **Dhokra** metal craft and the GI-tagged sweet **Magji**. It is also remembered as the home of the boy martyr **Baji Rout**.

## Overview

Dhenkanal lies in the Brahmani valley between the coastal plain and the central Odisha uplands. It covers 4,452 sq km and had a population of 1,192,811 in the 2011 Census, with literacy of 78.76 percent. The area's population has roughly quadrupled since 1901, when it stood at 292,497. Dhenkanal town had 67,414 residents in 2011. About 10 percent of the people lived in towns.

The district grew out of the princely state of Dhenkanal, which merged with India on **1 January 1948**. The old state covered much of present-day Angul district as well. Angul and the neighbouring former states of Talcher, Athmallik and Pallahara remained part of Dhenkanal district until **1 April 1993**, when Angul became a separate district.

According to the MSME district profile, Dhenkanal has three sub-divisions and eight blocks.

## Geography and climate

Dhenkanal is bordered by Kendujhar and Angul to the north, Jajpur to the east, Cuttack to the south and Angul to the west. The **Brahmani** is the main river; the Nilakanthapur ghat, where Baji Rout was killed, is on its bank.

The landscape is a mix of farmland and low, forested hills, including the Kapilas range north-east of the town. The MSME profile records about 1,738 sq km of forest, which supplies kendu leaves, bamboo and timber.

## History

According to the district administration, the name Dhenkanal is commonly traced to **Dhenka**, a Savara chief who once ruled the area. Little is known of the earliest period. The district administration links the region in turn to the Satavahanas (2nd century CE), the Guptas (3rd–4th century), the **Bhaumakaras** (6th–7th century) and the Sulki dynasty (until the 9th century).

The **Kapilas temple** was built in **1246 CE** by the Eastern Ganga king Narasimha Deva I, according to an inscription at the temple.

The princely line of Dhenkanal began in **1530**, when **Hari Singh Vidyadhar**, a commander of the Gajapati king, took control of the area. Hari Singh Vidyadhar ruled until 1594, and the district administration counts eighteen kings in the line that followed him over about four centuries. The last ruler was Shankar Pratap Singh Dev Mahendra, who reigned from 1918.

In the 19th century, **Mahima Gosain**, founder of the Mahima Dharma movement, made Joranda his base. According to Wikipedia, he entered *samadhi* there in 1876. Joranda has remained the faith's main seat (*gadi*) ever since.

In 1938 the **Dhenkanal Praja Mandal**, a people's movement led by Harmohan Patnaik and others, rose against heavy taxes and forced labour under the ruler. During this agitation, on the night of 10–11 October 1938, police shot dead 12-year-old **Baji Rout** at the Nilakanthapur ghat on the Brahmani after he refused to ferry them across. Others were killed with him. See [Baji Rout](/people/baji-rout).

The Odia poet Sachi Routray wrote the poem *Baji Rout* in 1938, and the boy's death became one of the best-remembered episodes of the Praja Mandal struggle.

In 1892 the princely state covered about 3,789 sq km, according to Wikipedia. Dhenkanal State merged with India on 1 January 1948. Angul was separated in 1993, giving the district its present shape.

## Places to visit

### Kapilas

Kapilas hill, about 26 km from Dhenkanal town, is topped by the temple of **Chandrasekhar** (Shiva), built in 1246 CE. Pilgrims climb **1,352 steps** or take the road. Sources differ on the height: the district administration gives the peak as 457 m, while other accounts put the temple at about 2,239 ft. The older Vishwanath shrine, known as Budha Linga, stands nearby, and tradition identifies the hill as the hermitage of the sage Kapila. Some regard it as a second *Kailash* of Shiva. The main tower is about 60 ft high, and two sacred tanks, Payamrta Kunda and Marichi Kunda, lie within the temple grounds. A science park run near the hill offers outdoor exhibits.

### Joranda

Joranda, about 24 km north of Dhenkanal town where the villages of Joranda, Natima and Patna meet, is the main seat of **Mahima Dharma** and the samadhi place of Mahima Gosain. Followers worship a formless supreme being, and there are no idols. The annual **Joranda Mela** (Magha Mela) around Magha Purnima draws ascetics and devotees from Odisha and neighbouring states. The poet-saint [Bhima Bhoi](/people/bhima-bhoi) was the movement's best-known voice.

### Saptasajya

A hill shrine about 11 km from Dhenkanal, with a **Raghunath** (Rama) temple complex. A motor road has reached the village since 1982, and shrines to Annapurna, Kali and Surya Narayan were added between 1982 and 1990. Local tradition says the Pandavas spent part of their exile here. Other accounts explain the name as the seven hills, or the hermitages of the seven sages.

### Dhenkanal Palace

The former royal palace reflects the history of the princely state and is listed by Odisha Tourism among the district's main sights.

### Other sites

- **Kualo**, about 72 km from Dhenkanal, has a group of temples dedicated to Kanakeswar.
- **Saranga**, about 67 km away, has an ancient sculpture of Vishnu carved on the rocky bed of a river.
- **Dandadhar**, about 67 km away, is an irrigation dam on the Ramial river, popular for outings.
- The **science centre** at Dhenkanal is one of the units of the National Council of Science Museums.

## Culture and festivals

The **Joranda Mela** is the district's best-known gathering, when Mahima Dharma ascetics and lay followers gather at the Mahima Gadi and sacred fires are lit.

Dhenkanal is one of Odisha's main centres of [Dhokra](/culture/dhokra-craft) lost-wax brass casting. OrissaPOST reported in 2022 that about 120 artisans worked in the craft at **Nabajibanpur** in Sadeibereni, under Saptasajya panchayat. A crafts village built there in 2019 for about Rs 5 crore was waiting to open. The MSME profile also lists brass and bell-metal utensil units. Dhokra from Odisha does not hold a GI tag.

Odia is spoken by about 96 percent of people (2011). Small Munda, Santal and Juang communities also live in the district.

## Food

- **Dhenkanal Magji**: a sweet made from buffalo-milk *chhena* (cottage cheese), registered as a Geographical Indication in 2024. See [GI tags of Odisha](/culture/odisha-gi-tags).
- Odisha Tourism's Dhenkanal guide also mentions **Dhenkanal bara** and *ghuguni*, alongside everyday [pakhala bhata](/food/pakhala-bhata) and [dalma](/food/dalma).

## Economy

Agriculture and forest produce support most rural households. The MSME profile (2011–12) records small-scale working of **chromite**, quartz and fire clay. Its list of large units includes Bhushan Steel at **Meramandali**, Navabharat Ferro Alloys and Shakti Sugars. The Meramandali steel plant, set up by Bhushan Steel, passed to Tata Steel in 2018 and is now run as Tata Steel's Meramandali works. Small enterprises include engineering units, fly-ash brick makers, brass and bell-metal workshops, and a garment cluster at Baulapur.

Dhenkanal is also home to a campus of the **Indian Institute of Mass Communication**, which Wikipedia describes as the only institute in Odisha for the study of journalism and mass communication.

## Notable people

- **Baji Rout** (1926–1938), boatman of Nilakanthapur, killed by police in 1938 and widely remembered as India's youngest martyr. See [Baji Rout](/people/baji-rout).
- **Sarangadhar Das** (1886–1957), born in Dhenkanal State, general secretary of the Orissa States People's Conference (1937–1946), member of the Constituent Assembly and of the first Lok Sabha.
- **Kalpana Dash** (1966–2019), born in Dhenkanal, the first Odia climber to reach the summit of Mount Everest (21 May 2008). She died on Everest in May 2019 while descending after a second summit.

## How to reach

- **By air:** Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, about 80 km away, according to Odisha Tourism.
- **By rail:** Dhenkanal station is on the **Cuttack–Sambalpur line**, with direct trains to Cuttack, Bhubaneswar and western Odisha.
- **By road:** Highways link Dhenkanal with Cuttack and Bhubaneswar to the south-east and Angul to the west, with frequent buses.

Odisha Tourism suggests one to two days for the district and October to March as the best season. See also [how to reach Odisha](/travel/how-to-reach-odisha).
