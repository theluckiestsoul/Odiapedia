## Overview

**Ganjam** (ଗଞ୍ଜାମ) is a coastal district in southern Odisha, lying along the Bay of Bengal between Chilika Lake in the north and the Andhra Pradesh border in the south. It is the most populous district of Odisha, with 3,529,031 people at the 2011 Census, and its administrative headquarters is **Chhatrapur**. Its largest city, **Brahmapur** (Berhampur), is the commercial centre of southern Odisha and is known as the "Silk City" for its Berhampur Patta sarees.

According to the district administration, Ganjam takes its name from the old township and European fort of Ganjam on the northern bank of the Rushikulya river. Other derivations have also been suggested, including the ancient name Kongoda and the Persian word *ganj*, meaning a granary or market.

Ganjam district came into existence on **1 April 1936**, when it was separated from the Madras Presidency and joined to the new province of Orissa. It is one of the oldest administrative units of the state. On 2 October 1992 its south-western part was carved out to form [Gajapati district](/district/gajapati).

The district covers 8,206 sq km by the Census figure (the district administration gives 8,070.6 sq km). It has three sub-divisions, Chhatrapur, Brahmapur and Bhanjanagar. Odia is the main language, and Telugu is spoken by about 7% of residents as a first language, reflecting the long border with Andhra Pradesh. The local speech, Ganjami, is one of the main [Odia dialects](/language/odia-dialects).

## Geography and climate

Ganjam has two broad landscapes. In the east is a fertile coastal plain, and in the west are hills and tablelands of the Eastern Ghats. The district borders Khordha and Nayagarh to the north, Kandhamal to the north-west, Gajapati to the south-west, Andhra Pradesh to the south and the Bay of Bengal to the east.

The main river is the **Rushikulya**. It rises in the Rushimala hills near Daringbadi in Kandhamal, flows about 165 km through Kandhamal and Ganjam, and meets the sea at Puruna Bandha without forming a delta. Its tributaries include the Baghua, Dhanei and Badanadi. The southern part of [Chilika Lake](/history/chilika-lake), around Rambha, lies within Ganjam.

The coast is one of India's major nesting grounds for **olive ridley sea turtles**. Large-scale nesting near the Rushikulya mouth was first recorded in 1993. In February 2025 Down To Earth reported that 698,718 turtles nested there over eight days, a record for the site.

## History

Ganjam formed part of ancient **Dakshina Kalinga** (southern Kalinga). After the [Kalinga War](/history/kalinga-war-en) of about 261 BCE, the Mauryan emperor Ashoka had his edicts inscribed at **Jaugada** on the Rushikulya, about 35 km north-west of Brahmapur. Jaugada was a fortified Mauryan provincial centre. Its rock carries Major Rock Edicts 1 to 10 and 14, plus the two Separate Edicts addressed to the people of Kalinga, dated to about 250 BCE. The Archaeological Survey of India excavated the site in 1956.

By the 7th century CE the region was known as **Kongoda**. It later came under the [Eastern Gangas](/history/eastern-ganga-dynasty) and the Gajapati kings of Odisha, and many small hill and coastal estates grew up under local chiefs. Among them was the Bhanja house of Ghumusar, into which the poet [Upendra Bhanja](/people/upendra-bhanja) was born.

In the 18th century the French commander Bussy briefly held the area (about 1757–1759) before the English defeated the French in 1759. The district collector's office dates from 1794. Ganjam was then governed as part of the **Madras Presidency**. In the 1830s the Ghumusar hill tracts saw armed resistance to Company rule. After the death of the Ghumusar ruler Dhananjaya Bhanja in 1835, the Kandha leader **Dora Bisoi** led the struggle until his capture in 1837. In the following years British officers such as Samuel Charters Macpherson worked in these hills to suppress the meriah sacrifice.

The movement for a separate Odia province drew strong support from Ganjam's Odia-speaking population. When Orissa province was formed on 1 April 1936 (now marked as [Utkal Divas](/culture/utkal-divas)), most of Ganjam was transferred to it.

## Places to visit

### Gopalpur-on-Sea

Gopalpur is a small seaside town about 16 km from Brahmapur. It was once a busy port. Today it is known for a quiet, east-facing beach, casuarina groves, an old lighthouse and colonial-era buildings. Odisha Tourism advises against swimming because the sea is rough. See the [Gopalpur-on-Sea travel guide](/travel/gopalpur-on-sea).

### Tara Tarini Temple

The twin goddesses Tara and Tarini are worshipped on the Purnagiri (Kumari) hills above the Rushikulya, about 30 km from Brahmapur. The shrine is revered as one of the four Adi Shakti Pithas. Odisha Tourism notes that it can be reached by road, by ropeway or by climbing 999 steps. The largest crowds come on the Tuesdays of the month of Chaitra (March–April).

### Rushikulya rookery

The beaches near the mouth of the Rushikulya are a mass-nesting site (*arribada*) for olive ridley turtles, usually in February–March. Nesting stretches are protected by the forest department, so visitors should follow local rules.

### Jaugada

The ruined Mauryan fort at Jaugada, near Purushottampur, holds one of Ashoka's two sets of Kalinga edicts in Odisha; the other is at Dhauli near Bhubaneswar.

### Taptapani

Taptapani is a sulphurous hot spring in the forested hills of western Ganjam, near the Gajapati border. Visitors and pilgrims bathe in the warm water before praying at the nearby Kandimata shrine. The water is traditionally believed to have healing properties. Odisha Tourism runs a guest house (Panthanivas) here.

### Biranchi Narayan Temple, Buguda

This Sun temple at Buguda is often called the "Wooden Konark" because its deity is carved in wood. According to Wikipedia, the temple faces west rather than east and was renovated in 1790 under the Bhanja king Srikara Bhanja. The district administration lists it among Ganjam's main attractions.

### Rambha and southern Chilika

Rambha, on the southern shore of Chilika, is a quieter base for boat trips to the lake's southern islands. See the [Chilika Lake travel guide](/travel/chilika-lake-travel-guide).

## Culture and festivals

Ganjam has a lively folk culture. **[Danda Nacha](/culture/danda-nacha)**, the penitential dance-ritual of the Chaitra month devoted mainly to Shiva, is strongest in Ganjam. It ends on Maha Vishuva Sankranti, known locally as Meru Sankranti (see [Pana Sankranti](/culture/pana-sankranti)).

In Brahmapur, the biennial **Thakurani Jatra** of Goddess Budhi Thakurani is the city's biggest festival. Historians trace the cult to around 1672, when the town grew up with the Dera weaver community. The Odisha government lists it among the state's official festivals. *Bagha Nacha* (the tiger dance) is a highlight of the festival.

**Prahallada Nataka** is a traditional musical play from Ganjam's former zamindari courts. Its best-known text was written in the mid-19th century under Raja Ramakrushna Chhotaraya of Jalantara, largely by the poet-musician Gourahari Parichha. A full performance can last all night. Other festivals listed by the district administration include Dola Yatra, the Tara Tarini Mela, Durga Puja, Ratha Yatra and Makar Sankranti.

Ganjam's crafts and products include four registered Geographical Indications (see [Odisha GI tags](/culture/odisha-gi-tags)):

- **Berhampur Patta (Phoda Kumbha) saree and joda**, silk weaves with temple-pattern borders made by Brahmapur's weavers.
- **[Bomkai saree](/culture/bomkai-saree)**, named after Bomkai village in Ganjam and also woven in Subarnapur.
- **Ganjam Kewda flower** and **Ganjam Kewda rooh**, the screwpine flower and its distilled essence, used in perfumery and flavouring.

## Food

Brahmapur is sometimes called the "food capital" of Odisha for its busy street-food scene. Its wholesale mango market is one of the largest in India. Coastal towns such as Gopalpur are known for fresh seafood. Kewda water distilled in Ganjam is used to scent sweets and drinks across India. Cashew processing is a local industry, centred on a cluster at Rambha.

## Economy

Agriculture is the main occupation. The district administration lists paddy, groundnut, sugarcane, oilseeds, ragi, mung and biri among the major crops. According to the district administration:

- About 150 Kewda essence units employ some 3,000 workers, and the Ganjam Kewra Development Trust holds the GI registration.
- About 76 cashew-processing units form the Jagannath Cashew Cluster, headquartered at Rambha.
- Granite cutting and polishing units work in the Kukudakhandi, Digapahandi and Rangeilunda blocks.
- The district also has rice mills, an Ayurvedic medicine cluster, coir training at Golabandha and salt production around Huma.

The beach sands near Chhatrapur are mined by **IREL (India) Limited** at its Odisha Sands Complex (OSCOM), next to Gopalpur port. The plant produces ilmenite, rutile, zircon, sillimanite and garnet. Gopalpur also has a deep-water port. Fisheries and tourism support coastal villages, while Brahmapur is a regional hub for trade, education (Berhampur University) and health care.

## Notable people

- **[Upendra Bhanja](/people/upendra-bhanja)**: celebrated poet of the late 17th and early 18th centuries, known by the later title *Kabi Samrat*. He was born at Kulada in the Ghumusar estate, and the town of Bhanjanagar is named after him.
- **V. V. Giri**: fourth President of India, born in Berhampur on 10 August 1894.
- **Sanjukta Panigrahi**: Odissi dancer, born in Berhampur in 1944 and awarded the Padma Shri in 1975.
- **[Biju Patnaik](/people/biju-patnaik)**: the former Chief Minister was born in Cuttack, but his parents lived at Ghumusar Nuagam near Bellaguntha in Ganjam. He represented Aska in the Lok Sabha from 1996 to 1997.

## How to reach

- **By air:** The nearest major airport is Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar. Brahmapur is roughly 170 km away by road, and Odisha Tourism gives Gopalpur as about 174 km from Bhubaneswar. A small airstrip at Rangeilunda near Brahmapur has had limited regional flights to Bhubaneswar since March 2023; check current services before planning.
- **By rail:** Brahmapur (Berhampur) station, on the Howrah–Chennai main line, is the main railhead. Stations such as Chhatrapur and Rambha are on the same line.
- **By road:** NH-16 runs along the coast through the district. NH-59 links Brahmapur with the interior towards Aska and Bhanjanagar, and NH-516 also serves the region. Regular buses connect Brahmapur with Bhubaneswar, Cuttack and southern Odisha.
