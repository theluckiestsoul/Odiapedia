## At a Glance
According to the district administration, Ganjam takes its name from the old township and European fort of Ganjam on the northern bank of the Rushikulya; other derivations (from Kongoda, or the Persian *ganj*, "granary" or "market") have also been suggested. It is a culturally vibrant district, famous for its folk dances like Bagha Nacha and Jodi Sankha. Berhampur, the "Silk City," is the commercial heart of the district, renowned for its **Berhampur Patta** sarees and intricate jewelry.

## The Heritage Diary
Ganjam was part of the ancient Kalinga empire. It came briefly under the French commander Bussy in the 18th century and later under the British as part of the Madras Presidency. The district has a unique mix of Odia and Telugu cultures due to its proximity to Andhra Pradesh. It has been the birthplace of many poets, including Upendra Bhanja, the "Kabi Samrat."

## Local Landmarks
- **Tara Tarini:** A major Shakti Peetha located on a hilltop on the banks of the Rushikulya river. It can be reached by a long flight of steps, by ropeway or by road.
- **Gopalpur-on-Sea:** A popular beach resort with a historic lighthouse and colonial-era ruins.
- **Taptapani:** Famous for its perennial hot sulfur spring, believed to have medicinal properties.
- **Chilika Lake (Rambha):** The southern sector of Chilika Lake, dotted with islands like Breakfast Island and Honeymoon Island.
- **Jaugarh:** Famous for the Ashokan rock edicts, proving the region's importance in ancient times.

## Taste of the Land
- **Achar & Papad:** Pickles and papads are popular local products.
- **Mutton Curry:** The local style of mutton preparation is popular.

## Local Heroes
- **Kabi Samrat Upendra Bhanja:** The emperor of Odia poetry.
- **Biju Patnaik:** Though born in Cuttack, his parents lived at Ghumusar Nuagam near Bellaguntha in Ganjam; he later represented Aska in the Lok Sabha (1996–1997).
- **V.V. Giri:** Former President of India, born in Berhampur.
