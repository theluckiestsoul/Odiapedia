## Overview

**Puri** (ପୁରୀ) is a coastal district in eastern Odisha, named after the pilgrim town of Puri. Puri is the seat of the **Shree Jagannath Temple** and one of the four Char Dham pilgrimage centres of Hinduism. The district headquarters is Puri town.

Beyond Puri town the district holds the **Konark Sun Temple**, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, along with a long sandy coastline and the eastern part of **Chilika Lake** around Satapada. It also has crafts villages such as **Raghurajpur** and **Pipili**, whose painters and appliqué workers have long served the Jagannath cult.

The district in its present form dates from the reorganisation of 1993. The old Puri district was then divided into Puri, [Khordha](/district/khordha) and [Nayagarh](/district/nayagarh), and the present Puri district kept the old Puri Sadar sub-division. Today it has one sub-division, 11 tahasils and 11 blocks.

At the 2011 Census the district had **1,698,730** people on **3,479 sq km**, a density of about 488 per sq km, with a literacy rate of 84.67%.

## Geography and climate

Puri district is a low, flat strip of the Mahanadi delta facing the **Bay of Bengal**. Wikipedia gives its coastline as about 150 km. Two belts run parallel to the coast. On the seaward side is a strip of sandy ridges and dunes several kilometres wide. Inland is a plain of rice fields crossed by river channels.

The rivers, mostly distributaries of the Mahanadi system, include the **Kushabhadra**, **Daya**, **Bhargavi**, **Kadua**, **Prachi** and **Devi**. In the south-west the district takes in part of **Chilika Lake**, a brackish lagoon that has been a Ramsar Wetland of International Importance since 1981. The outer channel near Satapada is the best place to see Irrawaddy dolphins.

The **Balukhand-Konark Wildlife Sanctuary** stretches along the coast between Puri and Konark, with casuarina and cashew plantations first planted in 1916–17. Olive ridley turtles nest on its beaches, and blackbuck have been reintroduced there since 2024.

The climate is humid and tropical. The sea keeps winters mild, and the region is exposed to cyclones before and after the monsoon.

## History

The Puri region was part of ancient Kalinga. Its history is closely bound to the [Jagannath Temple](/history/jagannath-temple), which was begun in the 12th century under **Anantavarman Chodaganga** of the [Eastern Ganga dynasty](/history/eastern-ganga-dynasty). About a century later, around 1250 CE, the Ganga king **Narasimhadeva I** built the [Konark Sun Temple](/history/konark-sun-temple) as a colossal stone chariot of the Sun god.

Older shrines also survive. The **Varahi temple at Chaurasi**, now a Monument of National Importance, is dated to the early 10th century under the Somavamsis. The poet [Jayadeva](/people/jayadeva), author of the *Gita Govinda*, is traditionally linked to **Kenduli Sasan** in the Prachi valley, although his birthplace is debated.

From 1568 the kings of Khurda controlled the Jagannath Temple. The East India Company took the region in 1803. Wikipedia records that in October 1828 the province was divided into three districts, Balasore, Cuttack and **Jagannath**, and the last of these was later renamed Puri. Rebels of the 1817 [Paika Rebellion](/history/paika-rebellion) briefly captured Puri town.

In the early 20th century **Gopabandhu Das** founded the Satyabadi Bana Vidyalaya (1909) at Satyabadi near Puri, which became a centre of Odia nationalism. Puri district was part of Odisha province from its creation in 1936. Bhubaneswar was added to the district in 1959, and the large district was divided in 1993.

## Places to visit

### Shree Jagannath Temple, Puri

The 12th-century temple of Jagannath, Balabhadra and Subhadra is one of Hinduism's great pilgrimage centres. Entry is restricted to Hindus, and temple rules on dress and phones are strictly enforced. See our [Puri travel guide](/travel/puri) and the [Jagannath Temple history](/history/jagannath-temple).

### Bada Danda and Gundicha Temple

The Bada Danda, or Grand Road, runs about 3 km from the main temple to the **Gundicha Temple**, where the chariots stop during Rath Yatra. Its shops sell pattachitra, brass lamps and sweets. The **Atharanala** bridge, a medieval stone bridge on the approach to Puri town, is a Monument of National Importance.

### Konark Sun Temple

UNESCO inscribed the Sun Temple at Konark as a World Heritage Site in 1984. It is built as the Sun god's chariot, with carved wheels and horses, and it is not in active worship, so visitors of all faiths may enter. Konark is about 34 km from Puri. See the [Konark travel guide](/travel/konark).

### Golden Beach and Chandrabhaga

Puri's Golden Beach was among the first eight Indian beaches to receive the Blue Flag eco-label, in October 2020. **Chandrabhaga** beach near Konark hosts a sand art festival during the [Konark Dance Festival](/culture/konark-dance-festival).

### Raghurajpur

Raghurajpur, on the Bhargavi river near Chandanpur, was declared a heritage village by INTACH in 2000. Its artists make [pattachitra](/culture/pattachitra) cloth paintings, [palm-leaf engravings](/culture/palm-leaf-engraving) and masks, and the village is home to [gotipua](/culture/gotipua-dance) troupes. See [Raghurajpur](/travel/raghurajpur).

### Pipili

Pipili, on the Bhubaneswar–Puri road where the Konark road branches off, is the centre of the GI-registered [Pipili appliqué](/culture/pipili-applique) craft. Its canopies and umbrellas are made for the Rath Yatra.

### Satapada and Chilika

Satapada, about 48 km from Puri, is the starting point for boat trips to see Irrawaddy dolphins near Chilika's mouth. See the [Chilika Lake travel guide](/travel/chilika-lake-travel-guide).

### Sakhigopal and Chaurasi

**Sakhigopal**, about 18 km from Puri, has a well-known Krishna shrine. The **Varahi Deula at Chaurasi**, on the Nimapara–Kakatpur road about 30 km from Konark, is a rare 10th-century temple with a vaulted, *khakhara*-style roof and Ramayana reliefs. The Puri district administration also lists **Beleswar**, a Shiva shrine on a sand dune about 17 km from Puri, and the quiet **Balighai** beach on the Puri–Konark Marine Drive, about 14 km from town.

## Culture and festivals

Puri is the home of the [Rath Yatra](/culture/rath-yatra), held in June or July, when Jagannath, Balabhadra and Subhadra are drawn on three huge chariots along the Bada Danda to the Gundicha Temple. The temple year also includes the [Snana Yatra](/culture/snana-yatra), the 42-day [Chandan Yatra](/culture/chandan-yatra) and, in certain years, the [Nabakalebara](/history/nabakalebara) renewal of the wooden deities.

The [Konark Dance Festival](/culture/konark-dance-festival) is held every December beside the Sun Temple. The district is a cradle of temple dance: [Odissi](/culture/odissi-dance) and the boys' dance [gotipua](/culture/gotipua-dance) both have strong roots here.

Puri district has three registered Geographical Indications: **Orissa Pattachitra**, **Pipli Applique Work** and **Konark Stone Carving**. See the [list of GI tags of Odisha](/culture/odisha-gi-tags).

## Food

The temple kitchen of Puri prepares [Mahaprasad](/food/mahaprasad), which is cooked in earthen pots and sold at the Ananda Bazar. Among its dry sweets, [khaja](/food/khaja) is the one most strongly associated with Puri. **Nimapara**, on the road to Konark, is known for [chhena jhilli](/food/chenna-jhilli). Coastal kitchens also serve sea fish, often in mustard gravies such as [machha besara](/food/machha-besara).

## Economy

Pilgrimage and tourism are the mainstays of Puri town, which receives very large numbers of visitors during the Rath Yatra and the winter season. Across the district, paddy farming dominates the delta plain. Marine fishing supports coastal villages, and Chilika fishing supports the Satapada area. Handicrafts such as pattachitra, appliqué and stone carving give work to artisan families in Raghurajpur, Pipili and Konark.

## Notable people

- **[Gopabandhu Das](/people/gopabandhu-das)** (1877–1928): known as Utkalmani, a social reformer, writer and founder of the Satyabadi school, born at Suando near Puri.
- **[Kelucharan Mohapatra](/people/kelucharan-mohapatra)** (born 1926): the Odissi guru who helped revive the classical form, born at Raghurajpur.
- **Jayee Rajguru** (1739–1806): royal priest and minister of the Khurda kingdom, born at Biraharekrushnapur near Puri, and executed by the British in 1806.
- **[Jayadeva](/people/jayadeva)** (12th century): poet of the *Gita Govinda*, whom Odisha tradition places at Kenduli Sasan in Puri district, though other places also claim him.

## How to reach

- **By air:** The nearest airport is Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, approximately 60 km from Puri.
- **By rail:** Puri railway station is a terminus of the East Coast Railway with direct trains from many cities, joined to the main line at Khurda Road.
- **By road:** National Highway 316 links Bhubaneswar with Puri via Pipili. Puri is approximately 60 km from Bhubaneswar, and Konark is approximately 65 km from Bhubaneswar and 34 km from Puri along the Marine Drive.

Many travellers combine Puri, Konark and Bhubaneswar as the [Golden Triangle](/culture/golden-triangle). See [how to reach Odisha](/travel/how-to-reach-odisha) for more.
