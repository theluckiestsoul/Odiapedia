## Overview

**Jagatsinghpur** (ଜଗତସିଂହପୁର) is a coastal district in the Mahanadi delta of eastern Odisha, where the Mahanadi meets the Bay of Bengal. Its headquarters is the town of **Jagatsinghpur**. It is the smallest district in Odisha by area. The district is best known for **Paradip Port**, one of India's major ports, for the shrine of **Goddess Sarala** at Jhankad, and as the land of the medieval poet [Sarala Das](/people/sarala-das).

The district was carved out of [Cuttack district](/district/cuttack) on **1 April 1993**. It has eight tehsils and eight blocks: Jagatsinghpur, Kujang, Tirtol, Balikuda, Biridi, Naugaon, Ersama and Raghunathpur. The [district administration](https://jagatsinghpur.odisha.gov.in/) lists 198 gram panchayats and 1,321 revenue villages (checked October 2026). The Local Government Directory snapshot of December 2022 used in the data section below counts 1,302 villages, because the two lists define a village differently.

The district covers 1,668 sq km by the Census figure; the district administration gives 1,759 sq km. It had 1,136,971 people at the 2011 Census. The literacy rate was high, at 86.59%, and about 10% of people lived in towns. About 92% named Odia as their first language. Four of the district's Assembly seats belong to the Jagatsinghpur (SC) Lok Sabha constituency.

Agriculture remains the main livelihood. However, the port town of Paradip has made the district one of Odisha's main industrial centres, with a refinery, fertiliser plants and cargo terminals.

## Geography and climate

Jagatsinghpur is low, flat and fertile delta country, crossed by branches of the Mahanadi. The district is bounded by Kendrapara to the north, Cuttack to the west, Puri to the south and the Bay of Bengal to the east. Its main rivers are the **Mahanadi**, **Kathajodi**, **Devi** and **Biluakhai**. The Mahanadi enters the sea near Paradip.

Being low-lying and on the coast, the district is exposed to cyclones and storm surges. The **super cyclone of 29 October 1999** killed about 10,000 people across coastal Odisha, and Jagatsinghpur was one of the two worst-hit districts. Down To Earth reports that in Jhatipari village in Ersama block alone, about 315 of some 850 residents died.

## History

The delta lands of present-day Jagatsinghpur shared in the history of the Kalinga coast and its sea trade with South-East Asia (see [Odisha's maritime history](/history/odisha-maritime-history)). The poet **Sarala Das** was born at Kanakavati Patana (Kanakapura) in this area in the 15th century, during the reign of the Gajapati king Kapilendra Deva. His Odia *Mahabharata* is regarded as the first great epic in the Odia language.

The town was earlier called **Hariharpur**. In the early 17th century the English East India Company set up one of its first trading posts in Odisha here; the district administration names Captain Bruton in connection with it, up to 1633. Under Maratha rule in the 18th century, **Jagat Singh** was appointed in 1748 to collect revenue. According to the district administration, he governed the area for a long time without challenge, and the town was renamed **Jagatsinghpur** in his honour around 1786.

The old **Kujang** estate, now in this district, was ruled by the Sandha family. According to the Kendrapara district administration, Raja Chandradhvaja Sandha of Kujang allied with Nilakantha Mangaraj against the British in the early colonial period.

In the national movement, the area produced leading figures such as **Sarala Devi**, the first Odia woman to join the Non-cooperation Movement in 1921, and **Nabakrushna Choudhury**, later Chief Minister of Odisha. Under British rule and after independence, the area formed part of Cuttack district until Jagatsinghpur district was created in 1993.

Paradip's foundation stone was laid by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru on 3 January 1962. The port opened on 12 March 1966 and was declared India's eighth major port on 18 April 1966.

## Places to visit

### Sarala Temple, Jhankad

The temple of **Goddess Sarala** at Jhankad is one of Odisha's major Shakta shrines. The district administration describes the goddess as a blend of Durga and Saraswati. Her eight-armed stone image shows her with one foot on a lion, in the Mahishamardini form. The administration also attributes the shrine to the Bhauma kings of the 8th century. The poet Sarala Das took his name from the goddess. The temple is about 18 km from Jagatsinghpur and about 55 km from Cuttack, and Jhankad Sarala Road railway station is about 2 km away.

### Paradip

**Paradip** (Paradeep), at the mouth of the Mahanadi, combines a busy port town with a long sea beach. Visitors come to see the river mouth, the beach and the ships offshore; port areas are not open to the public without permission.

### Gorakhnath Temple

The Gorakhnath temple in Jagatsinghpur is dedicated to **Guru Gorakhnath** of the Nath tradition.

### Bhubaneswar Mahadev Temple, Balia

The Shiva temple at **Balia**, known as the Bhubaneswar Mahadev temple, is protected by the Archaeological Survey of India as a Monument of National Importance.

## Culture and festivals

Jagatsinghpur is part of the coastal Odia heartland, and its festival calendar follows the coastal pattern: [Durga Puja](/culture/durga-puja), [Raja Parba](/culture/raja-parba), [Kumar Purnima](/culture/kumar-purnima) and the statewide [Boita Bandana](/culture/boita-bandana), which recalls Odisha's seafaring past. The Sarala temple at Jhankad is the main pilgrimage centre.

The district has a strong tradition of Odisha's popular **jatra** (open-air touring theatre). Wikipedia lists well-known companies from the district such as Parbati Gananatya, Tulasi Gananatya, Durgashree Gananatya and Gouri Gananatya, and the Benirampur and Tarapur operas. Literature also has deep roots here, from Sarala Das in the 15th century to the Jnanpith-winning novelist Pratibha Ray in modern times.

Handloom weaving is a traditional craft. Wikipedia counts about 603 handloom units in the district.

## Economy

About 70% of the population depends on agriculture. The main crops are paddy, sugarcane, turmeric, cotton and jute. Fishing is important along the coast and in the river mouths.

**Paradip** is the industrial centre of the district:

- **Paradip Port** handled about 150.41 million tonnes of cargo in 2024–25. It is one of the largest ports on India's east coast.
- **Indian Oil's Paradip Refinery**, with a capacity of 15 million tonnes a year, was dedicated to the nation on 7 February 2016.
- Fertiliser plants of **Paradeep Phosphates** and **IFFCO**, oil marketing terminals and other port-based industries operate in the area.
- Paradip is one of India's notified Petroleum, Chemicals and Petrochemicals Investment Regions (PCPIR).

Wikipedia records about 120 secondary industries in the district.

## Notable people

- **[Sarala Das](/people/sarala-das)**: 15th-century poet, author of the Odia *Mahabharata*, said by tradition to have been born at Kanakavati Patana (Kanakapura).
- **Sarala Devi** (born 1904): freedom fighter, writer and social reformer, born at Narilo. She was the first woman elected to the Odisha Legislative Assembly and the first woman member of the Utkal University senate.
- **Nabakrushna Choudhury**: freedom fighter and Chief Minister of Odisha, born at Kherasa village, now in Jagatsinghpur district.
- **Pranakrushna Parija** (born 1891, Ichhapur): botanist, vice-chancellor of Utkal University and Padma Bhushan recipient (1955).
- **Pratibha Ray** (born 1944, Alabol, Balikuda area): novelist, author of *Yajnaseni*, winner of the Jnanpith Award (2011) and the Padma Bhushan (2022).

## How to reach

- **By air:** The nearest airport is Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, about 70 km from Jagatsinghpur town.
- **By rail:** The Cuttack–Paradip broad-gauge line runs through the district, with about nine stations, including Jhankad Sarala Road and Paradip. Cuttack is the nearest main-line junction.
- **By road:** Jagatsinghpur is about 40 km from Cuttack and about 60 km from Bhubaneswar. Paradip is about 90 km from Cuttack by road along NH-53 and State Highway 12.

For neighbouring districts, see [Cuttack](/district/cuttack), [Kendrapara](/district/kendrapara) and [Puri](/district/puri).
