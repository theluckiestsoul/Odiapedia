# Ratha Yatra – The Grand Chariot Festival

**Ratha Yatra**, also known as the **Festival of Chariots**, is one of the most significant and oldest Hindu festivals celebrated annually in **Puri, Odisha**. It marks the annual journey of **Lord Jagannath**, his elder brother **Lord Balabhadra**, and their sister **Goddess Subhadra** from the **Srimandir** (Jagannath Temple) to the **Gundicha Temple**.

## The Three Chariots

The deities travel in three grand chariots, each distinct in size, color, and name:

1.  **Nandighosa (Lord Jagannath)**
    *   **Height**: 45 feet 6 inches
    *   **Wheels**: 16
    *   **Colors**: Red and Yellow
2.  **Taladhwaja (Lord Balabhadra)**
    *   **Height**: 45 feet
    *   **Wheels**: 14
    *   **Colors**: Red and Green
3.  **Darpadalana (Goddess Subhadra)**
    *   **Height**: 44 feet 6 inches
    *   **Wheels**: 12
    *   **Colors**: Red and Black

Sources differ slightly on the exact heights: some published figures are about a foot lower for each chariot. All agree on the order of size and the number of wheels.

## Key Rituals

### Pahandi Bije
The ceremonial procession where the deities are brought out of the temple in a rhythmic swaying motion. The atmosphere resonates with the beats of cymbals, drums, and chants of *"Jai Jagannath"*.

### Chhera Pahanra
A symbol of humility and equality, where the **Gajapati Maharaja** (King of Puri) sweeps the platforms of the chariots with a golden broom. This ritual signifies that everyone, from the king to the common man, is a servant of the Lord.

### Bahuda Yatra
After a seven-day stay at the Gundicha Temple, the deities return to the main temple on Ashadha Shukla Dashami. This return journey is called **Bahuda Yatra**.

## Significance

Ratha Yatra is a festival of equality and inclusion. It is the best-known occasion when Lord Jagannath leaves the temple and gives *darshan* (blessings) in public to all devotees, irrespective of caste, creed, or religion. Devotees believe that the sight of the Lord on the chariot washes away sins, as a traditional Sanskrit verse says:

> *"Rathe tu vamanam drishtwa punarjanmam na vidyate"*
> (Seeing the Lord on the chariot liberates one from the cycle of rebirth.)
