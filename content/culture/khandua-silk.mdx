**Khandua** (ଖଣ୍ଡୁଆ) is a silk **ikat** textile from **Nuapatna and Maniabandha in Cuttack district**, Odisha. It is also called **Khandua pata**, **Kataki** or **Maniabandhi**. The patterns are made by tie-dyeing (*bandha*) the silk yarn before weaving. Khandua is closely tied to the worship of **Lord Jagannath** at Puri, and some Khandua cloth carries woven verses from Jayadeva's **Gita Govinda**. "Khandua Saree and Fabrics" was registered as a **Geographical Indication** in 2010.

## What does "Khandua" mean?

The word is commonly explained as referring to a cloth worn on the lower half of the body. Odia accounts link the name to the lower garment offered to Jagannath. The alternative names come from places. "Kataki" refers to Cuttack, and "Maniabandhi" to the village of Maniabandha.

## History and the Jagannath connection

According to tradition, Khandua cloth has been woven for [Jagannath Temple](/history/jagannath-temple) at Puri for centuries. Popular accounts say it is mentioned in the *Madala Panji*, the temple chronicle. They also say that the cloth became associated with Nuapatna under a later king of Puri in the 18th century. These claims come from feature writing and community tradition. Odiapedia has not been able to check them against a primary source, so readers should treat the dates as traditional.

The best-known Khandua tradition is the **Gita Govinda Khandua**. This is a cloth into which lines from the 12th-century Sanskrit poem *Gita Govinda* by the poet [Jayadeva](/people/jayadeva) are woven using the ikat technique. Accounts of temple practice say that cloth bearing these verses is offered to Lord Jagannath. The words are tie-dyed into the yarn letter by letter before weaving. This takes great skill, because every character must line up exactly on the loom.

Khandua remains in demand in Odisha for weddings and religious occasions. Jagannath's festivals, such as the [Rath Yatra](/culture/rath-yatra), are part of the same devotional culture that Khandua grew out of.

## How is Khandua woven?

Khandua uses the **bandha (ikat)** technique shared with other Odisha textiles:

1. **Design.** The pattern is planned and marked on bundles of silk yarn.
2. **Tying.** Sections of yarn are tightly bound so they resist dye.
3. **Dyeing.** The bundles are dyed. Tying and dyeing are repeated for each colour.
4. **Weaving.** The dyed yarn is set on the loom so the pattern appears as the cloth is woven.

Because the dye bleeds slightly into the tied areas, ikat motifs have a soft, feathered edge. This is a sign of handwork, not a flaw.

## Colours and motifs

Traditional Khandua is associated with **red, orange and yellow tones**, with maroon and cream also common. Compared with some other Odisha ikats, Khandua often has **plainer borders**, with the main design in the body and pallu. Recorded motifs include:

| Motif | Description |
|---|---|
| Hati (elephant) | An auspicious elephant, often surrounded by vines |
| Peacocks and creepers | Birds among trailing vines |
| Large flowers | Bold floral forms |
| Nabagunjara | A mythical creature made of nine animals, a popular Odisha motif |
| Deula and kumbha | Temple spire and pot shapes |
| Fish and tortoise | Water creatures, also common in other Odisha weaves |
| Script | Woven verses, in Gita Govinda cloths |

## GI status

The GI Registry's record for application number 136 shows the following:

- **Product:** Khandua Saree and Fabrics
- **Applicant:** Cooperative Societies of Khandua Saree and Fabrics of Orissa
- **Filed:** 12 September 2008
- **Registered:** 22 October 2010, certificate number 146

Khandua is one of nine Odisha handloom GIs. See the full [list of GI tags of Odisha](/culture/odisha-gi-tags).

## Weaving communities

Khandua is woven by weaving families in and around **Nuapatna and Maniabandha** in [Cuttack district](/district/cuttack). The work is organised through weavers' cooperative societies, which together hold the GI registration. Tying and dyeing the yarn is often shared among family members, while the weaving itself is done on traditional handlooms.

## How to buy authentic Khandua

- **Check the weave.** Genuine Khandua is handwoven silk ikat. Motif edges are slightly blurred, and the pattern shows on both sides of the cloth. Printed imitations have sharp edges and a pale reverse.
- **Know the origin.** Ask whether the sari was woven in Nuapatna or Maniabandha, and by which society.
- **Be careful with "Gita Govinda" pieces.** Text-woven Khandua is rare and specialised. Ask the seller for details of the weaver.
- **Buy from official cooperatives.** **Boyanika**, the Odisha State Handloom Weavers' Co-operative Society, sells Khandua as one of its GI textiles. **Utkalika**, the Odisha State Co-operative Handicraft Corporation (utkalikaodisha.com), lists Nuapatna and Khandua silk saris.
