The **Bomkai saree** (ବୋମକାଇ ଶାଢ଼ୀ) is a handwoven sari from Odisha. It is named after **Bomkai village in Ganjam district** and is now woven mainly by the **Bhulia weavers of Subarnapur (Sonepur)** in western Odisha. Bomkai saris are known for thick, richly patterned borders and pallus made with an **extra-weft** technique. Their motifs include fish, lotus and temple spires. "Bomkai Saree & Fabrics" is a registered **Geographical Indication** of Odisha.

## Where does the Bomkai saree come from?

The style takes its name from Bomkai, a village in Ganjam district in southern Odisha. According to accounts cited in Odisha handloom histories, the design was later introduced to Sonepur during the rule of a king of Patna state, named in those accounts as Ramai Dev. In Sonepur it was taken up by the **Bhulia** weaving community. Today, Subarnapur district is the main centre of Bomkai production. That is why shops often sell these saris as "Sonepuri Bomkai". Cotton Bomkai saris are also still woven in Ganjam.

Sources do not agree on when the style began. Its origin story belongs to local and community tradition rather than to a dated record.

## How is a Bomkai saree woven?

The defining feature of Bomkai is **supplementary (extra) yarn patterning**. A weaver adds extra threads to the basic cloth to raise motifs on it:

- **Extra-warp** designs usually run along the **border**.
- **Extra-weft** designs decorate the **body and pallu**.
- The patterns are lifted with a **dobby, jacquard or the traditional jala** (a hand-tied pattern harness).

Many Bomkai saris also include **ikat (bandha)** sections, where the yarn is tie-dyed before weaving. This mixes the two main weaving traditions of western Odisha in a single sari. For borders in a different colour from the body, weavers use a **three-shuttle technique**, locally called *muhajorha*. Weavers use both pit looms and frame looms.

### Materials

Early Bomkai saris were cotton. Today they are woven in fine cotton, **mulberry silk**, **tussar silk** and sometimes **zari**. Silk Bomkai saris are worn at weddings and festivals. Cotton ones are lighter and suited to everyday wear.

## Motifs and colours

Bomkai motifs are drawn from nature and temple architecture:

| Motif | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Matsya (fish) | Fish, an auspicious symbol in Odia culture |
| Padma (lotus) | The lotus flower |
| Temple border | Rows of triangular temple spires along the edge |
| Kachhapa (tortoise) | The tortoise |
| Geometric squares | Small checks and squares in the body |

Traditional Bomkai saris often use bold base colours such as **red, black and white**. Today they are woven in many colours.

## GI status

According to the GI Registry's state-wise list (as on 31 December 2025), **"Bomkai Saree & Fabrics"** is a registered GI of Odisha. Its application number is 217, and the application was filed on 13 September 2010. Some secondary sources give a different year for the GI, so check the registry if the exact date matters. Bomkai is one of nine Odisha handloom GIs. See the full [list of GI tags of Odisha](/culture/odisha-gi-tags).

## Artisan communities

The **Bhulia** community of Subarnapur is most closely associated with Bomkai weaving today. They are also known for Sambalpuri ikat. Weaving is usually a household craft, with family members sharing the work of preparing yarn, tying and dyeing, and weaving. In Ganjam, weavers around Bomkai continue the older cotton tradition.

## How to buy authentic Bomkai

Power-loom and printed copies of Bomkai designs are common. A few checks help:

- **Look at the back.** Handwoven extra-weft motifs leave floating threads on the reverse, and small irregularities are normal. A perfectly flat, printed-looking back suggests a copy.
- **Check the selvedge and border.** On a handloom sari the border is woven in, not stitched on or printed.
- **Ask for provenance.** Ask who wove it, or which cooperative it came from. Ask whether it is sold as a GI product.
- **Buy from official cooperatives.** **Boyanika**, the Odisha State Handloom Weavers' Co-operative Society, applied for several of Odisha's handloom GIs and sells Bomkai saris. **Utkalika**, the Odisha State Co-operative Handicraft Corporation (utkalikaodisha.com), also lists Bomkai cotton and silk saris.
