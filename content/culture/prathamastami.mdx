**Prathamastami** (ପ୍ରଥମାଷ୍ଟମୀ) is an Odia family festival that celebrates the **eldest child** of a household and prays for his or her long life and prosperity. It is observed on **Margashira Krishna Ashtami**, the eighth day of the waning moon in the month of Margashira, which falls eight days after Kartika Purnima, usually in late November or early December. The day is marked with an aarti for the eldest child, new clothes, gifts from the maternal uncle's home and the steamed rice cake **enduri pitha**.

## Significance

The name joins *prathama*, "first", and *ashtami*, the eighth lunar day. Prathamastami is one of the most family-centred festivals in the Odia calendar: the eldest child is treated as the guest of honour, and the rituals ask the household's gods and protective goddess for the child's health and long life. Wikipedia notes that the same tithi is also known by other names, such as Kaal Bhairav Ashtami.

### For sons or for all firstborns?

Sources describe the festival in slightly different ways. A Kalinga TV report of 2025 speaks of prayers for "the eldest son of the family", reflecting older practice, while The Statesman and other accounts describe the celebration of "the eldest child" or "firstborn child".

## Rituals of Prathamastami

The household rituals vary by family and region, but the core elements are widely shared:

- **Aarti of the eldest child.** The mother and senior women of the family wave a lighted lamp before the child, apply sandalwood paste to the forehead and recite blessings and prayers.
- **New clothes.** The eldest child wears new clothes for the day.
- **Worship.** The family worships **Shashthi Devi**, the goddess who protects children, along with Ganesha and the family's own deities.
- **The maternal uncle's gift.** The child's maternal uncle (*mamu*) and his family traditionally send a gift basket, the *bhara*, with new clothes, sweets and the ingredients for the day's food: coconut, jaggery, rice, black gram and turmeric leaves.

### Prathamastami at the Jagannath Temple

The festival is also observed at the **Jagannath Temple in Puri**, where Balabhadra, as the elder brother (Bada Thakura), is honoured as the eldest child. Kalinga TV reported in 2025 that Balabhadra receives a special bath, sandalwood and new attire on the day, and that the Prathamastami *bhara* is brought to the temple from the **Madhaba temple at Niali** (Niali Madhaba), which in temple tradition stands as the deities' maternal uncle's home. See [Jagannath Temple, Puri](/history/jagannath-temple).

## Where is it celebrated?

Prathamastami is celebrated in Odia homes throughout Odisha and by Odia families elsewhere. The Government of Odisha Higher Education Department's 2026 holiday list for colleges includes Prathamastami on 1 December 2026.

## Food associated with Prathamastami

The signature dish is **enduri pitha**. A batter of rice and black gram is spread on fresh **turmeric leaves**, filled with a sweet mixture of grated coconut and jaggery, folded and steamed. The turmeric leaves give the cake its distinctive aroma. Enduri pitha is offered to the deities and then served to the eldest child and the family. For more Odia cakes, see [Odia pitha](/food/odia-pitha).
