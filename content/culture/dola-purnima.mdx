**Dola Purnima** (ଦୋଳ ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣିମା), also called **Dola Yatra** or Dola Jatra, is Odisha's spring festival of **Radha and Krishna**, celebrated on **Phalguna Purnima**, the full moon of the month of Phalguna. *Dola* means "swing": over several days, images of Krishna and of village deities are carried in decorated palanquins from house to house, smeared with **abira** (dry colour) and finally swung on festive swings at gatherings called **Dola Melana**. Holi, the festival of colours, follows on the next day.

## Significance

Dola Purnima welcomes spring, and is also known as **Basantotsava** (spring festival). In Odisha it is above all a festival of Krishna as the playful cowherd and of the divine couple Radha and Krishna. According to Hindu tradition, seeing Krishna on the swing on this day removes one's sins.

Sources also connect the season to the story of **Prahlada**, the young devotee of Vishnu who was saved from his father, the demon-king Hiranyakashipu. This legend, which underlies the bonfire and colour-play of Holi across India, gives the festival its theme of the victory of good over evil.

## How is Dola Purnima celebrated in Odisha?

### Vimana processions and abira

The distinctive Odia form of the festival takes place in villages, particularly in the coastal districts. Images of Krishna and other village deities are placed in decorated palanquins called **vimanas** and carried by bearers from house to house, led by drummers and groups singing devotional songs. At each home the family offers **bhoga** (food offerings) to the deity, and people smear each other with **abira**, dry coloured powder. OdishaBytes describes these processions as running for several days and culminating on the full moon.

### Dola Melana

On or around the full moon, the vimanas of several villages converge on a common ground, often known as the **Melana Padia**. Swings are set up on platforms, the deities are placed on them, and devotees gather to worship and celebrate together. These **Dola Melana** gatherings are local fairs, and their scale and exact customs vary from one area to another.

### Dola Purnima at the Jagannath Temple, Puri

At the Jagannath Temple in Puri, Jagannath is worshipped as **Dola Govinda** (Krishna) during the festival. On Dola Purnima the deities are adorned in **Suna Besha**, the golden attire also called **Rajadhiraja Besha**, one of several occasions in the year when the deities wear gold ornaments. Sambad English reported in 2020 that the temple doors opened at 3 am and that the representative image was taken to the **Dola Mandapa**, the swing pavilion, where devotees offered herbal colours. The festival draws large crowds; on Dola Purnima 2026 (3 March), Kalinga TV reported a stampede-like situation in the temple, without injuries. See [Jagannath Temple, Puri](/history/jagannath-temple).

## Where is it celebrated?

Dola Purnima is observed across Odisha, in Krishna and Jagannath temples in towns and in village communities; the vimana processions and Dola Melana are characteristic of rural coastal Odisha. It is a state public holiday in Odisha.

## Food associated with Dola Purnima

The main food custom of Dola Purnima is the **bhoga** offered to the visiting deity at each house during the vimana processions; the contents vary by family and village. The sources consulted do not identify a single signature dish for the festival.
