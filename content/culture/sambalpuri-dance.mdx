**Sambalpuri dance** (ସମ୍ବଲପୁରୀ ନୃତ୍ୟ) is the popular umbrella name for the **folk dances of Western Odisha**. These are sung and danced in the Sambalpuri (Western Odia) language to a loud ensemble of drums and the *mahuri* pipe. The best known is **Dalkhai**, a dance performed mainly by women at festivals such as **Bhai Jiuntia** and **Nuakhai**. It takes its name from the refrain *"Dalkhai bo"*. Related forms include **Rasarkeli, Maila Jada, Jaiphula** and the harvest-season **Karma**.

## Where is Sambalpuri dance from?

These dances belong to the western uplands of Odisha, in and around [Sambalpur](/district/sambalpur), [Bargarh](/district/bargarh), [Balangir](/district/balangir) and [Subarnapur](/district/subarnapur). They are rooted in village and tribal life. According to accounts of the form, Dalkhai is especially associated with communities including the **Binjhal, Kuda and Mirdha**. Over the past few decades Sambalpuri songs and dances have spread through stage shows, recordings and films. Today they are performed across Odisha and well beyond it.

The region's distinct culture includes the Sambalpuri language, the [Sambalpuri saree](/culture/sambalpuri-saree) and festivals like [Nuakhai](/culture/nuakhai). The dances are a central part of that identity. For the region's past, see [history of Sambalpur](/history/sambalpur-history).

## Dalkhai: the signature dance

### Name and songs

The name **Dalkhai** comes from the call *"Dalkhai bo"*, which dancers sing at the beginning and end of each stanza. Dalkhai songs are short, rhythmic verses. Many draw on stories of **Radha and Krishna** and episodes from Hindu epics. Others are playful, teasing or romantic exchanges. In performance, male singers or musicians often address the women dancers, and the women answer through the song.

### Dancers and costume

Dalkhai is danced mainly by **women and young girls**. They usually stand in a line or semicircle. They often wear colourful Sambalpuri saris with scarves. Movements combine bending at the knees and waist, swaying steps, and hand gestures that follow the beat. As the drumming speeds up, the dancers move faster too.

### When is Dalkhai performed?

Dalkhai is closely tied to the festival calendar of Western Odisha, including:

- **Bhai Jiuntia**, when sisters observe a fast and pray for their brothers' long life
- **Phagun Puni**, the full-moon festival of the month of Phalguna
- [Nuakhai](/culture/nuakhai), the harvest festival of new rice

It is also performed at weddings, fairs and cultural events.

## Music and instruments

Sambalpuri dances are driven by a powerful folk orchestra:

| Instrument | Type | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Dhol | Two-headed barrel drum | Sets and leads the rhythm |
| Nisan | Large kettle drum with a metal shell | Deep, booming beats |
| Tamki | Small one-sided drum played with two sticks | Fast, sharp patterns |
| Tasa | One-sided drum | Adds volume and drive |
| Mahuri | Double-reed wind instrument | Carries the melody |

The **dhol** player usually controls the pace and builds up to fast, energetic endings.

## Other Western Odisha folk forms

Western Odisha has a large family of song-and-dance forms. Writers on Odisha's culture group some of them as **youth songs and dances**: **Rasarkeli, Jaiphula, Maila Jada, Bayamana, Gunchikuta** and **Dalkhai**. Others are **work and seasonal songs**, such as **Karma** and **Jhumer**. These forms share the same language and drum-based music but differ in tune, rhythm and occasion. Sources vary in how they classify and spell them, so the names above follow common usage.

Nearby, Kalahandi district has its own drum dance, the [Ghumura](/culture/ghumura-dance). Elsewhere in Odisha, folk traditions include [Danda Nacha](/culture/danda-nacha).

## Sambalpuri dance today

Sambalpuri dance is now a regular part of college festivals, state cultural programmes and community events in Odisha's cities. Stage performances often bring together several folk forms in one choreographed item. Village performances at festival time remain the living source of the tradition.
