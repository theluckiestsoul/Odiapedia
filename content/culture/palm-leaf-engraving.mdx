**Palm-leaf engraving** (ତାଳପତ୍ର ଚିତ୍ର), also called **tala pattachitra** or **talapatra chitra**, is the Odisha art of **incising pictures and text on dried palm leaves with an iron stylus**. The artist then rubs dark pigment into the cut lines. For centuries this was how **Odia manuscripts** were written. It is still practised as an art form today, especially in the craft village of **Raghurajpur** near Puri. Its finest works are narrow, stitched panels that unfold like a book or fan to show scenes from Hindu epics and the life of Krishna.

## From manuscripts to art

Before paper and print became common, Odisha's literature, scripture, astrology, medicine and temple records were written on palm leaves. Scribes cut the letters into the leaf surface and blackened them. The leaves were then strung together and protected between wooden covers, forming a book known as a *pothi*.

The **Odisha State Museum in Bhubaneswar** holds one of the largest collections of these manuscripts. Wikipedia, citing the museum, puts the figure at around 40,000 palm-leaf manuscripts. Most are in **Odia script**, though many of the texts are in Sanskrit. Many Odisha manuscripts are also illustrated. Illustrated copies of works such as Jayadeva's *Gita Govinda* show how text and image were combined on the same leaf.

A common explanation for the **rounded shape of Odia letters** is the palm-leaf medium. A stylus drawn in long straight lines along the leaf could split it, so scribes favoured curves. This is the most widely held view, although it is not the only factor proposed. You can read more in [Odia script history](/language/odia-script-history).

## How is palm-leaf engraving made?

1. **Preparing the leaves.** Leaves are cut, dried and hardened, then trimmed into strips of equal size. Traditional treatments help make the leaves flexible and resistant to insects. The exact methods vary from family to family.
2. **Stitching.** For larger works, strips are sewn together side by side to form a panel or "canvas". Artists also make single leaves, long scrolls and fan-folding sets.
3. **Incising.** With an **iron stylus**, the artist cuts the outline and detail directly into the leaf surface. There is no pencil sketch to erase, so every line must be right the first time.
4. **Inking.** A dark pigment is rubbed over the surface so it settles into the grooves. Craft accounts commonly describe a natural black made from soot or burnt material mixed with oil, though recipes vary between families. The surface is then wiped clean, leaving the engraved drawing in black.
5. **Colouring (optional).** Some works add touches of colour.

Some advanced pieces are made in **layers**. Two leaves are glued together over most of their surface, but small "windows" in the top leaf open to reveal a second image beneath.

## Themes and motifs

Palm-leaf art draws on the same storytelling world as [Pattachitra](/culture/pattachitra) cloth painting:

| Theme | Examples |
|---|---|
| Krishna and Radha | Scenes from the *Gita Govinda* and Krishna's childhood |
| Epics | Episodes from the Ramayana and Mahabharata |
| Jagannath | The deities of [Jagannath Temple](/history/jagannath-temple) and temple festivals |
| Dashavatara | The ten avatars of Vishnu |
| Calendars and charts | Astrological and almanac panels |
| Decorative borders | Floral and geometric frames |

## Raghurajpur, the main centre

**Raghurajpur** lies on the southern bank of the Bhargavi river in [Puri district](/district/puri). After a documentation project by INTACH, it was chosen in **2000** to be developed as **Odisha's first heritage village**. Artists' homes there are painted with murals. Many families practise **pattachitra painting, palm-leaf engraving, tussar painting** and other crafts, often in workshops that visitors can enter. Raghurajpur is also known for its Gotipua dance tradition. See the [Raghurajpur travel guide](/travel/raghurajpur).

## GI status

**Palm-leaf engraving does not have a separate Geographical Indication.** The related tradition **Orissa Pattachitra** is a registered GI of Odisha (application 88), and its logo is registered separately (application 386). Odiapedia has not been able to confirm whether the Pattachitra registration covers palm-leaf work. Sellers should not describe palm-leaf engravings as GI products unless they can show this. See the [list of GI tags of Odisha](/culture/odisha-gi-tags).

## How to buy authentic palm-leaf engraving

- **Check the lines.** Real engraving has lines cut into the leaf that you can feel with a fingertip. Printed copies are flat.
- **Look at the leaves.** Each strip of a stitched panel should be a real palm leaf with natural grain, joined with thread.
- **Buy from the artist or a cooperative.** In Raghurajpur you can buy directly from artist families. **Utkalika**, the Odisha State Co-operative Handicraft Corporation (utkalikaodisha.com), sells palm-leaf paintings through its showrooms and online store.
- **Care for it.** Keep palm-leaf art dry, away from direct sun, and store fan-folded pieces closed.
