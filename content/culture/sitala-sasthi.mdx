**Sitala Sasthi** (ଶୀତଳ ଷଷ୍ଠୀ), also written Sital Sasthi or Sitalsasthi, is a festival of western Odisha that celebrates the **wedding of Shiva and Parvati**. It is held on **Jyeshtha Shukla Shashthi**, the sixth day of the bright fortnight of Jyeshtha (May–June), and is celebrated most grandly in **Sambalpur**, where local families take the roles of the divine parents and Shiva arrives at the wedding in a festive procession through the city.

## Significance

According to Hindu tradition, Sitala Sasthi marks the marriage of Shiva and Parvati; Wikipedia notes that the marriage is described in the Shiva Purana. The festival falls towards the end of the hot season. Kalinga TV describes Sital Sasthi as one of the most important festivals of western Odisha.

### Origins in Sambalpur

Local accounts summarised by Wikipedia link the festival's beginnings in Sambalpur to Brahmin families from Puri who settled there at the invitation of the Chauhan kings, naming **King Ajit Singh**, who ruled in the 18th century. According to this account, the festival began among the Brahmin community of **Nandapada**, was later joined by **Jharuapada**, and a third procession was added by the residents of **Mudipada** in 1972. Wikipedia also gives the festival's age as about 400 years, which does not match the 18th-century date given for Ajit Singh; Odiapedia treats the exact origin as uncertain. For the wider history of the city, see [history of Sambalpur](/history/sambalpur-history).

## Rituals of Sitala Sasthi

The festival is performed as a full Odia wedding, with the gods as bride and groom.

### Thala Utha

Preparations start weeks ahead with **Thala Utha** (Thala Utha Parba), held on **Akshaya Tritiya** at Shiva temples in localities such as Nandapada, Jharuapada and Mudipada. Kalinga TV describes Thala Utha as the ritual that marks the beginning of Sital Sasthi: the families who will act as the parents of the bride and groom are chosen, and fund-raising for the celebrations begins.

### The parents of the bride

A local family is selected to act as **Parvati's parents**. In the wedding they receive the groom's party and perform **kanyadana**, the giving away of the bride, just as parents would at a human wedding.

### Shiva's wedding procession

The high point is the **barat**, Shiva's wedding procession. Shiva, seated in a palanquin in the dress of a bridegroom, is taken through the city to the bride's side, accompanied by music and crowds of devotees. Kalinga TV reported in 2024 that the procession passed through almost all the *padas* (neighbourhoods) of Sambalpur and that the *hastagranthi*, the tying of the couple's hands, was performed in the last part of the night. On the following day the deities return to the temple.

Over the years the processions have grown into large public spectacles with folk dance, music and decorated floats, and the Government of Odisha now provides funding for the Sambalpur celebrations.

## Where is it celebrated?

The main celebrations are in **Sambalpur** city; see the [Sambalpur district](/district/sambalpur) page. According to Wikipedia, the festival has spread to other towns of western Odisha, including Bargarh, Padampur, Barpali and [Jharsuguda](/district/jharsuguda). On the same tithi, the wedding of Shiva and Parvati is also celebrated at the **Lingaraj Temple** in Bhubaneswar, where Lingaraj is taken in procession to the Kedar Gouri temple; see [Lingaraj Temple](/history/lingaraj-temple).

## Food associated with Sitala Sasthi

As a wedding, Sitala Sasthi involves feasts and offerings prepared by the host families and committees, but the sources consulted do not name a dish specific to the festival.
