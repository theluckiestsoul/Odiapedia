# Makar Sankranti — The Sun Festival of Odisha

**Makar Sankranti** is one of the most important festivals of Odisha, celebrated every year in the month of **Pausha** (January 14-15). It marks the Sun's entry into the zodiac sign of Capricorn (Makar Rashi) and is traditionally regarded as the start of Uttarayan, the Sun's northward course.

## When is Makar Sankranti Celebrated?

Makar Sankranti is a **solar festival** that falls on **January 14 or 15** every year according to the Gregorian calendar. Unlike other Hindu festivals that follow the lunar calendar, this festival's date remains nearly fixed.

| Year | Makar Sankranti Date |
|------|---------------------|
| 2026 | January 14 |
| 2027 | January 14 or 15 (check the Odia panjika) |
| 2028 | January 15 (check the Odia panjika) |

Almanacs can differ by a day depending on the exact time of the Sun's transit.

## Significance of Makar Sankranti

The festival holds deep meaning across multiple dimensions:

### Astronomical Significance
- **Beginning of Uttarayan** — Traditionally treated as the start of the Sun's northward journey
- **Longer Days** — Days are lengthening; astronomically this began at the winter solstice around 21 December, and the two dates have drifted apart over centuries because of precession
- **End of Harsh Winter** — The intense cold starts receding

### Religious Significance
- **Auspicious Period** — Ideal time for donations, holy baths, and worship
- **Bhishma's Departure** — In the Mahabharata, Bhishma chose this day to leave his body
- **Devas' Day** — Uttarayan is considered the day of the gods

### Agricultural Significance
- **Harvest Festival** — New paddy has been harvested
- **New Rice Celebration** — Celebrated as a thanksgiving for crops
- **Farmers' Blessings** — Prayers for a bountiful harvest

> "Makar Sankranti symbolizes new hope, new goals, and new beginnings."

## How Makar Sankranti is Celebrated in Odisha

In Odisha, Makar Sankranti is celebrated with great enthusiasm. It is locally known as **"Makara"** or **"Makara Mela"**.

### Key Rituals

#### Morning Holy Bath
- **River Bath** — Taking a dip in Mahanadi, Brahmani, and other rivers
- **Lake Bath** — Bathing at sacred spots in Chilika
- **Sesame Oil Massage** — Applying sesame oil before bathing

#### Worship and Prayers
1. **Sun Worship** — Offering Arghya (water) to the Sun God
2. **Home Puja** — Worshipping Lakshmi and other deities
3. **Tulsi Worship** — Lighting lamps at the Tulsi plant

#### Donations (Daan)
- **Sesame Donation** — Giving sesame-based items
- **Jaggery Donation** — Giving sweet items
- **Cloth Donation** — Giving new clothes
- **Cow Donation** — Donating cows (considered highly auspicious)

## Traditional Pithas and Sweets

In Odisha, Makar Sankranti is closely associated with **pithas**. Various types of traditional rice cakes (Pithas) are prepared in every household during this time.

![Traditional Odia Pithas](/images/odia-pithas-sweets.png)

### Popular Pithas

| Pitha Name | Description | Main Ingredients |
|-----------|-------------|------------------|
| **Chakuli Pitha** | Thin, round rice pancake | Rice and black gram batter |
| **Manda Pitha** | Steamed dumpling | Rice flour, coconut, jaggery |
| **Kakara Pitha** | Fried cake with a sweet filling | Flour, coconut, jaggery |
| **Enduri Pitha** | Steamed in turmeric leaves | Rice, black gram, coconut |
| **Arisa Pitha** | Sweet, dark brown cake | Rice flour, jaggery, ghee |
| **Poda Pitha** | Roasted coconut filling | Rice flour, coconut, jaggery |
| **Chitau Pitha** | Soft, white pancake | Parboiled rice |

### Other Delicacies

- **Makara Chaula** — Uncooked newly harvested rice mixed with banana, coconut, jaggery, sesame, milk and other ingredients
- **Til Laddu** — Sesame and jaggery balls
- **Muga Laddu** — Moong dal sweets
- **Khiri** — Rice pudding with milk
- **Chhena Jhili** — Cottage cheese sweets

## Famous Makar Melas in Odisha

Several grand fairs are held across Odisha during Makar Sankranti.

### Chandrabhaga Mela, Konark (on Magha Saptami)

Konark's best-known solar festival is held a few weeks after Makar Sankranti, on **Magha Saptami** (January–February).

- **Location**: Chandrabhaga beach, Konark, Puri District
- **Highlights**: Holy dip at sunrise, visiting the Sun Temple
- **Legend**: According to tradition, Samba, son of Krishna, was cured of leprosy here by worshipping the Sun
- **Visitors**: Thousands of pilgrims

Devotees take a holy dip at sunrise and then visit the Sun Temple.

### Jagannath Temple, Puri

- **Special Worship** — Special rituals, with Makara Chaula offered to Lord Jagannath

### Other Famous Melas

1. **Makara Mela** — Dhabaleswar, Cuttack
2. **Makara Mela** — Hatakeshwar temple, Atri, Khordha
3. **Makara Mela** — Makara Muni temple, Balasore

### Significance of Kite Flying

- **Sun Exposure** — Morning sunlight is beneficial for health
- **Social Gathering** — Meeting neighbors and friends

## Cultural Significance

Makar Sankranti is deeply connected with Odisha's culture:

### Family Reunions
- **Married Women's Visit** — Daughters return to their parents' home
- **Pitha Distribution** — Pithas are shared with relatives
- **Blessings** — Seeking blessings from elders

### Folk Songs and Dance
Various folk songs are sung during Makar Sankranti, and the songs differ from region to region.

### Nabanna Celebration
Farming families express gratitude for the new harvest.

---

## How the Day Usually Unfolds

Families differ, but a typical day runs from a sesame oil massage and early bath, to offering arghya to the rising Sun, puja and donations, then makara chaula, pithas and a festive meal, with kite flying and cultural programs later in the day.
