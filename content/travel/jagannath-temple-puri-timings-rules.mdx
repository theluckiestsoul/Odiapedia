This page collects the practical, frequently changing details for visiting **Shree Jagannath Temple** in Puri. For the temple's history and architecture see [Jagannath Temple](/history/jagannath-temple); for the town, beaches and food see the [Puri travel guide](/travel/puri). Rules can change at short notice — check local notices or the [Shree Jagannatha Temple Administration](https://www.shreejagannatha.in/) before you go. **Last checked: 6 October 2026.**

## Daily timings

The temple follows a fixed sequence of rituals (*niti*). These approximate times come from the temple administration's published schedule; it notes that it is "generally not possible to follow the time prescribed for each ritual", so expect delays, and longer closures on festival days.

| Approx. time | Ritual |
|---|---|
| 4:30–5:00 am | Dwarafita — doors opened |
| 5:30 am | Mangala Alati |
| 6:00–7:00 am | Mailam, Abakash, then Besha (dressing) |
| 8:30 am | Gopala Ballabha Bhoga (breakfast offering) |
| 9–10 am | Sakala Dhupa (morning food offering) |
| Midday–afternoon | Bhoga Mandap and Madhyahna Dhupa |
| 6:00 pm | Sandhya Alati, then Sandhya Dhupa |
| 9–10 pm | Badasinghara Besha |
| From 11:30 pm | Khataseja Lagi and Pahuda — temple closes |

Darshan is interrupted while the doors are closed for the dhupa (food offerings). Early morning after Mangala Alati and the evening after Sandhya Alati are usually the best times for a calmer darshan.

**Ratna Bhandar inventory (2026):** the inventory of the temple treasury that began on 25 March 2026 — the first since 1978 — has continued in stages. On inventory days devotees have been given darshan only from the outer barricade (*Bahara Katha*), so check the news before planning a visit around a specific day.

## Who may enter

By long-standing tradition **only Hindus** may enter the temple; foreigners and non-Hindus are not admitted. Anyone can see the **Patitapabana** image of Jagannath set above the **Singhadwara** (Lion Gate) from the street outside.

Since **13 June 2024** all four gates — Singhadwara (east), Ashwadwara (south), Vyaghradwara (west) and Hastidwara (north) — are open to devotees again; during and after the COVID period entry had been through a single gate.

## Dress code

From **1 January 2024** the temple administration made "decent clothes" compulsory. Not allowed: shorts and half pants, torn jeans, skirts and sleeveless dresses. Men commonly wear a dhoti or trousers and shirt, and women a saree or salwar kameez. Chewing paan or gutkha and carrying plastic are also banned on the premises, with fines for violators.

In **April 2026** the Odisha State Law Commission proposed a stricter, legally backed dress code (for example saree-blouse or salwar kameez only for women). When we last checked this was a proposal, not a rule — dress traditionally to be safe.

## Phones, cameras and belongings

- **Mobile phones** have been banned inside the temple for everyone — devotees, servitors and police — since **1 January 2023**. Deposit counters operate near the Singhadwara and the south gate.
- **Cameras** are also not allowed, and photography inside is prohibited.
- Leave bags, leather items and valuables at your hotel to save time at the security check.

## Mahaprasad and Ananda Bazar

**Mahaprasad** — rice, dal, vegetables and sweets cooked in earthen pots in the temple kitchen — is sold at **Ananda Bazar**, inside the temple complex, where it is eaten sitting on the floor. A promised official price list had still not been displayed as of November 2025, so agree prices before buying. Beware of websites offering "online puja": the temple administration says it never accepts donations or offerings for online puja. Read more: [Mahaprasad](/food/mahaprasad).

## How to reach

- **Air:** Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, about 60 km by road (1½–2 hours). Airport Express buses and taxis run to Puri.
- **Rail:** Puri railway station is a terminus with direct trains from Kolkata (Howrah), Delhi and other cities; the temple is a short auto-rickshaw ride away.
- **Road:** Bhubaneswar–Puri is about 60 km; Konark is about 35 km along the Marine Drive.

## Best time to visit

October to March is coolest. **Rath Yatra** (June–July) brings the largest crowds of the year — see [Rath Yatra 2027 dates](/travel/rath-yatra-2027) — and the monsoon follows.
