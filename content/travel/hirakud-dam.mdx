**Hirakud Dam** (ହୀରାକୁଦ ବନ୍ଧ) is a major earthen dam across the Mahanadi river, about 10 km from the city of Sambalpur in western Odisha. Hirakud Dam is a multipurpose project that provides flood control, irrigation and hydroelectric power, and its vast reservoir has been a Ramsar-listed wetland since 2021.

## Why is Hirakud Dam famous?

Hirakud Dam's main section is about 4.8 km long, and with the earthen dykes on both flanks the whole structure runs for about 25.8 km. Odisha Tourism calls it the world's longest earthen dam. The foundation stone was laid on 15 March 1946 by Sir Hawthorne Lewis, Governor of Odisha; construction was completed in 1953 and Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru inaugurated the dam on 13 January 1957. The reservoir behind it is one of the largest artificial lakes in India. According to the Ramsar Sites Information Service, it supports more than 130 bird species and about 54 fish species, irrigates around 436,000 hectares and supports the livelihoods of thousands of fishers.

## Top things to see and do at Hirakud

- **Gandhi Minar and Jawahar Minar:** two observation towers, one at each end of the dam, give long views over the reservoir and the Mahanadi valley. Odisha Tourism refers to the second tower as Nehru Minar.
- **Drive or walk along the dam:** the road along the crest is one of the best ways to appreciate the scale of the structure.
- **Sunrise and sunset:** the open water makes Hirakud a popular spot for photography at either end of the day.
- **Birdwatching:** in winter, migratory ducks and other waterbirds gather on the reservoir.
- **Boating and nature activities** are offered around the reservoir at the Debrigarh side (see below); check what is operating when you visit.

Odisha Tourism lists the dam as free to visit and suggests two to three hours. Its listed hours at the time of writing were 6 am to 6 pm, but security rules and access can change at a working dam, so confirm locally. Photography of some installations may be restricted; follow signs and security staff.

## When is the best time to visit Hirakud Dam?

**October to February** is the recommended period, according to Odisha Tourism. Days are pleasant and migratory birds are present. From March to May western Odisha is very hot. In the monsoon (June to September) Sambalpur receives heavy rain; when the reservoir fills, the authorities may open the dam's sluice gates, a sight that attracts many visitors, but access may then be controlled for safety. ## How to reach Hirakud Dam

| Mode | Details |
|------|---------|
| Air | Veer Surendra Sai Airport, Jharsuguda, the nearest airport, inaugurated in 2018 |
| Rail | Sambalpur is the nearest major railhead; Odisha Tourism names Sambalpur railway station |
| Road | Hirakud is about 10 km from Sambalpur by road. National Highway 53 passes through Sambalpur |

Taxis and auto-rickshaws from Sambalpur are the easiest way to reach the viewpoints. Odisha Tourism gives the distance from Bhubaneswar to nearby Huma as 287 km, which is a useful guide to the length of the drive from the capital. For more routes, see [how to reach Odisha](/travel/how-to-reach-odisha).

## Local food

Sambalpur is the hub of western Odisha's food culture. Freshwater fish from the reservoir and the Mahanadi is common in local cooking, and everyday meals include rice, [dalma](/food/dalma) and seasonal vegetables. If you visit around [Nuakhai](/culture/nuakhai), the harvest festival of western Odisha in August or September, you will find homes and temples celebrating the new rice crop. Sambalpur is also the place to look for [Sambalpuri sarees](/culture/sambalpuri-saree).

## Etiquette and rules

- **Respect security restrictions.** Hirakud is critical infrastructure; do not enter restricted zones or fly drones without permission.
- **Do not swim in the reservoir or below the spillways.** Currents can be strong, especially when gates are open.
- **Dress modestly at temples** such as Samaleswari and Huma.
- **Keep the viewpoints clean** and avoid disturbing birds.

## Nearby places to visit

- **Samaleswari Temple, Sambalpur:** the shrine of the presiding goddess of Sambalpur, established under the Chouhan king Balaram Dev, who ruled from 1542 CE according to Odisha Tourism.
- **Huma leaning temple:** a Shiva (Vimaleswara) temple on the Mahanadi about 23 km from Sambalpur, famous for its deliberate tilt; Odisha Tourism dates it to around the middle of the 16th century CE.
- **Debrigarh Wildlife Sanctuary:** a forested sanctuary on the shore of the reservoir, about 25 km from Hirakud according to Odisha Tourism, home to leopards, elephants, gaur and deer. Odisha Tourism recommends October to March.
- **Sambalpur town:** for markets, handloom shops and its festivals, including [Sitala Sasthi](/culture/sitala-sasthi).
