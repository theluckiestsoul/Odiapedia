**Satkosia** (ସାତକୋଶିଆ) is a forested stretch of the Mahanadi valley in central Odisha, centred on Angul district, where the river cuts a 22 km gorge through the Eastern Ghats. Travellers visit Satkosia to stay in riverside nature camps, take boat rides through the gorge, trek in the Satkosia Tiger Reserve and look for wildlife such as gharials, elephants and birds.

## What is Satkosia?

The **Satkosia Tiger Reserve**, about 963.87 sq km in area, was declared in 2007 and combines the Satkosia Gorge Wildlife Sanctuary (established in 1976) with the adjoining Baisipalli Wildlife Sanctuary. Its core is the Mahanadi gorge, flanked by hills of mixed deciduous and sal forest. The **Satkosia Gorge** wetland is also a Ramsar Site; the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change lists its designation date as 12 October 2021, and the addition was announced publicly in August 2022.

The name is generally explained as "seven kos", from *sat* (seven) and *kos*, an old unit of distance, referring to the length of the gorge.

Satkosia's forests hold tigers, leopards and elephants, but sightings of big cats are rare. The National Tiger Conservation Authority approved moving tigers here from Kanha in Madhya Pradesh, but the translocation programme was suspended in 2019 after a translocated male tiger died. The river supports the critically endangered **gharial**, the mugger crocodile and rare freshwater turtles.

## What are the top things to do in Satkosia?

- **Boating in the gorge.** Boat rides on the Mahanadi between forested hills are the highlight of a Satkosia visit. Operating months and routes depend on water levels; ask at your camp.
- **Tikarpada.** The gharial research area, interpretation centre and nature trails make Tikarpada the main base for learning about the reserve. Odisha Tourism lists the Tikarpada nature camp with Swiss tents.
- **Nature camps.** The Satkosia Wildlife Division lists nature camps at **Tikarpada, Chhotkei, Purunakote, Tarava and Baliput**. Chhotkei is a community-run camp where elephant movement is sometimes seen, according to Outlook Traveller. Camps typically offer guided walks, birding and sometimes kayaking.
- **Meadows and viewpoints.** Purunakote and Badamul have open meadows that are good for wildlife watching, and the granite outcrop at Sapapathar can be reached on foot.
- **Birdwatching.** The river, sandbanks and forest edge support a wide range of birds; early morning walks with camp naturalists are the best way to see them.

## When is the best time to visit Satkosia?

- **November to February** is the most comfortable time for camping, boating and trekking, with cool nights and dry trails. Outlook Traveller reports that the riverside eco-retreat operates from December to February.
- **Mid-February to mid-June** is considered the best period for wildlife sightings, as animals gather near water, but daytime temperatures can reach the mid-40s Celsius.
- **Monsoon (roughly July to September)** brings high water in the Mahanadi; boating and some camps may be suspended. Check with the forest division before travelling.

Operating seasons for individual camps can change from year to year, so confirm availability on the booking portal.

## How do I reach Satkosia?

- **By air:** The nearest airport is Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar. Odisha Tourism gives the distance from Bhubaneswar as about 160 km.
- **By rail:** Angul is the nearest railway station, about 60 km from the reserve according to Outlook Traveller.
- **By road:** Angul is well connected by highway to Bhubaneswar, Cuttack and Sambalpur. From Angul, hire a vehicle to Tikarpada, Purunakote or your chosen camp. Confirm the best approach road with your camp when you book.

## What food should I try at Satkosia?

Nature camps generally serve simple home-style Odia meals cooked by local staff. Expect rice, [dalma](/food/dalma) and seasonal vegetables; in the hot months, ask for [pakhala bhata](/food/pakhala-bhata). Angul and Cuttack on the way have more options, including [dahibara aloodum](/food/dahibara-aloodum) in Cuttack.

## Rules and etiquette

- Book accommodation in advance through the official ecotourism portal; casual walk-ins at camps are not reliable.
- Enter forest areas only with forest department guides and on permitted routes.
- Do not swim in the Mahanadi; the river holds crocodiles and strong currents.
- Keep noise low, do not feed animals, and take your litter out of the reserve.
- Be respectful in community-run camps and villages, and ask before photographing people.
