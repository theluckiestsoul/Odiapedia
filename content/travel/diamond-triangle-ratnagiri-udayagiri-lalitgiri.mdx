The **Diamond Triangle** of Odisha refers to three Buddhist archaeological sites, **Ratnagiri** (ରତ୍ନଗିରି), **Udayagiri** (ଉଦୟଗିରି) and **Lalitgiri** (ଲଳିତଗିରି), which lie close together in the hills between the Birupa and Brahmani river valleys in Jajpur and Cuttack districts. Excavated over the past century, the Diamond Triangle sites reveal monasteries, stupas and sculpture spanning more than a thousand years of Buddhist activity in ancient Odisha.

## Why visit the Diamond Triangle?

These three sites show that Odisha, better known today for its Hindu temples, was once an important centre of Buddhism, with large monasteries active for many centuries. Odisha Tourism describes Lalitgiri as the oldest known Buddhist monastic site in Odisha. Ratnagiri flourished between about the 5th and 13th centuries CE, and Udayagiri, according to inscriptions found there, was known as Madhavapura Mahavihara. The sites are quieter than the [Golden Triangle](/culture/golden-triangle) of Bhubaneswar, Puri and Konark, and they reward visitors interested in art, history and archaeology. For a wider route, see our [Odisha Buddhist trail itinerary](/travel/odisha-buddhist-trail-itinerary).

## Ratnagiri

Ratnagiri stands on a hill in Jajpur district. The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) excavated it between 1958 and 1961 under Debala Mitra, with further work from 1997 to 2004. The site includes:

- **Monastery 1**, the largest, with richly carved stone doorways that are regarded among the finest surviving monastic carving in India.
- **Two smaller monasteries**, one of which is thought to be the earliest building at the site (around the 5th century CE).
- **A main stupa** dating to around the 9th century CE, surrounded by hundreds of smaller votive stupas, many from the 9th to 13th centuries.
- **A site museum** displaying sculptures, seals, terracottas and other finds.

Odisha Tourism notes that Ratnagiri has the only monastery in India with a curvilinear roof. Ratnagiri was once identified with the Puspagiri monastery described by the 7th-century Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang), but that identification has been questioned since the discovery of a site at Langudi hill in the 1990s.

## Udayagiri

Udayagiri, also in Jajpur district, is described by Odisha Tourism as the largest and most picturesque site of the three, and also the least excavated. Excavations began in 1985 and continued intermittently, with further phases in the late 1990s and early 2000s. Two monastic complexes have been identified, known from inscriptions as **Madhavapura Mahavihara** and **Simhaprastha Mahavihara**, along with a main stupa with seated Buddha images at the cardinal points, brick votive stupas, sculptures including Avalokiteshvara, and an inscribed stone stepped well.

## Lalitgiri

Lalitgiri, in Cuttack district and also called Naltigiri, lies between the Birupa and Chitrotpala river valleys. Odisha Tourism dates the monastery to the 1st century CE, and excavated material suggests occupation from the post-Mauryan period through to about the 13th century CE. The ASI's excavations between 1985 and 1991 uncovered a large stupa containing **relic caskets**: nested containers of khondalite, steatite, silver and gold, the innermost holding a bone relic. The site museum displays these caskets together with images of the Buddha, Bodhisattvas, Tara and Jambhala.

## When is the best time to visit?

**October to March** is the most comfortable time, as the sites are largely open-air with limited shade. April to June is very hot, and the monsoon months can make paths muddy. Site and museum opening days and hours are set by the ASI; check the ASI or Odisha Tourism website before you go. See [best time to visit Odisha](/travel/best-time-to-visit-odisha).

## How to reach the Diamond Triangle

| From | Approximate distance |
|------|---------------------|
| Bhubaneswar | Lalitgiri about 90 km; Ratnagiri about 100 km |
| Cuttack | Lalitgiri about 60 km; Ratnagiri about 70 km |
| Between sites | Sources give roughly 7 to 12 km between each pair |

The nearest airport is Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, and Cuttack is the most convenient major railway station. There is little public transport between the sites, so most visitors hire a car from Bhubaneswar or Cuttack and see all three in a long day. See [how to reach Odisha](/travel/how-to-reach-odisha).

## Food and facilities

The sites are rural, with few eating places nearby. Carry water and snacks, or plan a meal in Cuttack or along the highway. Cuttack is a good place to try [dahibara aloodum](/food/dahibara-aloodum) on the way back; read more in our [Cuttack guide](/travel/cuttack).

## Etiquette and rules

- **Do not climb on stupas, walls or sculptures**; they are protected monuments.
- **Follow ASI rules** on photography, especially inside the museums.
- **Dress modestly and remove shoes** where asked at active places of worship.
- **Do not litter**; carry your rubbish away.

## Nearby places

- **Jajpur town:** the old Viraja Kshetra, with the Biraja temple; see the [Jajpur district page](/district/jajpur).
- **Cuttack:** the former capital and the nearest major city.
- **Bhitarkanika National Park:** the mangrove park in Kendrapara district, which can be combined with a longer trip; see [Bhitarkanika](/travel/bhitarkanika-national-park).
