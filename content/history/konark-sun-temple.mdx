# Konark Sun Temple — The Chariot of the Sun God

The **Konark Sun Temple** (କୋଣାର୍କ ସୂର୍ଯ୍ୟ ମନ୍ଦିର) is a 13th-century architectural masterpiece and **UNESCO World Heritage Site**. Designed as a colossal chariot carrying the Sun God, Surya, it represents the pinnacle of Odishan temple architecture.

## The Giant Chariot

The entire temple is designed as a **chariot** with:

- **24 elaborately carved wheels** — arranged as **12 pairs**, commonly interpreted as the 12 months of the year (other interpretations link the 24 wheels to the hours of the day)
- **7 horses** — traditionally said to represent the 7 days of the week

## Historical Background

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Built by** | King Narasimhadeva I (r. 1238–1264) |
| **Dynasty** | Eastern Ganga Dynasty |
| **Year** | c. 1250 CE |
| **Time to build** | 12 years (according to tradition) |
| **Workers** | 1,200 artisans (according to tradition) |

The temple was dedicated to **Surya**, the Sun God, and oriented to catch the first rays of sunrise.

## Architecture

### The Three Main Parts

1. **Vimana (Sanctuary)** — Original tower (now collapsed) is estimated to have been about 229 feet (70 m) tall
2. **Jagamohana (Assembly Hall)** — Still standing at about 128 feet (39 m)
3. **Nata Mandira (Dance Hall)** — For temple dancers

### The Wheels

Each of the 24 wheels is about **3 m (roughly 9 to 10 feet)** in diameter. They are widely described as sundials:
- Guides at the site show how the shadow cast by the spokes can be used to read the time of day
- Popular accounts claim the reading is accurate to within minutes, though this is a traditional claim rather than a measured result

## Sculptural Wonder

The temple is covered with thousands of sculptures depicting:

- **Daily life scenes** — Hunting, war, court life
- **Mythological stories** — Hindu deities and legends
- **Erotic carvings** — Similar to Khajuraho (controversial but artistic)
- **Animals** — Elephants, lions, horses
- **Decorative patterns** — Geometric and floral

## The Mystery of Collapse

The main tower collapsed, but **why?** Historians do not agree, and the tower was already in ruins by the 19th century.

Theories include:
- **Natural disaster** — Earthquake or cyclone
- **Lodestone removal** — A popular legend says a magnetic stone held the structure together; there is no evidence for this
- **Deliberate destruction** — Raids between the 15th and 17th centuries damaged the temple
- **Foundation issues** — Sandy soil problems

In 1903, to stop the Jagamohana from collapsing, the British administration had it sealed and filled with sand.

## The Black Pagoda

European sailors called it the **"Black Pagoda"** because:
- It appeared dark from the sea and served as a coastal landmark
- Contrasted with the "White Pagoda" (Jagannath Temple in Puri)

## UNESCO World Heritage

Inscribed as a World Heritage Site in **1984**, UNESCO recognized it for:

- Outstanding universal value
- Masterpiece of creative genius
- Exceptional testimony to Odishan civilization

## Visiting Konark

- **Location** — approximately 35 km from Puri and 65 km from Bhubaneswar
- **Best time** — October to March
- **Entry fee and timings** — Konark is a ticketed monument of the Archaeological Survey of India; fees and hours change, so check the ASI website before visiting

### The Dance Festival

Every December (usually 1 to 5 December), the **Konark Dance Festival** brings classical dancers from across India to perform against the temple backdrop.

## Preservation Efforts

The Archaeological Survey of India maintains the temple. Ongoing challenges:
- Salt air erosion
- Tourist impact
- Structural stabilization
