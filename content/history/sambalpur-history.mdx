# Sambalpur — The Lion City of Western Odisha

<LanguageSelector 
  currentLang="en" 
  languages={{

    od: "/history/sambalpur-history-od"

/>

**Sambalpur** (ସମ୍ବଲପୁର) is the cultural capital of western Odisha, a city with a **fierce history** of resistance, a legendary textile tradition, and the Hirakud Dam, one of the longest earthen dams in the world. Named after the presiding goddess **Samaleswari** (ସମଲେଶ୍ୱରୀ), Sambalpur has been a center of power and culture for over 500 years.

## Ancient History

### Early Period
- Buddhist and Jain influences in early centuries
- Parts of the region were ruled by early medieval dynasties such as the Somavamsis (9th–12th century)

### Pre-Chauhan Period
- Controlled by various tribal chieftains
- The **Binjhal** and **Gond** communities were dominant
- Goddess Samaleswari worship established as the central cult

## The Chauhan Dynasty (c. 1570–1849)

The **Chauhan dynasty** first ruled the neighbouring Patna kingdom from the 14th century; around **1570**, **Balaram Dev** founded the separate Sambalpur State, which his successors ruled for nearly 300 years:

| Ruler | Period | Notable Events |
|-------|--------|----------------|
| **Balaram Dev** | c. 1570 | Founded Sambalpur State |
| **Baliar Dev** | 1650–1688 | Expanded the kingdom |
| **Chhatra Sai** | 1690–1725 | |
| **Ajit Singh** | 1725–1766 | |
| **Maharaj Sai** | 1820–1827 | His death led to a succession dispute |
| **Rani Mohan Kumari** | 1827–1833 | Installed by the British |
| **Narayan Singh** | 1833–1849 | Last ruler; state annexed under the doctrine of lapse |

### Origin Legend
The Chauhans of Sambalpur claimed descent from the great **Prithviraj Chauhan** of Delhi. While historically debated, this connection shaped their warrior identity.

### Achievements
- Built the **Samaleswari Temple** as the state temple
- Promoted **textile weaving** as royal craft
- Established **trade routes** through western Odisha
- Kept considerable autonomy, though the Marathas occupied Sambalpur in the early 19th century until 1817, after which it came under British protection

## Veer Surendra Sai — Freedom Fighter

**Veer Surendra Sai** (ବୀର ସୁରେନ୍ଦ୍ର ସାଇ, 1809–1884) is Sambalpur's greatest hero — a Chauhan prince and freedom fighter who resisted the British for decades:

### Timeline

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| **1809** | Born on 23 January in Khinda village, north of Sambalpur |
| **1827** | Took up arms at about 18, after the British installed Rani Mohan Kumari |
| **1840** | Captured and imprisoned in Hazaribagh jail |
| **1857** | Freed from Hazaribagh jail by rebels during the 1857 uprising |
| **1857–1862** | Led guerrilla warfare in Sambalpur forests |
| **1862** | Surrendered under a British policy of conciliation |
| **1864** | Re-arrested; later held at Asirgarh Fort, Madhya Pradesh |
| **1884** | Died in captivity at Asirgarh on 28 February |

In all, Surendra Sai spent about **37 years** in British prisons.

### His Legacy
- One of Odisha's earliest anti-colonial leaders — his resistance began decades before 1857
- Inspired tribal communities to resist colonial rule
- His adopted guerrilla tactics were ahead of his time
- Honored with a statue at **Sambalpur's main square**
- **Veer Surendra Sai Airport** in Jharsuguda named after him

## Hirakud Dam — Engineering Marvel

The **Hirakud Dam** (ହୀରାକୁଦ ବନ୍ଧ) is Sambalpur's most iconic modern landmark:

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Built** | 1948–1953 (inaugurated 1957) |
| **Length** | 4.8 km main dam; about 25.8 km including dykes (often described as the longest earthen dam in the world) |
| **River** | Mahanadi |
| **Reservoir** | 743 sq km (Hirakud Reservoir) |
| **Purpose** | Flood control, irrigation, power generation |
| **Power** | 347.5 MW hydroelectric capacity |

### Impact
- Controls devastating **Mahanadi floods** that ravaged coastal Odisha
- Provides irrigation to about **1,556 sq km** in the kharif season and about **1,084 sq km** in the rabi season
- Created a massive reservoir (an "inland sea")
- Two viewing towers: **Gandhi Minar** and **Jawahar Minar**
- **Debrigarh Wildlife Sanctuary** lies beside the reservoir

## Goddess Samaleswari

The presiding deity of Sambalpur is **Maa Samaleswari**:
- Temple located on the banks of **Mahanadi** at **Sambalpur town center**
- Goddess is believed to be a form of **Shakti (Durga)**
- Devotees from across western Odisha worship here

## Sambalpuri Culture

### Nuakhai Festival
The most important festival of western Odisha:
- Celebrated to welcome the **new rice harvest**
- Families eat new rice together (Nua = new, Khai = eat)
- Falls one day after **Ganesh Chaturthi**
- A festival of **agriculture, family bonding, and gratitude**

### Sambalpuri Textiles
Sambalpur is one of India's best-known centres of **ikat (bandha) weaving**:
- Sambalpuri sarees are **GI-tagged**
- In the **bandha** technique, yarns are tied and dyed before weaving, so the design appears on both sides of the fabric
- Major weaving centers: Bargarh, Sonepur, Boudh

### Sambalpuri Music
- **Dalkhai** — Folk dance song of harvest season
- **Rasarkeli** — Romantic folk song
- **Mailajada** — Wedding songs
- Sambalpuri pop music is hugely popular in Odisha
