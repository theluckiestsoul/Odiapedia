**Upendra Bhanja** (ଉପେନ୍ଦ୍ର ଭଞ୍ଜ) was an Odia poet of the late 17th and early 18th century, celebrated as **Kabi Samrat** (Emperor of Poets). A prince of the Bhanja ruling family of Ghumusar in southern Odisha, Upendra Bhanja is the leading figure of the ornate, highly crafted style of Odia poetry often called the riti period, and his works such as **Baidehisha Bilasa** and **Labanyabati** remain landmarks of classical Odia literature.

## When did Upendra Bhanja live?

His dates are uncertain. The Odia Virtual Academy states that he was born **between 1670 and 1688**; other accounts give either 1670 or 1688. A death year around **1740** is often quoted, but it is not confirmed and opinions vary. Readers should treat all specific years for his life as approximate.

## Early life and family

Upendra Bhanja was born at **Kulada** (Kullada) in the Ghumusar estate, near present-day Bhanjanagar in [Ganjam district](/district/ganjam), roughly 80 km from Berhampur according to the Odia Virtual Academy. He belonged to the ruling Bhanja family, but he showed little interest in governing and devoted himself to scholarship and poetry.

Poetry ran in the family: his grandfather, **King Dhananjaya Bhanja**, was himself a respected poet. Upendra Bhanja studied Sanskrit lexicons such as the **Amarakosha**, **Trikandashesha** and **Medini Kosha**, which helps explain the extraordinary vocabulary of his verse. According to the Odia Virtual Academy, his first wife was a sister of the king of Nayagarh, and his second wife, a daughter of the king of Banapur, was a learned princess who encouraged his literary work.

## Poetry and style

Upendra Bhanja's poetry is known for:

- **Verbal virtuosity:** puns, double meanings, alliteration and complex rhyme schemes.
- **Formal constraints:** in Baidehisha Bilasa, each line of the poem begins with the same letter, a feat often cited as a high point of Odia technical poetry.
- **Pictorial verse:** his Chitrakabya Bandodaya explores verse arranged in visual patterns.
- **Romance and devotion:** many works are love narratives set within a Vaishnava devotional frame.
- **Musicality:** his verses were set to traditional ragas and became part of the repertoire of **Odissi music** and dance.

The Odia Virtual Academy notes that he wrote about 52 books, of which around 22 survive, and estimates that he added some 32,300 words to the literary language. He also composed **Gita Abhidhana** (Gitabidhana), a dictionary in verse that is often described as the first Odia lexicon.

## Recognition and debates

The title **Kabi Samrat** was attached to Upendra Bhanja after his lifetime; earlier he was known by the epithet Birabara. In the 19th century his ornate style became the centre of a literary controversy, when supporters of the modern poet [Radhanath Ray](/people/radhanath-ray) and admirers of the classical tradition debated the merits of each. Today both are honoured as major figures of different eras in Odia poetry.

## Legacy and honours

- **Bhanjanagar:** the town earlier called Russellkonda, in Ganjam district, was renamed **Bhanjanagar** in memory of Upendra Bhanja.
- **Music and dance:** his songs (chhanda) remain staples of Odissi music and are performed in [Odissi dance](/culture/odissi-dance).
- **Scholarship:** his works continue to be the subject of doctoral research at Odisha's universities.
