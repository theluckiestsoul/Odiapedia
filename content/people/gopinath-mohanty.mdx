**Gopinath Mohanty** (ଗୋପୀନାଥ ମହାନ୍ତି, 1914–1991) was an Odia novelist, short story writer and translator, and the **first Odia writer to receive the Jnanpith Award**, India's highest literary honour. Drawing on three decades as a government officer in the tribal hills of Koraput, Gopinath Mohanty wrote novels such as **Paraja**, **Amrutara Santana** and **Mati Matala** that gave Odia fiction a new depth of social and psychological realism.

## Early life and education

Gopinath Mohanty was born on **20 April 1914** at **Nagabali**, a village on the outskirts of Cuttack. The Odia Virtual Academy names his parents as Suryamani Mohanty and Durgadevi. He studied at **Ravenshaw College**, Cuttack, and at **Patna University**, earning a master's degree in English literature.

## Career in the administrative service

In **1938** Gopinath Mohanty joined the **Orissa Administrative Service**, from which he retired in **1969**. The Wire notes that much of his service was spent in the tribal regions of undivided [Koraput](/district/koraput), where he worked closely with Kondh, Paraja, Gadaba and Saora communities. This experience shaped both his fiction and his studies of tribal languages and culture.

After retirement he held a **University Grants Commission** distinguished visiting professorship for creative writing at the Department of English, **Utkal University** (from 1976, according to the Odia Virtual Academy). In his last years he taught at **San Jose State University** in California, and he died in San Jose on **20 August 1991**.

## Writing

Gopinath Mohanty's literary career spanned more than five decades. The Odia Virtual Academy credits him with 25 novels, 11 short story collections, plays, biographies, an autobiography, books on tribal languages and culture, and translations; other sources give slightly different totals.

### Major themes

- **Tribal life:** sympathetic, detailed portraits of adivasi communities of Koraput, their land, customs and exploitation by moneylenders and officials.
- **Rural Odisha:** the inner lives of peasants, families and villages.
- **Change and loss:** the pressures of modernisation, debt and displacement.

### Paraja

**Paraja** (1945) follows a family of the Paraja tribe in the hills of Koraput as debt bondage and exploitation destroy their way of life. It is among the most widely read Odia novels in English translation, and The Wire has described it as a work that treated forests as living worlds long before modern conflicts over mining.

### Amrutara Santana

**Amrutara Santana** (1947), a novel of Kondh life, won the **Sahitya Akademi Award** in **1955**, the first year in which the award was given.

### Mati Matala

**Mati Matala** (The Fertile Soil, 1964) is a long novel of village life and social transformation in Odisha. It won the **Jnanpith Award** for **1973**. The 1973 award was a joint award, shared with the Kannada poet **D. R. Bendre** for his poetry collection *Naaku Tanti*. The Odia Virtual Academy gives the year as 1974, which may reflect the year of presentation; most lists, including OrissaPOST and The Wire, give 1973.

## Legacy and honours

- **Sahitya Akademi Award (1955)** for Amrutara Santana.
- **Soviet Land Nehru Award (1970)**, which the Odia Virtual Academy links to his Odia translation of Maxim Gorky.
- **Jnanpith Award (1973)** for Mati Matala, shared with D. R. Bendre; the first Jnanpith for Odia.
- **D.Litt.** from Sambalpur University (1975, per the Odia Virtual Academy).
- **Padma Bhushan (1981)** for his contribution to literature.

Cornell University scholar Satya P. Mohanty has called him perhaps the most important Indian novelist of the second half of the 20th century, a judgement The Wire cites while noting that he remains less known nationally than he deserves.
