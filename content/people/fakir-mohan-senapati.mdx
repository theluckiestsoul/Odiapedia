# Fakir Mohan Senapati — Father of Modern Odia Literature

**Fakir Mohan Senapati** (1843-1918) is revered as the **father of modern Odia literature**. His groundbreaking novel *Chha Mana Atha Guntha* (Six Acres and a Third) is often described as the first Indian novel to deal with the exploitation of landless peasants by a feudal landlord.

## Early Life

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Birth** | January 13, 1843 |
| **Place** | Mallikashpur, Balasore, Odisha |
| **Death** | June 14, 1918, Balasore |
| **Education** | Largely self-taught |

Despite losing his father at about one and a half years of age and his mother soon after, and growing up in poverty under his grandmother's care, Fakir Mohan became one of the most learned men of his time.

## Languages He Mastered

Fakir Mohan worked across several languages, including:
- **Odia** (native)
- **Bengali**
- **Sanskrit** (from which he translated classics into Odia)
- **English**

## Major Works

### Novels
- **Chha Mana Atha Guntha** (serialised 1897–1899, published as a book 1902) — Masterpiece about land exploitation
- **Lachhama** — Historical novel
- **Mamu** — Novel
- **Prayaschita** — Social novel

### Short Stories
- **Rebati** (1898) — First Odia short story
- **Patent Medicine**
- **Daka Munshi**

### Poetry
- **Utkala Bhramanam** (1892)

### Translations
- Odia translations of the **Ramayana**, **Mahabharata** and other Sanskrit texts

### Autobiography
- **Atma Jibana Charita** — Candid life story and the first autobiography in Odia

## Why He Matters

### Literary Revolution

Before Fakir Mohan:
- Odia prose was ornate and Sanskrit-heavy
- Stories were mythological and unrealistic
- Common people weren't subjects of literature

After Fakir Mohan:
- Simple, conversational prose
- Realistic stories of ordinary life
- Social issues became themes
- Common people became protagonists

### Chha Mana Atha Guntha

This novel tells the story of a poor weaver couple, Bhagia and Saria, whose land and cow are seized by the scheming landlord Ramachandra Mangaraj. Its significance:

- Often described as the **first Indian novel about the exploitation of landless peasants** by a feudal lord
- Used humor and irony as weapons
- Exposed how the colonial legal and land systems could be abused
- Written in colloquial, accessible Odia
- Influenced generations of writers

## Social Activism

Beyond writing, Fakir Mohan:
- Fought for **Odia as a distinct language** at a time when some officials sought to replace it with Bengali
- Worked as an **administrator** in the service of princely states

## Legacy

### Honors
- Called **"Vyasakabi"** (Odisha's Vyasa) for his translations of the Sanskrit epics
- Numerous institutions named after him, including **Fakir Mohan University** and **Fakir Mohan College** in Balasore
- His birthplace is a pilgrimage for literature lovers
- Celebrated on **Fakir Mohan Jayanti**

### Influence
Modern Odia writers who followed his path of realistic prose include later novelists and short-story writers of the 20th century, and his contemporaries Radhanath Ray and Madhusudan Rao worked alongside him to promote Odia.
