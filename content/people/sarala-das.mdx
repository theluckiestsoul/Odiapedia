**Sarala Das** (ସାରଳା ଦାସ) was a 15th-century poet who composed the **Mahabharata in Odia**, the first great epic of the Odia language. Revered as the **Adi Kabi** (first poet) of Odia literature, Sarala Das wrote in the speech of ordinary people rather than in Sanskrit, and his retelling of the epic laid the foundation for a written literary tradition in Odia.

## When did Sarala Das live?

Precise dates of birth and death are not recorded. The Odia Virtual Academy, run by the Government of Odisha, states that he can safely be placed in the **15th century**. Scholars link him to the reign of **Gajapati Kapilendra Deva** (r. 1435–1467), founder of the Suryavamsi Gajapati line, during whose rule he is believed to have begun his Mahabharata.

## Early life

According to the Odia Virtual Academy, Sarala Das was born **Siddheswara Parida** in the village of **Kanakavati Patana** (Kanakapura) in present-day [Jagatsinghpur district](/district/jagatsinghpur). He came from a Khandayat (peasant-militia) family, and tradition holds that he served as a soldier in the Gajapati army. He took the name **Sarala Dasa**, servant of the goddess Sarala, whose shrine is associated with the region. He styled himself **Sudra Muni**, a humble self-description that underlined his non-Brahmin background.

He is said to have had little formal schooling in Sanskrit, and later accounts attribute his learning to devotion and self-study. His working life and compositions are associated with religious establishments of the area, and according to the Odia Virtual Academy he died at a monastery on the Budha Nadi.

## The Odia Mahabharata

Sarala Das's **Mahabharata** is not a translation of Vyasa's Sanskrit epic but a free and vigorous retelling. Key features noted by scholars and by the Odia Virtual Academy:

- **Language of the people:** he used everyday spoken Odia and avoided heavy Sanskritisation, so that the epic could be recited and understood in villages.
- **Local setting:** the story is placed in an Odia landscape, with references to places in Odisha and to the worship of Lord Jagannath.
- **New episodes:** it contains many episodes and characters not found in the Sanskrit text, which later Odia writers drew upon.
- **Tone:** its dominant mood is heroic and martial rather than romantic, and its verse, in a metre drawn from folk song, is described as simple, forceful and musical.

The Press Information Bureau recorded in 2021 that the then Vice President of India described Sarala Das not only as Adi Kabi but also as "Adi Aitihasika" (first historian) and "Adi Bhougolbith" (first geographer), because the Mahabharata preserves so much about the history and geography of medieval Odisha.

## Other works

Besides the Mahabharata, the Odia Virtual Academy lists:

- **Bilanka Ramayana**, regarded as his earliest work, a narrative connected with the Ramayana tradition.
- **Chandi Purana**, a devotional poem on the goddess.
- **Laxmi Narayana Vachanika**.

## Legacy and honours

- **Foundation of Odia literature:** literary historians treat the Sarala Mahabharata as the starting point of Odia classical literature, and its influence runs through the later Panchasakha poets and beyond. See [milestones of Odia literature](/language/odia-literature-milestones).
- **Classical language status:** the PIB release of 2021 linked Odia's recognition as a classical language partly to the long literary heritage that begins with Sarala Das. Read more on the [classical status of Odia](/language/odia-classical-language).
- **Popular memory:** his Mahabharata continues to be recited and read in Odisha more than five centuries after its composition.
