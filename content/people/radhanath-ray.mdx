**Radhanath Ray** (ରାଧାନାଥ ରାୟ, 1848–1908) was an Odia poet and educationist honoured with the title **Kabibar** and widely regarded as a founder of **modern Odia poetry**. Drawing on Sanskrit classics, Odia history and English Romantic poetry, Radhanath Ray wrote long narrative and descriptive poems such as **Chilika** and **Kedara Gauri**, and he stood with Fakir Mohan Senapati and Madhusudan Rao in defending the Odia language in the schools of 19th-century Odisha.

## Early life

Radhanath Ray was born on **28 September 1848** at **Kedarpur** in present-day [Balasore district](/district/balasore), then part of the Bengal Presidency. The Odia Virtual Academy notes that he came from a zamindar family. Balasore lay on the border of Bengali and Odia cultural spheres, and like many educated young men of the region he first wrote in Bengali: his earliest collection, **Kabitabali**, was composed when he was about eighteen, followed by **Lekhabali**.

## Educationist and defender of Odia

Radhanath Ray made his career in education and rose to serve as an **inspector of schools**. In the second half of the 19th century some officials and scholars argued that Odia was merely a dialect of Bengali and should be replaced by Bengali in schools. Radhanath Ray, together with [Fakir Mohan Senapati](/people/fakir-mohan-senapati) and Madhusudan Rao, worked to keep Odia as the medium of instruction and to build a modern literature in the language. This struggle was a key part of the wider Odia identity movement described in our [Odisha history overview](/history/odisha-history-brief).

## Poetry

After his early Bengali work, Radhanath Ray wrote exclusively in Odia. His poetry is noted for:

- **New subjects:** landscapes, history and legend of Odisha, rather than only devotional or courtly romance.
- **Western influence:** echoes of English Romantic writers such as Wordsworth, Scott and Keats, and experiments such as blank verse and satire that were new to Odia.
- **Classical retellings:** adaptations of stories from classical sources, including Latin ones, recast in Odia settings.
- **Patriotic feeling:** a strong sense of Odisha's past greatness and present condition.

### Chilika

**Chilika**, regarded as a semi-epic, is a descriptive poem on [Chilika Lake](/history/chilika-lake). It moves between the beauty of the lake, the life around it and reflections on Odisha's history, and it is often considered his finest poem.

### Mahajatra

**Mahajatra** (the great journey), listed by the Odia Virtual Academy as *Mahajatra – Jajatikeshari*, draws on the legend of the early Odisha king Jajati Keshari and reflects Radhanath Ray's interest in Odisha's past.

### Literary controversy

His modern style provoked a debate with admirers of the ornate classical tradition of [Upendra Bhanja](/people/upendra-bhanja). The controversy helped define the shift from classical to modern Odia poetry.

## Legacy and honours

- **Kabibar:** the Odia Virtual Academy records that the title was conferred by Raja Sudhaldev of **Bamanda**, a former princely state in western Odisha.
- **Father of modern Odia poetry:** he is widely described in Odia literary history as the poet who opened Odia verse to modern themes and forms.
- **National poet of Odisha:** the Odia Virtual Academy describes him as "a national poet of the first order in Odisha".
