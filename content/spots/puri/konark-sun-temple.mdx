# Konark Sun Temple — The Architectural Marvel

![Konark Sun Temple](/images/konark-sun-temple.png)

The **Konark Sun Temple** (କୋଣାର୍କ ସୂର୍ଯ୍ୟ ମନ୍ଦିର), built around 1250 CE by King Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga Dynasty, is an unparalleled architectural wonder located on the coastline of Odisha.

## The Giant Stone Chariot

The entire temple is designed in the shape of a colossal **stone chariot** belonging to the Sun God, Surya. It is mounted on 24 intricately carved stone wheels (each about 3 metres in diameter), led by 7 mighty stone horses.

### Significance of the Architecture
- **24 Wheels:** The twelve pairs of wheels are commonly read as the 12 months of the Hindu calendar, each with its bright and dark fortnights (some interpretations link them to the 24 hours). The wheels are also said to work as sundials.
- **7 Horses:** Commonly interpreted as the seven days of the week.

## The Black Pagoda

European sailors navigating the Bay of Bengal would use the temple as a landmark. Because its great tiered tower looked dark from the sea, European accounts (as early as 1676) called it the **"Black Pagoda"**; a popular legend also speaks of a magnetic lodestone that drew ships ashore. (Conversely, the Jagannath Temple in Puri was called the "White Pagoda").

## The Legend of Dharmapada

According to legend, 1,200 artisans worked for 12 years to build the temple, but struggling to place the crowning stone (kalasha). A 12-year-old boy, **Dharmapada** (the son of the chief architect Bishu Maharana), successfully placed the final stone but sacrificed his own life in the Chandrabhaga river to save the 1,200 artisans from the king's wrath.

## World Heritage Site

In 1984, UNESCO declared the Konark Sun Temple a **World Heritage Site**, recognizing it as an outstanding testament to the Kalinga architecture and Indian culture.
